# PRODUCT CATEGORIES

The SAB’A Product Categories framework defines the categories through which SAB’A may develop, organize, and expand its product portfolio.

The purpose of this document is to establish a clear structure for understanding product categories while protecting the focus, identity, and long-term positioning of the SAB’A brand.

SAB’A begins as a fragrance-focused luxury house. Any future expansion should be intentional and must remain consistent with the brand’s philosophy, product architecture, and overall strategic direction.

This document defines the framework for product categories. It does not constitute final approval of future categories, products, formats, or commercial launches.

---

## 1. Category Philosophy

SAB’A should develop product categories with purpose rather than pursuing expansion for its own sake.

Each category should contribute meaningfully to the SAB’A world and strengthen the overall brand experience.

A product category should only be considered when it demonstrates:

- Strategic relevance
- Brand alignment
- Customer value
- Product quality potential
- Operational feasibility
- Long-term relevance

The existence of a possible category does not automatically imply that SAB’A should enter it.

---

## 2. Primary Category

### Fragrance

Fragrance is the foundational product category of SAB’A.

The brand begins with fragrance because scent represents the central sensory expression of the house and provides the strongest connection between product, emotion, storytelling, and cultural identity.

The fragrance category should establish the initial product language and provide the foundation for future product development.

The category may include different fragrance products and formats as approved through the wider SAB’A product system.

Specific fragrances, formulas, sizes, concentrations, and technical specifications are documented separately.

---

## 3. Fragrance Product Structure

Within the fragrance category, products may be organized according to their role within the portfolio.

A fragrance product may include:

- Core fragrance products
- Different approved presentation formats
- Travel-oriented formats
- Gifting-oriented formats
- Discovery-oriented formats
- Future special or limited expressions

The introduction of any specific format should be based on a defined customer, strategic, or commercial purpose.

Formats should remain part of the same fragrance identity and product family.

---

## 4. Core Fragrance Products

Core fragrance products represent the primary expression of the SAB’A fragrance portfolio.

They should establish:

- The fragrance identity
- The primary product experience
- The principal visual expression
- The core packaging experience
- The long-term presence of the fragrance within the portfolio

Core products should receive the highest level of attention during development because they represent the principal relationship between the customer and the SAB’A fragrance house.

---

## 5. Supporting Fragrance Formats

Supporting formats may be developed to complement the core fragrance product.

Potential purposes include:

- Travel
- Gifting
- Discovery
- Sampling
- Collection building
- Customer accessibility

Supporting formats should not undermine the perceived value or identity of the core product.

They should remain visually, conceptually, and experientially connected to the primary fragrance.

Specific formats and sizes remain subject to product development and approval.

---

## 6. Limited and Special Expressions

SAB’A may consider limited or special product expressions when they provide meaningful strategic or creative value.

Such products may be developed for purposes such as:

- Significant brand moments
- Special collections
- Cultural storytelling
- Collaborations
- Controlled experimentation
- Exceptional occasions

Limited or special expressions should remain consistent with SAB’A standards.

Scarcity alone should not be used as a substitute for product value.

Any such expression should have a clear reason for existing.

---

## 7. Future Product Categories

As SAB’A develops, additional product categories may be considered.

However, future categories should not be introduced simply because they are commercially available or common within the luxury market.

A future category should demonstrate a meaningful relationship with the SAB’A world.

Potential future categories are therefore considered:

**Exploratory / Not Yet Finalized**

They should remain subject to strategic evaluation, product development, and formal approval.

No future category should be treated as an official SAB’A category until it has been intentionally approved.

---

## 8. Category Expansion Criteria

Before entering a new product category, SAB’A should evaluate the following:

### Brand Alignment

Does the category naturally belong within the SAB’A identity?

### Customer Value

Does the category provide meaningful value to the customer?

### Cultural Relevance

Can the category contribute meaningfully to the cultural and creative world of SAB’A?

### Product Quality

Can SAB’A achieve the level of quality expected from a luxury house?

### Differentiation

Can SAB’A create a distinctive expression within the category?

### Operational Feasibility

Can the category be developed, produced, managed, and supported responsibly?

### Long-Term Potential

Can the category contribute to the long-term development of SAB’A?

A category should not proceed simply because it passes one or two criteria.

The decision should be evaluated as a complete system.

---

## 9. Category Relationship to the Brand

Every product category must remain connected to the central SAB’A brand principles:

**Luxury Minimalism + Saba Heritage + Contemporary Global Positioning**

The category should therefore support:

- Refined presentation
- Contemporary relevance
- Cultural depth
- Product quality
- Coherent storytelling
- Long-term brand recognition

A category that weakens the clarity of the SAB’A identity should be reconsidered.

---

## 10. Category Relationship to Product Architecture

Product Categories exist within the wider SAB’A Product Architecture.

The relationship can be understood as:

**SAB’A Brand → Product Portfolio → Product Category → Product Collection → Individual Product → Product Format**

Each level should have a clear role.

Product Categories define broad areas of product activity.

Product Architecture defines how those products relate to one another.

Product Development defines how individual products are created.

Product Specifications define the technical requirements of approved products.

---

## 11. Category Consistency

Different product categories may eventually have different functional requirements.

However, they should remain connected through the broader SAB’A product language.

Consistency should be maintained through principles such as:

- Quality
- Restraint
- Proportion
- Materials
- Craftsmanship
- Presentation
- Storytelling
- Customer experience

Category-specific differences are acceptable when they are necessary for function or purpose.

Unnecessary differences should be avoided.

---

## 12. Category Growth

SAB’A should prioritize depth before excessive breadth.

A category should be developed sufficiently to establish quality, recognition, and credibility before significant expansion into unrelated areas.

The initial focus should therefore remain on building a strong fragrance foundation.

Future categories should be introduced progressively when the brand has the appropriate strategic, operational, and product capabilities to support them.

Growth should be deliberate rather than reactive.

---

## 13. Category Lifecycle

Product categories should be reviewed over time.

A category may:

- Be introduced
- Be developed
- Expand
- Evolve
- Be repositioned
- Be reduced
- Be discontinued

Category decisions should be based on:

- Strategic relevance
- Customer response
- Product quality
- Brand contribution
- Operational performance
- Long-term potential

No category should be considered permanent simply because it has previously existed.

---

## 14. Category Governance

New product categories should require deliberate internal approval before becoming part of the official SAB’A portfolio.

Category approval should consider:

- Strategic fit
- Brand alignment
- Product architecture
- Financial implications
- Operational requirements
- Legal considerations
- Quality requirements
- Customer experience

The appropriate Codex sections should be updated when a new category is formally approved.

---

## 15. Category Decision Framework

Before introducing a new category, SAB’A should be able to answer:

1. Why does this category belong to SAB’A?
2. What role does it serve?
3. What customer need does it address?
4. How does it connect to the SAB’A product architecture?
5. How does it strengthen the brand?
6. Can SAB’A deliver the required quality?
7. Can it be supported operationally?
8. Does it create meaningful differentiation?
9. Does it have long-term potential?
10. Is the category worth the complexity it introduces?

If the answers are unclear, the category should remain under consideration rather than being introduced prematurely.

---

## 16. The SAB’A Category Standard

An approved SAB’A product category should be:

- **Relevant** — It should have a clear role within the brand.
- **Aligned** — It should reflect SAB’A principles.
- **Distinctive** — It should allow meaningful brand expression.
- **Valuable** — It should provide genuine customer value.
- **High Quality** — It should support luxury-level execution.
- **Cohesive** — It should integrate with the wider product system.
- **Scalable** — It should allow controlled development when appropriate.
- **Manageable** — It should remain operationally practical.
- **Purposeful** — It should exist for a clear reason.
- **Long-Term** — It should contribute to sustainable brand development.

---

## 17. Current Category Position

At the current stage of SAB’A development:

**Primary Category:**  
Fragrance

**Current Strategic Focus:**  
Building a strong and coherent fragrance foundation.

**Supporting Formats:**  
To be developed according to approved product strategy.

**Future Categories:**  
Exploratory / Not Yet Finalized.

The current priority is not category expansion.

The priority is to establish SAB’A as a distinctive and credible luxury fragrance house.

---

## 18. Core Principle

SAB’A should not measure growth by the number of product categories it owns.

Growth should be measured by the strength, relevance, quality, and coherence of the product system.

The guiding principle is:

> **Build depth before breadth.**

Every new category should strengthen the SAB’A world rather than dilute it.

The ultimate objective is to create a product portfolio that feels intentional, refined, culturally grounded, and capable of evolving into a complete luxury house over time.
