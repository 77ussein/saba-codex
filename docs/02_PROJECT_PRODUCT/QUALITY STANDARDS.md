# QUALITY STANDARDS

The SAB’A Quality Standards define the framework used to maintain, evaluate, and protect the quality of SAB’A products throughout development, production, storage, handling, and final delivery.

At the current stage of the brand, these standards are primarily focused on fragrance products.

The purpose of this document is to establish a consistent quality mindset and control framework that supports the SAB’A Product Standards while allowing specific technical requirements to be defined separately.

Quality is not considered a final inspection step only.

It is a continuous responsibility that begins during development and continues throughout the product lifecycle.

---

## 1. Quality Philosophy

SAB’A considers quality to be an essential part of luxury.

Quality should be demonstrated through:

- Consistency
- Precision
- Reliability
- Material integrity
- Functional performance
- Fragrance integrity
- Manufacturing discipline
- Presentation
- Customer experience

A product should not be considered high quality simply because it appears visually premium.

The complete product must perform, remain consistent, and meet the standards established by SAB’A.

---

## 2. Quality as a Continuous Process

Quality should be controlled throughout the product lifecycle.

The general quality cycle is:

**Define → Develop → Test → Verify → Approve → Monitor → Improve**

Quality should therefore be considered during:

- Product development
- Component selection
- Sampling
- Prototyping
- Production
- Assembly
- Packaging
- Storage
- Distribution
- Final inspection

The objective is to prevent quality problems rather than simply discover them after production.

---

## 3. Quality Objectives

SAB’A quality management should aim to achieve:

- Consistent fragrance quality
- Reliable product functionality
- Consistent physical presentation
- Stable packaging quality
- Controlled manufacturing variation
- Accurate product identification
- Safe and compliant products
- Consistent customer experience

Quality objectives should support both the immediate product and the long-term reputation of the SAB’A house.

---

## 4. Fragrance Quality Control

Because fragrance is the core product category, fragrance quality requires particular attention.

Quality evaluation may consider:

- Olfactive consistency
- Formula consistency
- Appearance
- Color
- Fragrance development
- Performance
- Stability
- Batch consistency

Approved fragrance references should be maintained where appropriate to provide a basis for comparison during production and quality review.

Any significant deviation should be investigated before the affected product is released.

---

## 5. Batch Consistency

Each production batch should aim to remain consistent with the approved product standard.

Consistency should be evaluated across relevant characteristics, including:

- Fragrance
- Appearance
- Color
- Fill
- Components
- Assembly
- Packaging
- Labeling
- Overall presentation

Minor manufacturing variation may occur.

However, variation should remain within acceptable and controlled limits.

Specific tolerances should be defined within the relevant technical and quality documentation.

---

## 6. Component Quality

All major physical components should meet the approved quality requirements.

This may include:

- Bottle
- Cap
- Sprayer
- Collar or related components
- Labels
- Box
- Carton
- Inserts
- Protective materials

Components should be evaluated for:

- Physical integrity
- Appearance
- Function
- Compatibility
- Consistency
- Surface quality
- Assembly suitability

A component that does not meet the approved standard should not be accepted simply because it is visually acceptable in isolation.

Its effect on the complete product should also be considered.

---

## 7. Material Inspection

Materials and components should be inspected before being incorporated into finished products where practical.

Inspection may identify:

- Damage
- Defects
- Incorrect materials
- Surface imperfections
- Printing errors
- Dimensional inconsistencies
- Contamination
- Packaging damage

The level of inspection should be appropriate to the importance and risk of the material.

---

## 8. Functional Quality

All functional product components should operate reliably.

Quality evaluation may include:

- Sprayer operation
- Cap fit
- Component attachment
- Opening and closing
- Packaging fit
- Product protection
- General handling

Functional defects should be treated as quality issues even when they do not significantly affect appearance.

Luxury quality requires both visual refinement and reliable performance.

---

## 9. Visual Quality

The finished product should maintain a consistent visual standard.

Visual inspection may consider:

- Logo application
- Typography
- Alignment
- Printing
- Surface finish
- Color consistency
- Scratches
- Marks
- Chips
- Dents
- Assembly alignment
- Packaging presentation

Visible defects that materially affect the perceived quality of the product should be identified and evaluated before release.

---

## 10. Packaging Quality

Packaging should protect the product while maintaining the intended luxury experience.

Quality evaluation may include:

- Structural integrity
- Printing accuracy
- Alignment
- Surface finish
- Material condition
- Closure
- Product fit
- Protection during handling

Packaging defects should be evaluated based on both functional impact and customer perception.

---

## 11. Assembly Quality

Assembly should be performed consistently according to approved processes.

Quality checks may consider:

- Component alignment
- Secure attachment
- Fit
- Finish
- Cleanliness
- Functional operation
- Overall presentation

The final assembled product should correspond to the approved reference.

---

## 12. Cleanliness and Presentation

Finished products should be presented in a clean and controlled condition.

The product should be free from unnecessary:

- Dust
- Residue
- Adhesive marks
- Fingerprints
- Packaging debris
- Visible contamination

Cleanliness contributes directly to the customer's perception of quality.

---

## 13. Quality Inspection Levels

Quality inspection may occur at different stages.

### Incoming Inspection

Materials and components are evaluated when received.

### In-Process Inspection

Products are checked during manufacturing and assembly.

### Final Inspection

Completed products are evaluated before release.

### Periodic Review

Approved products may be reviewed periodically to confirm continued consistency.

The exact inspection frequency and sampling methods should be defined according to production scale, risk, and operational requirements.

---

## 14. Defect Classification

Quality issues should be classified according to their severity and potential impact.

A general classification may include:

### Critical Defect

A defect that may affect safety, regulatory compliance, or fundamental product integrity.

Such defects should prevent product release until resolved.

### Major Defect

A defect that materially affects functionality, quality, appearance, or customer experience.

Major defects should normally prevent release of the affected product.

### Minor Defect

A limited deviation that does not materially affect safety, functionality, or overall product experience.

Minor defects should still be documented and reviewed according to the applicable quality process.

Specific defect criteria should be defined as the production system matures.

---

## 15. Non-Conforming Products

A product or component that does not meet an approved requirement should be considered non-conforming.

Non-conforming products should be:

- Identified
- Separated where practical
- Evaluated
- Documented
- Corrected, reworked, accepted under controlled approval, or rejected

A non-conforming product should not enter normal customer distribution without appropriate evaluation and authorization.

---

## 16. Quality Records

Important quality information should be documented.

Records may include:

- Inspection results
- Batch information
- Defect records
- Test results
- Supplier issues
- Corrective actions
- Approval records
- Rework records
- Product release decisions

Quality records provide traceability and help identify recurring problems.

---

## 17. Traceability

SAB’A should develop appropriate traceability systems as production increases.

Where practical, products and materials should be traceable through relevant information such as:

- Product identity
- Batch or production reference
- Production date
- Component source
- Supplier
- Quality status

The level of traceability should develop alongside the scale and complexity of the business.

---

## 18. Supplier Quality

Suppliers contribute directly to SAB’A product quality.

Supplier evaluation should consider:

- Material quality
- Consistency
- Reliability
- Communication
- Production capability
- Quality control
- Delivery consistency
- Ability to meet approved requirements

Supplier samples should be evaluated before important materials or components are approved for production.

Changes in supplier or component should be reviewed when they may affect the approved product.

---

## 19. Quality During Storage

Product quality should be protected during storage.

Storage conditions should be appropriate to the characteristics of the product and its materials.

Considerations may include:

- Temperature
- Light exposure
- Humidity
- Physical protection
- Cleanliness
- Product orientation
- Storage duration

Specific storage requirements should be documented within the relevant product and operational specifications.

---

## 20. Quality During Handling and Distribution

Products should remain protected during handling and distribution.

Packaging and handling processes should minimize the risk of:

- Damage
- Leakage
- Scratching
- Breakage
- Contamination
- Packaging deformation
- Incorrect product handling

Distribution requirements should be reviewed according to the intended market and delivery conditions.

---

## 21. Quality Release

A product should only be released for customer distribution when the required quality checks have been completed.

Release should confirm, where applicable:

- Product identity
- Fragrance quality
- Component quality
- Functional condition
- Packaging condition
- Labeling
- Regulatory requirements
- Overall presentation

Release decisions should be based on defined requirements rather than subjective judgment alone.

---

## 22. Corrective Action

When a quality issue is identified, SAB’A should seek to understand its cause rather than simply correcting the visible symptom.

Corrective action may include:

- Identifying the source of the issue
- Containing affected products
- Correcting the process
- Replacing defective components
- Revising procedures
- Reviewing suppliers
- Increasing inspection
- Updating documentation

The objective is to reduce the likelihood of the same issue occurring again.

---

## 23. Continuous Improvement

Quality standards should evolve as SAB’A gains experience.

Information from:

- Production
- Testing
- Customer feedback
- Supplier performance
- Defect analysis
- Product reviews

may be used to improve quality processes.

Continuous improvement should strengthen the system without unnecessarily increasing complexity.

---

## 24. Customer Feedback as a Quality Signal

Customer feedback may provide valuable information about product quality.

Feedback may reveal issues involving:

- Fragrance performance
- Product functionality
- Packaging
- Physical components
- Usability
- Customer expectations

Customer feedback should not automatically be treated as proof of a manufacturing defect.

However, recurring or credible patterns should be investigated.

---

## 25. Quality and Brand Integrity

Quality control is directly connected to brand integrity.

A single poor-quality product can affect the customer's perception of the wider SAB’A house.

For this reason, quality decisions should consider both:

- The individual product
- The long-term reputation of SAB’A

Protecting quality is therefore not only an operational responsibility.

It is a brand responsibility.

---

## 26. Quality Approval Framework

Before a product is released, the relevant quality requirements should be reviewed.

A simplified framework is:

**Inspect → Evaluate → Resolve → Verify → Approve → Release**

If a product does not meet the required standard, it should not automatically proceed because of production pressure, cost, or schedule.

Quality requirements should remain protected throughout the process.

---

## 27. Relationship to Product Standards

Product Standards define the qualities and requirements that SAB’A products are expected to achieve.

Quality Standards define the systems used to verify and maintain those requirements.

The relationship is:

**Product Standards = What quality should be achieved**

**Quality Standards = How quality is controlled and maintained**

Both systems should work together.

---

## 28. Relationship to Product Development

Quality should be integrated into Product Development from the beginning.

The relationship is:

**Develop → Test → Inspect → Refine → Validate → Approve**

Quality should not be introduced only after the product has already been developed.

Early quality consideration reduces the risk of expensive or difficult corrections later.

---

## 29. Future Product Categories

The current Quality Standards are primarily designed for fragrance products.

If SAB’A formally introduces additional product categories in the future, category-specific quality standards should be developed according to the characteristics and risks of each category.

Future category quality standards may define:

- Category-specific inspection requirements
- Performance criteria
- Material requirements
- Functional testing
- Safety requirements
- Defect classifications
- Storage requirements
- Handling requirements
- Release criteria

Future standards should build upon the core SAB’A quality philosophy while allowing each category to have its own appropriate quality controls.

The framework is:

**Core SAB’A Quality Standards → Category-Specific Quality Standards → Product-Specific Quality Controls**

No future category-specific quality requirements should be considered final until the relevant category has been formally approved and developed.

---

## 30. Core Principle

The SAB’A Quality Standards can be summarized as:

> **Quality must be designed, controlled, verified, and protected — not assumed.**

A luxury product is not defined only by how it looks when it leaves production.

It is defined by how consistently it performs, how reliably it is produced, how carefully it is presented, and how confidently it reaches the customer.

SAB’A should therefore treat quality as a continuous system that protects the product, the customer, and the long-term reputation of the house.
