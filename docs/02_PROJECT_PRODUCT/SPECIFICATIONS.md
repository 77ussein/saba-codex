# PRODUCT SPECIFICATIONS

The SAB’A Product Specifications framework defines how the approved technical, physical, functional, and presentation requirements of SAB’A products are documented and controlled.

At the current stage of the brand, this framework is primarily intended for fragrance products.

The purpose of this document is to establish a structured system for recording the final specifications of an approved product while keeping technical decisions separate from product philosophy, development principles, and general quality standards.

Product Specifications should represent approved decisions rather than assumptions, concepts, or temporary development ideas.

---

## 1. Specification Philosophy

A product specification defines what the approved product must be.

It should provide sufficient clarity to ensure that the product can be:

- Developed consistently
- Manufactured accurately
- Inspected objectively
- Reproduced reliably
- Communicated clearly to suppliers
- Maintained consistently over time

Specifications should be precise where precision is required.

At the same time, specifications should not be finalized before the relevant product decisions have been properly evaluated and approved.

---

## 2. Purpose of Specifications

Product Specifications exist to create a reliable reference for the approved product.

They should help SAB’A control:

- Product identity
- Physical characteristics
- Fragrance characteristics
- Components
- Materials
- Dimensions
- Functional requirements
- Packaging
- Presentation
- Production requirements
- Quality references

The specification should function as a controlled reference throughout the product lifecycle.

---

## 3. Specification Hierarchy

SAB’A product documentation should distinguish between different levels of information.

The general hierarchy is:

**Brand Principles → Product Philosophy → Product Architecture → Product Standards → Product Development → Approved Specifications**

This means Product Specifications should reflect decisions that have already been developed and approved.

Specifications should not be used to establish unresolved strategic or creative decisions.

---

## 4. Product Identification

Every approved product should have a clear identity.

Product identification may include:

- Product name
- Product code
- Product category
- Product type
- Collection
- Size
- Version
- Status

The exact naming and coding system should be established and maintained consistently across the SAB’A product system.

Unapproved or temporary products should not be presented as finalized products.

---

## 5. Fragrance Specifications

For fragrance products, the fragrance specification should define the approved characteristics of the fragrance.

Depending on the product, this may include:

- Fragrance name
- Fragrance type
- Concentration
- Formula reference
- Olfactive profile
- Intended character
- Approved formula version
- Performance reference
- Stability requirements

Specific formulas and detailed ingredient information should be maintained within the appropriate fragrance documentation and controlled records.

The Product Specification should reference the approved fragrance rather than unnecessarily duplicating sensitive or detailed technical records.

## 23. Version Control

Product specifications should be version controlled.

Each approved specification should identify:

- Version number
- Date
- Status
- Relevant product
- Major changes
- Approval reference

Changes should not overwrite historical information without maintaining an appropriate record.

This allows SAB’A to understand how a product specification has evolved over time.

---

## 24. Specification Changes

Changes to an approved specification should be controlled.

A change may require:

- Evaluation
- Testing
- Supplier confirmation
- Quality review
- Design review
- Regulatory review
- Formal approval

The level of review should depend on the significance of the change.

A change that affects product quality, function, appearance, or customer experience should not be treated as a minor administrative update.

---

## 25. Specification Completeness

Before a product specification is considered complete, the relevant product elements should be sufficiently defined.

The specification should provide an appropriate reference for:

- Product identity
- Fragrance
- Format
- Bottle
- Cap
- Sprayer
- Materials
- Dimensions
- Finishes
- Colors
- Packaging
- Function
- Performance
- Quality references

Not every product will require identical specification fields.

The specification should contain what is necessary for the specific product.

---

## 26. Relationship to Product Development

Product Development explains how a product is created and refined.

Product Specifications document the final approved result.

The relationship is:

**Concept → Development → Testing → Refinement → Approval → Specification**

Specifications should therefore be finalized after the relevant product decisions have been sufficiently validated.

---

## 27. Relationship to Quality Standards

Quality Standards define how quality is controlled and maintained.

Product Specifications define the approved characteristics against which the product can be evaluated.

The relationship is:

**Quality Standards = How quality is controlled**

**Product Specifications = What the approved product must be**

Together, they create a controlled reference for production and inspection.

## 30. Core Principle

The SAB’A Product Specifications principle can be summarized as:

> **Document what has been approved. Do not treat assumptions as specifications.**

A specification exists to create clarity, consistency, and control.

It should provide a reliable reference for the approved product while remaining connected to the wider SAB’A product system.

As SAB’A develops, specifications should become increasingly precise without becoming unnecessarily complicated.

The objective is to ensure that the product envisioned, the product approved, and the product manufactured remain as consistent as possible.
