# PRODUCT NAMING

The SAB’A Product Naming framework defines the principles and structure used to name SAB’A products.

Names are an important part of the product experience.

A product name should contribute to recognition, identity, storytelling, and long-term brand value while remaining consistent with the positioning of SAB’A as a contemporary luxury fragrance house rooted in the legacy of Saba.

This document establishes the naming framework for SAB’A products without prematurely finalizing individual product names.

Specific product names should be approved separately through the appropriate product development and brand decision processes.

---

## 1. Naming Philosophy

SAB’A product names should be intentional rather than decorative.

A strong name should provide meaning, recognition, and distinction without requiring unnecessary explanation.

Product naming should support the central SAB’A positioning:

**Luxury Minimalism + Saba Heritage + Contemporary Global Positioning**

Names should feel:

- Refined
- Distinctive
- Memorable
- Meaningful
- Timeless
- Culturally grounded
- Contemporary
- Internationally appropriate

The name should feel like a natural part of the SAB’A world.

---

## 2. Purpose of a Product Name

A product name may serve several functions.

It can:

- Identify the product
- Differentiate it from other products
- Support recognition
- Create emotional association
- Communicate character
- Support storytelling
- Strengthen the product portfolio
- Contribute to long-term brand equity

A name does not need to explain the entire fragrance.

Its primary role is to give the product a distinctive and meaningful identity.

---

## 3. Relationship to the SAB’A Brand

Every product name should remain connected to the SAB’A brand.

The naming system should reinforce the perception of SAB’A as a coherent luxury house rather than a collection of unrelated products.

Names should therefore feel as though they belong to the same world.

The relationship should be:

**SAB’A → Product Family → Product → Product Name**

The product name should strengthen the wider identity rather than compete with it.

---

## 4. Heritage as a Naming Source

Saba and Yemeni heritage may provide an important source of inspiration for product naming.

Potential references may include:

- Historical places
- Historical figures
- Cultural concepts
- Ancient terminology
- Geography
- Natural elements
- Trade and civilization
- Architecture
- Historical narratives
- Symbolic concepts

However, heritage references should be used thoughtfully.

The objective is not to create names that feel historical simply for the sake of appearing historical.

Names should translate heritage into a contemporary luxury context.

---

## 5. Contemporary Interpretation

Historical or cultural references should be interpreted rather than reproduced without consideration.

A strong SAB’A name may draw from heritage while remaining:

- Simple
- Elegant
- Modern
- Pronounceable
- Memorable
- Relevant to an international audience

The name should not depend entirely on the customer already understanding its historical background.

The story behind the name can provide additional depth without becoming a requirement for appreciation.

---

## 6. International Relevance

SAB’A is intended to operate within an international luxury market.

Product names should therefore be evaluated for their suitability across different markets and languages.

Considerations may include:

- Pronunciation
- Spelling
- Memorability
- Visual appearance
- Translation implications
- Cultural interpretation
- Potential unintended meanings
- Ease of communication

International relevance does not require removing cultural identity.

The objective is to make cultural identity accessible through refined naming.

---

## 7. Simplicity and Memorability

SAB’A product names should generally favor clarity over unnecessary complexity.

A strong name should be reasonably easy to:

- Read
- Remember
- Pronounce
- Recognize
- Communicate

Long or complicated names should only be used when their meaning and strategic value justify the additional complexity.

The name should remain distinctive without becoming difficult for the customer to engage with.

---

## 8. Distinctiveness

Each product name should be sufficiently distinctive within the SAB’A portfolio.

Names should avoid unnecessary similarity with:

- Existing SAB’A products
- Common fragrance naming conventions
- Competitor products
- Generic luxury terminology

Distinctiveness helps prevent confusion and strengthens product recognition.

A name should have its own identity while remaining part of the SAB’A naming system.

---

## 9. Naming Architecture

SAB’A product naming should operate within a structured hierarchy.

The general framework is:

**Brand → Collection → Product → Format**

For example:

**SAB’A → [Collection] → [Product Name] → [Format]**

Not every product must use every level.

The architecture should be adapted according to the needs of the product portfolio.

The purpose of the hierarchy is to create clarity as the product portfolio grows.

---

## 10. Product Name vs. Product Description

The product name should remain distinct from descriptive information.

The name identifies the product.

Supporting information may communicate:

- Olfactive character
- Fragrance family
- Key notes
- Story
- Inspiration
- Concentration
- Format

The name should not be overloaded with technical or descriptive information that belongs elsewhere in the product presentation.

---

## 11. Fragrance Naming

For fragrance products, the name should contribute to the identity of the fragrance without attempting to describe every aspect of its composition.

A fragrance name may evoke:

- Place
- Memory
- Atmosphere
- Character
- Emotion
- Heritage
- Nature
- Material
- Time
- Cultural meaning

The relationship between the name and the fragrance should feel intentional.

A name should not promise an experience that the fragrance itself cannot support.

---

## 12. Naming by Place

Geographical and historical places may be appropriate sources for SAB’A fragrance names.

Places may provide:

- Cultural depth
- Historical association
- Sense of origin
- Narrative potential
- Strong identity

However, place-based naming should be evaluated carefully.

The use of a place name should have a meaningful relationship to the fragrance, story, or SAB’A world.

A geographical name should not be used solely because it sounds exotic or luxurious.

---

## 13. Naming by Heritage

Historical or cultural names may also be considered when they have a meaningful connection to SAB’A.

Such names should be evaluated for:

- Historical relevance
- Cultural sensitivity
- Pronunciation
- Meaning
- International usability
- Brand fit
- Long-term relevance

The objective is to create names that honor heritage through thoughtful interpretation rather than superficial reference.

---

## 14. Naming by Concept

Not every product needs a geographical or historical name.

Conceptual naming may also be appropriate.

Concept-based names may express:

- Emotion
- Atmosphere
- Character
- Material
- Sensory experience
- Abstract ideas
- Symbolic meaning

Conceptual names should still feel connected to the SAB’A world.

---

## 15. Naming Consistency

The portfolio should maintain a recognizable naming language.

Different products may have different individual meanings, but the overall naming system should feel coherent.

Consistency may be achieved through:

- Similar level of refinement
- Clear naming hierarchy
- Appropriate length
- Consistent tone
- Cultural depth
- Contemporary language
- Controlled use of descriptive terms

Consistency does not mean that every product name must follow the exact same formula.

---

## 16. Naming Tone

SAB’A product names should generally avoid names that feel:

- Cheap
- Generic
- Overly commercial
- Trend-dependent
- Excessively decorative
- Artificially exotic
- Overly complicated
- Clichéd

Names should instead communicate confidence through restraint.

A strong luxury name does not need to shout.

---

## 17. Language Strategy

SAB’A may use Arabic, transliterated Arabic, English, or other language forms where strategically appropriate.

Language selection should consider:

- Cultural authenticity
- Brand identity
- International readability
- Pronunciation
- Visual appearance
- Meaning
- Legal considerations

Arabic may provide an important source of cultural identity.

International presentation should remain accessible without unnecessarily weakening the original cultural meaning.

---

## 18. Transliteration

When Arabic-origin names are presented in Latin characters, transliteration should be handled consistently.

A chosen Latin spelling should remain consistent across:

- Packaging
- Website
- Ecommerce
- Marketing
- Product documentation
- Customer communication

Different spellings of the same approved product name should be avoided unless there is a deliberate reason.

The official transliteration standard should be established as the naming system develops.

---

## 19. Naming Validation

Before a product name is approved, it should be evaluated against relevant criteria.

Evaluation may include:

- Brand alignment
- Meaning
- Cultural relevance
- Pronunciation
- Memorability
- Distinctiveness
- International suitability
- Visual appearance
- Portfolio fit
- Legal availability

A name should not be approved solely because it sounds attractive.

It should function effectively within the complete SAB’A brand system.

---

## 20. Legal and Availability Review

Potential product names should be reviewed for relevant legal and commercial risks before final approval.

This may include checking:

- Trademark availability
- Existing fragrance usage
- Similar brand names
- Domain considerations
- Market-specific conflicts
- Translation issues
- Unintended meanings

Legal review should be performed through the appropriate SAB’A legal process.

A creative name should not be considered final until the necessary checks have been completed.

---

## 21. Naming Approval Process

The general naming process may follow:

**Research → Naming Exploration → Shortlist → Evaluation → Legal Review → Brand Review → Final Approval**

Not every proposed name should proceed through every stage if it is rejected earlier.

The purpose of the process is to ensure that final names are intentional, usable, and aligned with SAB’A.

---

## 22. Naming Documentation

Approved product names should be documented clearly.

The naming record may include:

- Official product name
- Arabic name where applicable
- Official Latin spelling
- Pronunciation reference where needed
- Meaning
- Naming rationale
- Cultural reference
- Approved format
- Date of approval
- Version
- Legal status

This documentation should become the authoritative reference for the approved product name.

---

## 23. Naming Changes

Once a product name has been formally approved, changes should be controlled.

A naming change may affect:

- Packaging
- Product documentation
- Website
- Ecommerce
- Marketing
- Legal records
- Customer recognition

For this reason, renaming an established product should require deliberate evaluation and approval.

Historical naming records should be retained where appropriate.

---

## 24. Current Preliminary Naming Direction

Several fragrance names have previously been discussed as preliminary concepts, including:

- Ma’rib
- Hadramawt
- Bilqis

These names remain **preliminary concepts** unless separately and formally approved.

They should not be treated as final product names solely because they appear within earlier project discussions.

Final naming decisions should follow the SAB’A naming framework and approval process.

---

## 25. Future Product Categories

The current naming framework is primarily designed for fragrance products.

If SAB’A introduces additional product categories in the future, naming principles may be adapted according to the characteristics of each category.

Future category naming systems may define:

- Category-specific naming structures
- Product naming conventions
- Format naming
- Collection naming
- Edition naming
- Limited expression naming

However, future naming systems should continue to follow the core SAB’A principles of:

- Clarity
- Distinctiveness
- Meaning
- Cultural depth
- Contemporary relevance
- International usability
- Long-term consistency

No future category-specific naming system should be considered final until the relevant category has been formally approved.

---

## 26. Naming Standard

A strong SAB’A product name should be:

- **Meaningful** — It should have a clear reason for existing.
- **Distinctive** — It should create recognition.
- **Memorable** — It should remain accessible to the customer.
- **Refined** — It should reflect luxury through restraint.
- **Culturally grounded** — Where relevant, it should carry meaningful cultural depth.
- **Contemporary** — It should remain relevant in a modern luxury context.
- **Internationally appropriate** — It should function across relevant markets.
- **Consistent** — It should belong within the SAB’A naming system.
- **Legally viable** — It should be appropriately reviewed before approval.
- **Long-Term** — It should remain suitable as the brand develops.

---

## 27. Core Principle

The SAB’A Product Naming principle can be summarized as:

> **Name with meaning. Build recognition through restraint.**

A SAB’A product name should not exist simply to sound luxurious.

It should contribute to the identity, story, recognition, and long-term value of the product.

The strongest names should feel natural within the SAB’A world: culturally grounded, contemporary, memorable, and capable of standing alongside the product for many years.
