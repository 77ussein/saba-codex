# PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT

The SAB’A Product Development framework defines the process through which SAB’A products move from an initial concept to an approved, production-ready product.

The purpose of this document is to establish a controlled and intentional development process that protects product quality, brand identity, customer experience, and long-term consistency.

Product development should transform the principles established in the SAB’A Product Philosophy, Product Architecture, Product Categories, and Product Standards into real products through a structured process of research, development, testing, refinement, and approval.

This document defines the development framework. It does not replace Product Specifications, Quality Standards, Fragrance documentation, Packaging documentation, or other specialized technical requirements.

---

## 1. Development Philosophy

SAB’A product development should be intentional, disciplined, and iterative.

A product should not move directly from an idea to production without sufficient evaluation and refinement.

The development process should allow SAB’A to:

- Explore ideas
- Validate concepts
- Identify weaknesses
- Test product performance
- Refine the product
- Control quality
- Protect brand consistency
- Approve the final product with confidence

The objective is not simply to complete a product.

The objective is to develop the right product to the required SAB’A standard.

---

## 2. Development Principles

The SAB’A Product Development process should follow several fundamental principles:

- **Intentionality** — Every major decision should have a clear reason.
- **Quality First** — Quality should be protected throughout development.
- **Iteration** — Products should be improved through testing and refinement.
- **Validation** — Important decisions should be supported by evidence where possible.
- **Consistency** — Development should remain aligned with the wider product system.
- **Restraint** — Unnecessary complexity should be avoided.
- **Documentation** — Important decisions should be recorded.
- **Controlled Progression** — Products should move through defined stages.
- **Customer Focus** — The final product should provide a meaningful customer experience.
- **Long-Term Thinking** — Development should consider the future of the product and brand.

---

## 3. Development Lifecycle

The general SAB’A product development lifecycle is:

**Concept → Research → Creative Direction → Fragrance Development → Product Design → Component Development → Prototype → Testing → Refinement → Validation → Approval → Production**

Not every product will require identical activities or timelines.

However, the general sequence should provide a consistent framework for development.

Each stage should provide sufficient confidence before the product progresses to the next stage.

---

## 4. Stage 1 — Concept

The development process begins with a product concept.

The concept should establish the initial reason for creating the product.

A concept may define:

- Product purpose
- Intended customer
- Fragrance direction
- Emotional objective
- Cultural or creative inspiration
- Product positioning
- Relationship to the wider SAB’A portfolio

The concept should be clear enough to guide further development.

A concept should not be considered an approved product.

---

## 5. Stage 2 — Research

Research should provide relevant information to support development.

Research may include:

- Market research
- Customer research
- Competitive analysis
- Cultural research
- Fragrance research
- Material research
- Packaging research
- Product feasibility research

Research should inform decisions without causing SAB’A to imitate competitors or follow trends without purpose.

The purpose of research is to understand the environment in which the product will exist and identify opportunities for meaningful differentiation.

---

## 6. Stage 3 — Creative Direction

Once the concept and research are sufficiently developed, the product should receive a defined creative direction.

Creative direction may establish:

- Product character
- Visual direction
- Sensory direction
- Storytelling direction
- Cultural references
- Design principles
- Relationship to the SAB’A product family

The creative direction should translate the concept into a coherent product vision.

It should remain aligned with:

**Luxury Minimalism + Saba Heritage + Contemporary Global Positioning**

---

## 7. Stage 4 — Fragrance Development

For fragrance products, fragrance development is a central stage of the process.

The objective is to develop a fragrance that expresses the approved concept and meets the intended olfactive direction.

Development may include:

- Olfactive exploration
- Formula development
- Evaluation
- Modification
- Performance assessment
- Stability consideration
- Sample comparison
- Final formula refinement

The fragrance should remain the core sensory expression of the product.

Specific fragrance formulas, ingredients, concentrations, and technical records should be maintained within the SAB’A Fragrance Library and relevant product documentation.

---

## 8. Stage 5 — Product Design

Once the product direction is sufficiently defined, the physical product should be developed.

Product design may address:

- Bottle
- Cap
- Sprayer
- Identification elements
- Packaging
- Product proportions
- Physical interaction
- Overall presentation

The design should translate the product concept into a physical expression.

The design should remain consistent with the SAB’A Product Philosophy and Product Architecture.

Specific technical requirements should be documented separately.

---

## 9. Stage 6 — Component Development

Individual components should be evaluated as part of the complete product system.

Component development may involve:

- Component selection
- Material evaluation
- Compatibility evaluation
- Functional testing
- Assembly testing
- Visual evaluation
- Supplier samples
- Prototype components

Components should not be approved independently of the final product experience when their interaction affects product quality.

The objective is to ensure that all components work together functionally, visually, and sensorially.

---

## 10. Stage 7 — Prototype

A prototype should be created before final production whenever practical and appropriate.

The prototype provides an opportunity to evaluate the product as a physical object rather than as separate concepts or specifications.

Prototype evaluation may include:

- Physical appearance
- Proportion
- Weight
- Component interaction
- Functionality
- Packaging interaction
- Fragrance presentation
- Customer experience

The prototype should reveal issues that may not be visible during conceptual development.

---

## 11. Stage 8 — Testing

Testing should be used to validate important product decisions.

Testing may address:

- Fragrance performance
- Stability
- Component functionality
- Material compatibility
- Packaging protection
- Assembly
- User interaction
- Visual consistency
- Product handling

Testing requirements should be appropriate to the product and its intended use.

Testing should not be performed merely as a formality.

Its purpose is to identify weaknesses before final approval.

---

## 12. Stage 9 — Refinement

Findings from testing and evaluation should be used to refine the product.

Refinement may involve:

- Formula adjustments
- Component changes
- Design adjustments
- Packaging improvements
- Functional improvements
- Material changes
- Proportion adjustments
- User experience improvements

The product should be reviewed as a complete system after significant changes.

A change made to one component may affect other parts of the product.

---

## 13. Stage 10 — Validation

Before final approval, the product should undergo a structured validation process.

Validation should confirm that the product:

- Meets the approved concept
- Meets applicable Product Standards
- Functions correctly
- Provides the intended experience
- Maintains brand alignment
- Meets relevant quality requirements
- Is sufficiently stable and consistent
- Can be produced to the required standard

Validation should provide evidence that the product is ready to move toward final approval.

---

## 14. Stage 11 — Final Approval

A product should receive formal approval before entering final production.

Approval should confirm that the relevant product elements have been reviewed and accepted.

Depending on the product, approval may include:

- Fragrance approval
- Design approval
- Component approval
- Packaging approval
- Quality approval
- Regulatory approval
- Commercial approval

Approval records should be documented where appropriate.

No product should enter final production based solely on an informal or incomplete decision.

---

## 15. Stage 12 — Production Readiness

Before production begins, the product should be considered production-ready.

Production readiness should confirm the availability of:

- Approved product specifications
- Approved fragrance
- Approved components
- Approved packaging
- Required production processes
- Quality requirements
- Relevant documentation
- Required regulatory information
- Appropriate production resources

The production stage should reproduce the approved product as consistently as possible.

---

## 16. Development Documentation

Product development decisions should be documented throughout the process.

Documentation may include:

- Product concepts
- Research findings
- Creative directions
- Development notes
- Samples
- Prototypes
- Test results
- Evaluation records
- Revision history
- Approval records
- Final references

Documentation should make it possible to understand how the product developed from concept to final approval.

This creates continuity and reduces dependence on informal knowledge.

---

## 17. Development Changes

Changes should be controlled throughout development.

A significant change to an approved element should be evaluated before implementation.

Changes may affect:

- Fragrance
- Formula
- Bottle
- Cap
- Sprayer
- Packaging
- Materials
- Manufacturing
- Customer experience

Where a change may affect the approved product, the relevant stage should be reviewed again.

The objective is to prevent uncontrolled changes from entering the final product.

---

## 18. Development Gates

The development process should use decision points between major stages.

A simplified framework is:

**Concept Gate → Development Gate → Prototype Gate → Validation Gate → Approval Gate → Production**

At each gate, SAB’A should determine whether the product should:

- Proceed
- Be refined
- Return to an earlier stage
- Be placed on hold
- Be discontinued

A product should not progress simply because development has already begun.

Progress should be based on readiness.

---

## 19. Cross-Functional Review

A product should be evaluated from multiple perspectives when appropriate.

Depending on the stage, review may involve:

- Brand
- Product
- Fragrance
- Design
- Packaging
- Quality
- Operations
- Regulatory
- Commercial considerations

The purpose of cross-functional review is to identify conflicts or weaknesses before they become expensive or difficult to correct.

The product should ultimately remain one coherent system despite being developed through multiple areas.

---

## 20. Prototype and Sample Discipline

Samples and prototypes should be treated as development references rather than automatically as final products.

Each significant sample should be identifiable where practical.

Records may include:

- Version
- Date
- Purpose
- Changes
- Evaluation
- Status
- Next action

This creates a clear development history and reduces confusion between approved and unapproved versions.

---

## 21. Development Priorities

When competing decisions arise, SAB’A should prioritize:

1. Product safety
2. Fragrance quality
3. Functional reliability
4. Product quality
5. Brand alignment
6. Customer experience
7. Long-term consistency
8. Commercial practicality

Commercial considerations are important, but they should not automatically override essential product quality or brand integrity.

---

## 22. Development Efficiency

SAB’A should aim to develop products efficiently without sacrificing quality.

Efficiency should come from:

- Clear decision-making
- Early identification of risks
- Structured testing
- Controlled revisions
- Accurate documentation
- Appropriate supplier communication
- Clear approval points

Speed should not be treated as a substitute for development quality.

The objective is **controlled progress**, not simply faster progress.

---

## 23. Product Development and Sustainability

Where appropriate, product development should consider the long-term impact of materials, production choices, packaging, and product lifecycle.

Considerations may include:

- Material efficiency
- Packaging efficiency
- Durability
- Waste reduction
- Product longevity
- Responsible sourcing

These considerations should be balanced with product quality, safety, functionality, and the intended luxury experience.

Specific sustainability requirements may be developed as the SAB’A operational and product systems mature.

---

## 24. Product Development and Product Specifications

Product Development defines how the product is created.

Product Specifications define what the approved product must be.

The relationship is:

**Development Process → Approved Product → Product Specifications**

Once the product reaches an approved state, its final technical requirements should be documented in:

**Product Specifications.md**

This separation ensures that specifications represent approved decisions rather than unvalidated ideas.

---

## 25. Product Development and Quality Standards

Product Development and Quality Standards operate together.

Product Development creates and refines the product.

Quality Standards establish the level the product must achieve.

The relationship can be understood as:

**Develop → Test → Refine → Validate → Verify Against Standards → Approve**

Quality should therefore be considered throughout development rather than inspected only at the end.

---

## 26. Product Development and Future Categories

The current development framework is primarily designed around SAB’A fragrance products.

If SAB’A formally introduces additional product categories in the future, the development process should be adapted to the specific requirements of each category.

Future category development may require:

- Additional development stages
- Category-specific testing
- Specialized technical expertise
- Additional quality requirements
- Category-specific regulatory review
- Specialized production processes

However, all future categories should continue to follow the core SAB’A development principles established in this document.

The framework is:

**Core SAB’A Development Process → Category-Specific Development Requirements → Product-Specific Development**

This allows the system to evolve without losing its underlying structure.

---

## 27. Development Completion Criteria

A product development process may be considered complete when:

- The concept has been validated
- The fragrance has been approved
- The physical product has been approved
- Required components have been validated
- Packaging has been approved
- Testing requirements have been completed
- Quality requirements have been met
- Relevant documentation has been completed
- Required regulatory requirements have been addressed
- Final approval has been granted
- Production readiness has been confirmed

Only after these conditions are appropriately satisfied should the product proceed to final production.

---

## 28. Core Principle

The SAB’A Product Development philosophy can be summarized as:

> **Develop deliberately. Test honestly. Refine continuously. Approve confidently.**

A product should not be considered finished simply because it looks complete.

It is finished when its concept, fragrance, physical expression, functionality, quality, experience, and supporting systems have been sufficiently developed and validated.

The objective of Product Development is therefore not simply to move an idea into production.

It is to transform an idea into a product that deserves to carry the SAB’A name.
