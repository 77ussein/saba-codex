# PRODUCT STANDARDS

The SAB’A Product Standards define the minimum quality, performance, design, and experience requirements that a product must meet before it can be considered an official SAB’A product.

At the current stage of the brand, these standards are primarily established for fragrance products, which represent the foundational product category of SAB’A.

The purpose of this document is to protect product quality, consistency, and brand integrity while providing a clear standard for product evaluation and approval.

These standards define the expected level of the product without prematurely establishing specific technical specifications, formulas, materials, dimensions, suppliers, or manufacturing methods.

---

## 1. Standard Philosophy

Every SAB’A product should meet a level of quality consistent with the positioning of the brand as a contemporary luxury fragrance house.

Product quality should be evaluated as a complete system rather than through a single component.

The standard should consider:

- Fragrance quality
- Product performance
- Material quality
- Physical construction
- Design consistency
- Packaging quality
- User experience
- Brand alignment
- Manufacturing consistency
- Regulatory compliance

No individual strength should be used to compensate for a significant weakness in another essential area.

---

## 2. Fragrance Quality

The fragrance is the core of the SAB’A product.

Every fragrance should demonstrate:

- A clear and intentional olfactive identity
- Balanced composition
- Consistent character
- Appropriate development over time
- A refined sensory experience
- Consistency between approved samples and final production

The fragrance should feel intentional and complete rather than simply functional.

The quality of the fragrance should remain the primary consideration throughout product development.

---

## 3. Fragrance Performance

A SAB’A fragrance should provide a performance level appropriate to its intended positioning and formulation.

Performance evaluation may consider:

- Longevity
- Projection
- Sillage
- Development on skin
- Stability over time
- Consistency between batches

Performance should be evaluated in relation to the intended fragrance character.

Performance should not be pursued at the expense of balance, refinement, or the intended olfactive experience.

Specific performance targets remain subject to fragrance development and validation.

---

## 4. Formula and Ingredient Integrity

Fragrance formulas and ingredients should meet the required standards for quality, safety, stability, and intended performance.

Ingredients should be selected based on their suitability for the final fragrance rather than cost alone.

The product should maintain consistency between:

- Approved formula
- Approved materials
- Production formula
- Final product

Any significant change to an approved formula or essential ingredient should be evaluated and approved through the appropriate development and quality processes.

Specific formulas and ingredient records should be maintained separately within the fragrance documentation system.

---

## 5. Product Stability

A SAB’A fragrance should demonstrate appropriate stability throughout its intended lifecycle.

Stability evaluation should consider factors such as:

- Fragrance integrity
- Appearance
- Color stability
- Packaging compatibility
- Component interaction
- Storage conditions
- Expected product lifespan

The final product should remain within approved quality parameters throughout its intended use and storage conditions.

Specific testing methods and acceptance criteria should be defined during product development and quality validation.

---

## 6. Bottle and Physical Construction

The physical product should communicate quality through its construction, proportion, and execution.

The bottle and associated components should demonstrate:

- Structural integrity
- Appropriate weight and presence
- Clean construction
- Consistent finishing
- Reliable functionality
- Compatibility between components

The physical product should feel substantial and refined without unnecessary complexity.

Specific bottle dimensions, materials, component specifications, and suppliers remain subject to Product Specifications and approved development processes.

---

## 7. Cap and Sprayer Quality

The cap and sprayer are functional and sensory components of the product experience.

They should provide:

- Reliable operation
- Appropriate fit
- Consistent performance
- Secure assembly
- Appropriate tactile quality
- Compatibility with the overall product design

The interaction between the customer and these components should feel deliberate and premium.

Any selected component should be evaluated as part of the complete product rather than as an isolated part.

---

## 8. Design Quality

The physical design of a SAB’A fragrance should reflect the established product philosophy and architecture.

The design should demonstrate:

- Clean geometry
- Considered proportion
- Visual balance
- Restraint
- Distinctiveness
- Timelessness
- Contemporary relevance

Design elements should have a clear purpose.

Unnecessary decoration, excessive visual complexity, or trend-dependent elements should be avoided unless specifically justified.

---

## 9. Brand Alignment

Every fragrance product must remain clearly connected to the SAB’A brand.

The product should reinforce:

**Luxury Minimalism + Saba Heritage + Contemporary Global Positioning**

Cultural references should remain:

- Subtle
- Refined
- Contemporary
- Meaningful
- Purposeful

The product should communicate its connection to Saba without becoming folkloric, stereotypical, or overly decorative.

---

## 10. Packaging Quality

Packaging should provide both protection and a premium product experience.

Packaging should demonstrate:

- Structural integrity
- Appropriate material quality
- Clean construction
- Accurate printing
- Consistent finishing
- Clear product identification
- Protection of the product during handling and storage

Packaging should complement the fragrance and physical product rather than compete with them.

Specific packaging materials, dimensions, finishes, and production specifications should be defined separately.

---

## 11. Visual Consistency

All product elements should operate as one visual system.

Consistency should be maintained across:

- Logo application
- Typography
- Color usage
- Product identification
- Packaging
- Bottle presentation
- Supporting visual elements

The product should remain recognizable as SAB’A even when viewed without extensive brand explanation.

---

## 12. Functional Performance

Luxury products must also perform reliably.

Every essential product component should function according to its intended purpose.

This includes:

- Spraying
- Opening and closing
- Component fit
- Packaging protection
- Product handling
- Storage
- General usability

Functional reliability is a fundamental part of perceived quality.

A visually premium product that performs poorly does not meet the SAB’A standard.

---

## 13. Tactile Experience

The physical interaction with the product should contribute to the overall luxury experience.

The product should communicate quality through:

- Weight
- Surface quality
- Texture
- Movement
- Component interaction
- Opening experience

Tactile qualities should feel intentional rather than excessive.

The objective is to create a product that feels premium before and during use.

---

## 14. Customer Experience

The product should provide a coherent experience from first interaction through continued use.

The experience may include:

- First visual impression
- Handling
- Opening
- Fragrance application
- Product interaction
- Packaging experience
- Overall perception

Every stage should support the same sense of quality and intentionality.

The customer should experience the product as a complete expression of SAB’A rather than as a collection of unrelated components.

---

## 15. Manufacturing Consistency

Approved SAB’A products should be manufactured consistently.

Production should maintain consistency across:

- Fragrance
- Bottle
- Components
- Packaging
- Printing
- Finishing
- Assembly

The final product should remain aligned with the approved reference standard.

Manufacturing variation should be identified, evaluated, and controlled where it can affect product quality or brand perception.

---

## 16. Quality Control

Quality control should be integrated into product development and production.

Quality checks should be used to identify defects, inconsistencies, or deviations before products reach the customer.

Quality evaluation may include:

- Incoming component inspection
- Fragrance evaluation
- Assembly inspection
- Packaging inspection
- Visual inspection
- Functional testing
- Final product review

Specific inspection procedures and acceptance criteria should be documented within the appropriate quality and operational systems.

---

## 17. Presentation Standard

The final product should be presented at a level consistent with a luxury fragrance house.

Before release, the complete product should be reviewed as a whole:

**Fragrance + Bottle + Components + Packaging + Presentation**

The final result should feel:

- Refined
- Cohesive
- Premium
- Intentional
- Complete

No major visible or functional weakness should remain unresolved before approval.

---

## 18. Regulatory and Safety Compliance

All SAB’A fragrance products must comply with the applicable legal, regulatory, safety, labeling, and product requirements of the markets in which they are sold.

Compliance should be considered throughout product development rather than treated as a final-stage requirement.

Relevant documentation, testing, labeling, and regulatory requirements should be maintained within the appropriate SAB’A Codex legal, product, and operational documentation.

Specific regulatory requirements will depend on the target market and product format.

---

## 19. Product Approval Standard

A fragrance should not be considered ready for official release until it has been evaluated against the relevant SAB’A product standards.

The approval process should confirm that the product is:

- Safe
- Functional
- Consistent
- Refined
- Cohesive
- Brand-aligned
- Adequately tested
- Appropriately presented
- Compliant with applicable requirements

A product that does not meet essential standards should not proceed to final release until the identified issues have been resolved or formally accepted through the appropriate approval process.

---

## 20. Minimum Standard vs. Product Excellence

The Product Standards define the minimum level required for a product to be accepted as a SAB’A product.

Meeting the minimum standard does not mean that the product has reached its highest possible level of excellence.

SAB’A should continuously seek improvement in:

- Quality
- Craftsmanship
- Performance
- Materials
- Experience
- Consistency
- Product refinement

The standard protects the minimum threshold.

Product development and creative ambition should continue to push beyond that threshold.

---

## 21. Future Product Category Standards

The current Product Standards are primarily designed for SAB’A fragrance products.

As SAB’A develops and formally approves additional product categories, each new category should receive its own category-specific standards.

Future category standards should:

- Build upon the core SAB’A Product Standards
- Reflect the functional requirements of the new category
- Define category-specific quality expectations
- Establish appropriate performance requirements
- Address relevant materials and construction
- Consider category-specific safety and regulatory requirements
- Maintain consistency with the SAB’A brand and product architecture

Future category standards should not be assumed or finalized before the relevant category is formally approved.

The framework is therefore:

**Core SAB’A Product Standards → Category-Specific Standards → Product-Specific Requirements**

This allows SAB’A to expand its product portfolio without weakening the standards or identity established by the brand.

---

## 22. Core Principle

The SAB’A Product Standard can be summarized as:

> **Every product carrying the SAB’A name must earn its place through quality, consistency, function, refinement, and meaning.**

The objective is not simply to produce a functional fragrance.

The objective is to create a complete luxury product that meets the expectations of SAB’A as a contemporary fragrance house rooted in the legacy of Saba.

Quality should be intentional.

Execution should be consistent.

Every essential element should meet the standard.
