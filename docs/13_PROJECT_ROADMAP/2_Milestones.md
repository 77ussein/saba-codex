# Milestones

The SAB’A Milestones document defines the major points of progress that indicate meaningful advancement of the SAB’A project.

Milestones translate the broader Project Roadmap into identifiable achievements that can be reviewed, validated, and recorded.

A milestone represents meaningful progress toward a defined outcome. It should not be treated as a simple task or activity.

The purpose of milestones is to help SAB’A understand what has been achieved, what remains dependent on further work, and when the project is ready to move into its next major stage.

---

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to establish a structured milestone framework for SAB’A.

It provides a reference for:

- Measuring meaningful project progress
- Identifying major completion points
- Connecting project activities to strategic outcomes
- Establishing readiness for subsequent phases
- Supporting project reviews
- Improving accountability
- Identifying delays and dependencies
- Supporting decision-making
- Preserving project continuity

Milestones should provide clarity without creating unnecessary administrative complexity.

---

## 2. Milestone Philosophy

SAB’A should measure progress by meaningful outcomes rather than activity alone.

Completing a task does not necessarily mean that a milestone has been achieved.

For example:

- Contacting a supplier is an activity.
- Selecting and validating a suitable supplier is a milestone.

Similarly:

- Creating a product sample is an activity.
- Validating the product sample against defined standards is a milestone.

A milestone should therefore represent a meaningful change in the state or readiness of the project.

---

## 3. Milestone Principles

### 3.1 Outcome-Based

Milestones should describe meaningful outcomes rather than lists of actions.

### 3.2 Evidence-Based

A milestone should be considered complete when there is sufficient evidence that its intended outcome has been achieved.

### 3.3 Sequential Where Necessary

Some milestones depend on earlier milestones and should not be treated as independent.

### 3.4 Flexible Where Appropriate

Not every milestone requires a fixed calendar date.

Timing may change as information, resources, suppliers, or business conditions change.

### 3.5 Traceable

Important milestone decisions should be recorded so that the project can later understand why a milestone was considered complete, delayed, revised, or reopened.

### 3.6 Quality-Protected

A milestone should not be marked complete simply to maintain schedule momentum if its quality requirements have not been satisfied.

## 20. Milestone Dependencies

Milestones should be understood as a connected system.

Examples include:

**Brand Definition → Product Definition → Product Development → Validation → Production**

**Packaging Direction → Packaging Prototype → Packaging Specification → Procurement → Production**

**Cost Model → Cost Validation → Pricing Approval → Commercial Launch**

**Digital Foundation → E-Commerce Readiness → Launch Readiness → Launch**

**Customer Experience Framework → Service Readiness → Launch → Post-Launch Improvement**

**Financial Readiness → Production Commitment → Launch → Growth Assessment**

Dependencies should be reviewed whenever a milestone is delayed or changed.

## 22. Milestone Completion Criteria

A milestone should generally require four elements:

### 22.1 Defined Outcome

The intended result is clearly understood.

### 22.2 Evidence

There is sufficient evidence that the outcome has been achieved.

### 22.3 Review

The result has been reviewed by the appropriate decision owner.

### 22.4 Record

The milestone status and relevant decision are documented where appropriate.

---

## 23. Milestone Reopening

A completed milestone may be reopened when new information materially changes its validity.

Examples include:

- Supplier change
- Formula change
- Packaging change
- Regulatory change
- Material quality issue
- Significant cost change
- Customer evidence
- Operational failure

Reopening a milestone is not automatically a failure.

It is a mechanism for maintaining accuracy and control.

---

## 24. Milestone Review

Milestones should be reviewed according to their importance and stage.

A review should consider:

- Current status
- Evidence
- Dependencies
- Delays
- Risks
- Changes
- Next action
- Impact on the roadmap

Major milestones should receive greater attention than routine activities.

## 25. Milestones and the Project Roadmap

The Project Roadmap defines the broader direction and phases of SAB’A.

Milestones provide identifiable points that demonstrate progress through those phases.

The relationship can be summarized as:

**Roadmap = Direction**

**Milestones = Evidence of Progress**

The two should remain synchronized.

If major project direction changes, milestone sequencing may also require review.

---

## 26. Milestones and Project Plans

Milestones should not replace detailed project plans.

A project plan may contain:

- Tasks
- Owners
- Deadlines
- Dependencies
- Resources
- Deliverables

Milestones represent the meaningful outcomes those activities are intended to produce.

Detailed execution should therefore remain flexible while milestone outcomes remain clear.

---

## 27. Milestone Prioritization

When multiple milestones compete for limited resources, SAB’A should generally prioritize milestones that:

1. Protect product quality
2. Protect customer safety and compliance
3. Remove critical dependencies
4. Reduce material risk
5. Improve financial visibility
6. Enable the next major stage
7. Improve customer readiness
8. Support sustainable growth

Priority should be based on strategic importance and dependency, not simply on which milestone is easiest to complete.
