# Launch Plan

The SAB’A Launch Plan defines how SAB’A moves from final preparation into controlled commercial launch.

It connects product, packaging, inventory, operations, digital commerce, customer experience, marketing, finance, and compliance into one coordinated launch process.

The objective is to launch when SAB’A is sufficiently prepared to deliver its product and customer promise reliably.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the framework for:

- Launch preparation
- Readiness assessment
- Launch execution
- Early monitoring
- Post-launch review

It provides the launch framework without replacing the detailed standards and operating documents within the SAB’A Codex.

---

## 2. Launch Principles

SAB’A should launch according to the following principles:

- Readiness over haste
- Quality over volume
- Evidence over assumptions
- Customer trust over short-term attention
- Financial discipline over uncontrolled spending
- Controlled execution over unnecessary complexity

Marketing activity must remain aligned with actual product availability and operational capacity.

---

## 3. Launch Scope

Before launch, SAB’A should define:

- Product or products being launched
- Target market
- Sales channels
- Launch pricing
- Available inventory
- Customer proposition
- Launch window
- Marketing direction

The launch scope should remain consistent with the Project Roadmap.

---

## 4. Product and Packaging Readiness

The product and packaging must be sufficiently finalized and approved.

Readiness should include:

- Approved fragrance and formula
- Product specifications
- Bottle and components
- Packaging
- Quality requirements
- Required product records

No critical unresolved product-quality or packaging issue should remain at launch.

---

## 5. Supply and Inventory Readiness

SAB’A should confirm sufficient saleable inventory and supporting materials.

This includes:

- Finished products
- Packaging components
- Storage
- Inventory records
- Supplier availability
- Reorder requirements

Saleable inventory should account for quality holds, damaged units, samples, and other planned allocations where applicable.

---

## 6. Operational Readiness

The complete order lifecycle should be prepared:

**Order → Processing → Quality Check → Packing → Shipping → Delivery → Support**

SAB’A should have clear processes for:

- Inventory handling
- Fulfillment
- Shipping
- Returns and exchanges
- Customer support

Critical processes should be tested before launch where practical.

---

## 7. Digital Commerce Readiness

The digital purchasing experience should be reviewed end-to-end.

This includes:

- Website
- Mobile experience
- Product pages
- Product information
- Cart
- Checkout
- Payments
- Shipping
- Customer accounts
- Authentication
- Order confirmation

Critical technical, payment, or checkout issues must be resolved before launch.

---

## 8. Customer Experience Readiness

The customer experience should be prepared across:

1. Discovery
2. Product consideration
3. Purchase
4. Confirmation
5. Fulfillment
6. Delivery
7. Product experience
8. Support
9. After-sales

Customer-facing communication should follow SAB’A Brand Voice and Customer Experience standards.

---

## 9. Content and Marketing Readiness

Launch content should accurately represent the approved product and brand.

Key assets may include:

- Product photography
- Product video
- Product descriptions
- Product story
- Brand story
- Website content
- Social content
- Campaign assets
- Launch communications

All claims, specifications, availability, and pricing must be verified before publication.

---

## 10. Financial Readiness

Before launch, SAB’A should understand:

- Product cost
- Packaging cost
- Shipping and fulfillment costs
- Applicable taxes and duties
- Marketing expenditure
- Payment costs
- Expected margin
- Working capital requirements
- Available cash

Planning assumptions must remain clearly distinguished from validated figures.

Pricing should be approved before commercial launch.

---

## 11. Legal and Compliance Readiness

Applicable legal, regulatory, importation, product, labeling, privacy, and commercial requirements should be identified and addressed before launch.

Where professional or regulatory interpretation is required, appropriate professional or official guidance should be obtained.

No launch date should take priority over a mandatory legal or regulatory requirement.

---

## 12. Final Launch Review

Before launch, SAB’A should conduct one cross-functional readiness review covering:

- Product and packaging
- Quality
- Inventory
- Operations
- Digital commerce
- Customer experience
- Marketing
- Finance
- Legal and compliance

The review should identify unresolved critical risks, dependencies, or conditions.

---

## 13. Launch Decision

The final decision should be one of:

- **Ready** — Launch may proceed.
- **Ready with Conditions** — Launch may proceed with controlled non-critical limitations.
- **Delayed** — Specific requirements must be completed first.
- **Not Ready** — Critical issues prevent launch.

The decision should be documented with the date, scope, risks, conditions, and required follow-up.

---

## 14. Launch Execution

Once approved, launch execution should coordinate:

1. Final inventory confirmation
2. Final digital verification
3. Product availability
4. Marketing activation
5. Order monitoring
6. Fulfillment
7. Customer communication
8. Financial monitoring
9. Issue resolution

Launch activity should be actively monitored throughout the initial period.

---

## 15. Post-Launch Review

After sufficient early data is available, SAB’A should review:

- Sales and revenue
- Customer response
- Product feedback
- Inventory movement
- Fulfillment and shipping
- Returns and support
- Marketing performance
- Actual costs
- Cash flow

Actual results should be compared with the assumptions used during planning.

Findings may lead to:

- Continued execution
- Operational improvements
- Inventory adjustments
- Marketing adjustments
- Pricing review
- Product or experience improvements
- Preparation for the next stage

---

## 16. Launch Records

Important launch records should be maintained where applicable:

- Launch scope
- Readiness review
- Product approval
- Inventory confirmation
- Pricing approval
- Compliance confirmation
- Launch decision
- Major issues
- Post-launch review
- Corrective actions

These records provide accountability, continuity, and reliable reference for future decisions.

---

## 17. Core Principle

SAB’A should launch when it is ready to fulfill its promise.

A successful launch is not simply the moment a product becomes available for purchase. It is the point at which product, brand, operations, commerce, customer experience, marketing, finance, and compliance are sufficiently aligned to support a controlled and credible market entry.
