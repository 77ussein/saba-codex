# Procurement
## Purpose
Procurement defines how SAB’A identifies, evaluates, sources, purchases, and manages the materials, components, products, and services required for operations.
The objective is to secure the right requirements at the right quality, quantity, timing, and commercial terms while protecting product standards and operational continuity.
## Procurement Principles
1. **Quality before Cost**
2. **Specification before Substitution**
3. **Reliability before Convenience**
4. **Planning before Urgency**
5. **Transparency before Assumption**
6. **Long-Term Relationships over Short-Term Savings**
7. **Traceability where Practical**
8. **Operational Continuity without Compromising Standards**
## Procurement Scope
Procurement may cover:
- Fragrance materials
- Raw materials
- Packaging components
- Finished goods
- Manufacturing services
- Printing
- Logistics services
- Warehousing services
- Operational supplies
- Technology and business services
The procurement approach should reflect the importance and risk of each category.
## Requirements
Before procurement begins, requirements should be sufficiently defined.
Requirements may include:
- Item or service
- Specification
- Quantity
- Quality requirements
- Required date
- Delivery location
- Approved alternatives
- Budget or target cost
- Compliance requirements
Purchasing should not proceed on assumptions where an approved specification is required.
## Supplier Selection
Suppliers should be evaluated according to relevant criteria, including:
- Quality
- Reliability
- Capacity
- Lead time
- Communication
- Compliance
- Pricing
- Payment terms
- Minimum order quantities
- Production capabilities
- Geographic and logistical suitability
The importance of each criterion should depend on the category being sourced.
## Supplier Qualification
Critical suppliers may require additional qualification before approval.
Qualification may include:
- Company information
- Product or service documentation
- Samples
- Technical specifications
- Certifications where relevant
- Quality records
- References or previous performance
- Commercial terms
Supplier approval should be documented where the relationship is operationally significant.
## Quotations and Commercial Evaluation
Where practical, significant purchases should be evaluated using comparable commercial information.
Evaluation may consider:
- Unit cost
- Total landed cost
- Minimum order quantity
- Lead time
- Shipping
- Payment terms
- Tooling or setup costs
- Quality requirements
- Expected order frequency
The lowest quoted price should not automatically be considered the best option.
## Purchase Orders
Purchase orders or equivalent documented confirmations should clearly identify:
- Supplier
- Item or service
- Specification
- Quantity
- Agreed price
- Delivery requirements
- Payment terms
- Relevant conditions
Changes to confirmed orders should be documented.
## Quality Requirements
Procured materials and components must meet approved specifications.
Where applicable, incoming materials should be checked for:
- Identity
- Quantity
- Condition
- Specification
- Documentation
- Packaging integrity
- Batch or lot information
Non-conforming materials should not enter normal production or inventory without appropriate review.
## Substitution
Supplier or material substitutions must not be made solely for convenience or short-term cost reduction.
Any material or component that affects product quality, performance, safety, appearance, fragrance, packaging, or customer experience should follow the appropriate approval process before substitution.
## Lead Times and Planning
Procurement should account for:
- Supplier lead time
- Production lead time
- Shipping time
- Customs or regulatory requirements where applicable
- Inspection time
- Buffer requirements
- Launch deadlines
Critical materials should be planned early enough to reduce avoidable operational risk.
## Cost Control
Procurement should support financial discipline without compromising established standards.
Cost evaluation should consider total operational impact rather than purchase price alone.
Relevant factors may include:
- Quality failures
- Waste
- Minimum order quantities
- Storage
- Shipping
- Delays
- Rework
- Supplier reliability
## Supplier Performance
Supplier performance should be monitored where practical.
Possible measures include:
- On-time delivery
- Quality consistency
- Order accuracy
- Lead-time reliability
- Communication
- Responsiveness
- Pricing consistency
- Issue resolution
Repeated performance problems should trigger review and corrective action.
## Risk Management
Procurement risks may include:
- Single-source dependency
- Supplier failure
- Material shortages
- Long lead times
- Price volatility
- Quality inconsistency
- Logistics disruption
- Regulatory restrictions
Critical dependencies should be identified and appropriate mitigation considered.
## Documentation and Records
Procurement records should be maintained according to the importance of the purchase.
Records may include:
- Supplier information
- Quotations
- Evaluations
- Purchase orders
- Confirmations
- Specifications
- Certificates or technical documents
- Delivery records
- Quality records
- Supplier performance reviews
Relevant records should remain traceable to the applicable purchase or supplier.
## Ethical and Responsible Procurement
SAB’A should conduct procurement professionally and transparently.
Procurement decisions should avoid:
- Conflicts of interest
- Misrepresentation
- Unapproved commitments
- Hidden commercial arrangements
- Deliberate circumvention of standards
Relevant legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements should be respected.
## Core Principle
> Procurement should secure what SAB’A needs without compromising what SAB’A stands for.
