# Production Operations
## Purpose
Production Operations defines how SAB’A products are planned, prepared, produced, inspected, documented, and released.
The objective is to ensure that production consistently follows approved specifications while maintaining quality, traceability, efficiency, and operational control.
## Production Principles
1. **Specification before Production**
2. **Quality before Speed**
3. **Consistency before Volume**
4. **Traceability where Required**
5. **Documentation before Assumption**
6. **Control before Scale**
7. **Repeatability over Improvisation**
## Production Planning
Production planning should translate demand and inventory requirements into defined production needs.
Planning may consider:
- Product demand
- Current inventory
- Production capacity
- Material availability
- Packaging availability
- Supplier lead times
- Launch schedules
- Production priorities
- Required completion dates
Production should not be scheduled without sufficient consideration of critical material and operational requirements.
## Production Readiness
Before production begins, the required inputs should be confirmed.
Readiness may include:
- Approved product specification
- Approved formula where applicable
- Required materials
- Packaging components
- Equipment
- Production area
- Personnel
- Procedures
- Quality requirements
- Documentation
- Batch identification
Production should not proceed when a critical requirement is unresolved.
## Materials
Materials used in production should be:
- Approved
- Correctly identified
- Within applicable specifications
- Available in the required quantity
- Properly stored
- Traceable where required
Materials that are damaged, expired, quarantined, or otherwise restricted must not be used without appropriate authorization.
## Production Process
Production should follow the approved process for the relevant product.
A general production flow may include:
**Material Preparation**  
→ **Production / Filling / Assembly**  
→ **In-Process Checks**  
→ **Finishing**  
→ **Packaging**  
→ **Final Inspection**  
→ **Release**
The exact process should be defined according to the product.
## Batch Control
Where products are produced in batches, each batch should have an appropriate identification reference.
Batch records may include:
- Product
- Batch number
- Production date
- Materials used
- Quantities
- Process information
- Personnel or responsible party
- In-process checks
- Deviations
- Final results
- Release status
Batch documentation should provide sufficient traceability for the product category.
## In-Process Controls
Where appropriate, production should include checks during the process rather than relying only on final inspection.
Checks may include:
- Quantity
- Appearance
- Fill level
- Assembly
- Component placement
- Process parameters
- Product characteristics
- Packaging integrity
Requirements should be defined according to the product and applicable standards.
## Quality Control
Quality checks should verify that the finished product meets approved requirements.
Evaluation may include:
- Product appearance
- Packaging
- Labeling
- Quantity
- Fragrance characteristics where applicable
- Functional requirements
- Batch information
- General condition
Products that do not meet requirements should not be released as approved inventory.
## Deviations
A deviation occurs when production does not follow an approved requirement or expected process.
Deviations should be:
1. Identified
2. Documented
3. Assessed
4. Corrected where appropriate
5. Reviewed for impact
6. Closed through the appropriate process
Significant or recurring deviations should trigger further investigation.
## Rework
Rework should only be performed when:
- The product remains suitable for rework
- The rework process is defined or appropriately approved
- Quality can be verified afterward
- Traceability is maintained
Rework should never be used to conceal recurring production problems.
## Non-Conforming Products
Non-conforming products should be clearly identified and separated from approved inventory.
Possible dispositions may include:
- Rework
- Repair
- Repackaging
- Return to supplier
- Controlled alternative use
- Disposal
The appropriate disposition should be documented.
## Packaging Operations
Packaging operations should ensure that the correct:
- Bottle or container
- Closure
- Label
- Carton
- Insert
- Protective component
- Product information
are used for the relevant product.
Packaging should protect the product while maintaining approved SAB’A standards.
## Production Environment
Production areas should be maintained according to the requirements of the product and applicable standards.
Considerations may include:
- Cleanliness
- Organization
- Environmental conditions
- Equipment condition
- Material separation
- Safety
- Access control
The production environment should support consistent and safe execution.
## Equipment
Production equipment should be appropriate for its intended use and maintained in suitable condition.
Where required, equipment should be:
- Cleaned
- Inspected
- Maintained
- Calibrated
- Documented
Equipment issues that may affect product quality should be addressed before continued use.
## Production Records
Production records should be maintained according to the importance and requirements of the product.
Records may include:
- Production plans
- Batch records
- Material records
- Quality checks
- Equipment records
- Deviations
- Rework records
- Release records
- Disposal records
Records should remain accessible and traceable.
## Product Release
Finished products should only be released to available inventory when required production and quality checks have been completed.
Release status should be clear and distinguish approved products from restricted or pending products.
## Production Performance
Production performance may be monitored through:
- Output
- Production lead time
- Yield
- Waste
- Batch consistency
- Rework rate
- Defect rate
- Downtime
- On-time completion
- Production cost
Performance data should be used to improve reliability and efficiency.
## Continuous Improvement
Production processes should be reviewed when:
- Repeated issues occur
- Quality problems emerge
- Capacity changes
- Product specifications change
- New equipment is introduced
- Efficiency improvements are identified
Improvements should be evaluated carefully to ensure that efficiency does not compromise established standards.
## Core Principle
> Production should make the intended SAB’A product repeatable — not merely possible once.
