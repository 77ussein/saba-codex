# Fulfillment
## Purpose
Fulfillment defines how SAB’A customer orders are processed, prepared, packed, dispatched, tracked, and completed.
The objective is to deliver the correct product to the correct customer in the expected condition and within the communicated timeframe while protecting the intended SAB’A experience.
## Fulfillment Principles
1. **Accuracy before Speed**
2. **Customer Experience throughout the Process**
3. **Clear Communication**
4. **Reliable Execution**
5. **Product Protection**
6. **Traceability where Required**
7. **Efficient Handling without Compromising Standards**
## Fulfillment Flow
The standard fulfillment process may follow:
**Order Received**  
→ **Order Verification**  
→ **Inventory Confirmation**  
→ **Picking**  
→ **Quality Check**  
→ **Packing**  
→ **Dispatch**  
→ **Tracking**  
→ **Delivery**  
→ **Completion**
The exact process may vary according to order type, destination, and operational requirements.
## Order Verification
Before fulfillment begins, the order should be checked for:
- Order number
- Customer information
- Product
- Quantity
- Availability
- Shipping address
- Payment status
- Applicable notes or requirements
Orders with unresolved issues should be held until the issue is appropriately addressed.
## Inventory Allocation
Products should only be allocated when sufficient approved inventory is available.
Inventory status should be updated appropriately to reduce the risk of:
- Overselling
- Duplicate allocation
- Stock discrepancies
- Fulfillment delays
## Picking
Picking should verify:
- Correct SKU
- Correct quantity
- Product condition
- Appropriate batch or lot where required
Products should be handled carefully to prevent damage or contamination.
## Quality Check
Before packing, the order should be checked for:
- Product condition
- Correct quantity
- Correct product
- Packaging condition
- Required inserts or components
- Order-specific requirements
The level of inspection should reflect the product and fulfillment process.
## Packing
Packaging should protect the product during handling and transportation while maintaining the intended SAB’A presentation.
Packing may include:
- Product
- Primary packaging
- Protective materials
- Brand materials
- Documentation
- Customer inserts where applicable
Packaging materials should be selected according to product protection, presentation, and operational requirements.
## Dispatch
Before dispatch, the shipment should be verified for:
- Correct destination
- Shipping service
- Package identification
- Tracking information
- Required documentation
Dispatch information should be recorded where appropriate.
## Shipping and Tracking
Customers should receive relevant shipping information according to the communication standards established by SAB’A.
Tracking information should be provided when available.
Shipping performance should be monitored to identify recurring delays or service issues.
## Delivery
Fulfillment is considered complete when the order has been delivered according to the applicable delivery confirmation process.
Delivery issues should be handled through defined customer service and logistics procedures.
## Exceptions
Fulfillment exceptions may include:
- Incorrect inventory
- Missing product
- Damaged product
- Incorrect address
- Failed delivery
- Shipping delay
- Lost shipment
- Order discrepancy
- Customer-requested change
Exceptions should be:
1. Identified
2. Documented
3. Communicated where necessary
4. Resolved
5. Reviewed when recurring
## Damaged Shipments
If a product is damaged during fulfillment or transportation, the issue should be documented and assessed.
Possible actions may include:
- Replacement
- Refund
- Return
- Carrier claim
- Internal investigation
The response should follow SAB’A customer experience and returns standards.
## Returns and Exchanges
Returned products should be processed according to the approved Returns and Exchanges policy.
Returned inventory should be assessed before being returned to available stock.
Products that do not meet the requirements for resale should remain restricted.
## Customer Communication
Fulfillment communication should be:
- Clear
- Accurate
- Timely
- Relevant
- Respectful
Customers should not be given delivery promises that cannot reasonably be supported.
## Fulfillment Performance
Performance may be monitored through:
- Order accuracy
- Fulfillment time
- Dispatch time
- Delivery performance
- Damage rate
- Failed delivery rate
- Return rate
- Fulfillment cost
- Customer complaints
- Customer satisfaction
Metrics should be used to identify operational improvements.
## Fulfillment and Customer Experience
Fulfillment is part of the SAB’A customer experience.
The customer should experience continuity between:
**Brand Promise**  
→ **Purchase**  
→ **Order Confirmation**  
→ **Unboxing**  
→ **Product Arrival**
Operational execution should support the same level of care established across the wider SAB’A experience.
## Documentation
Relevant fulfillment records may include:
- Orders
- Picking records
- Packing records
- Dispatch records
- Tracking information
- Delivery confirmation
- Exception records
- Return records
- Damage reports
Records should remain accessible according to operational and legal requirements.
## Continuous Improvement
Fulfillment processes should be reviewed when:
- Delivery performance declines
- Errors increase
- Customer complaints recur
- Shipping costs change materially
- Order volume increases
- New logistics providers are introduced
Improvements should focus on reliability, efficiency, and customer experience.
## Core Principle
> Fulfillment is not the final operational step. It is the moment when SAB’A’s promise reaches the customer’s hands.
