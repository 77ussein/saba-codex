# Suppliers
## Purpose
Supplier management defines how SAB’A selects, qualifies, evaluates, develops, and maintains relationships with suppliers and operational partners.
The objective is to build a reliable supplier network capable of supporting SAB’A’s quality, product, operational, and growth requirements.
## Supplier Principles
1. **Quality before Cost**
2. **Reliability before Convenience**
3. **Long-Term Value over Short-Term Savings**
4. **Transparency over Assumption**
5. **Performance over Promises**
6. **Resilience over Single Dependency**
7. **Relationship with Accountability**
## Supplier Categories
Suppliers may include:
- Fragrance Material Suppliers
- Raw Material Suppliers
- Packaging Suppliers
- Manufacturing Partners
- Printing Suppliers
- Logistics Providers
- Warehousing Providers
- Technology Providers
- Professional Service Providers
- Other Operational Partners
Supplier requirements should vary according to their role and operational importance.
## Supplier Classification
Suppliers may be classified according to:
- Strategic importance
- Operational dependency
- Spend
- Product impact
- Quality risk
- Supply risk
- Replacement difficulty
Critical suppliers should receive a higher level of monitoring and contingency planning.
## Supplier Selection
Supplier selection should consider relevant criteria such as:
- Quality
- Reliability
- Capacity
- Technical capability
- Lead time
- Communication
- Compliance
- Pricing
- Payment terms
- Minimum order quantities
- Scalability
- Geographic suitability
The final decision should consider overall value rather than price alone.
## Supplier Qualification
Critical or new suppliers may require qualification before approval.
Qualification may include:
- Company information
- Product or service specifications
- Samples
- Technical documentation
- Certifications where applicable
- Quality systems
- Production capabilities
- References
- Commercial terms
- Relevant compliance documentation
Qualification requirements should be proportionate to the supplier's risk and importance.
## Supplier Approval
Approved suppliers should be documented where operationally significant.
Supplier records may include:
- Supplier name
- Contact information
- Category
- Products or services
- Approved specifications
- Commercial terms
- Lead times
- Minimum order quantities
- Payment terms
- Performance records
- Approval status
Changes to critical supplier status should be documented.
## Supplier Performance
Supplier performance should be reviewed periodically where appropriate.
Evaluation may include:
- Product quality
- On-time delivery
- Order accuracy
- Lead-time reliability
- Communication
- Responsiveness
- Documentation
- Pricing consistency
- Issue resolution
- Capacity
Performance should be assessed using actual evidence wherever practical.
## Supplier Communication
Communication with suppliers should be:
- Clear
- Professional
- Documented where necessary
- Timely
- Specific
Important requirements, changes, approvals, and exceptions should not depend solely on informal communication.
## Quality Management
Suppliers providing materials or components that affect product quality should operate according to the approved requirements.
Quality issues should be:
1. Identified
2. Documented
3. Communicated
4. Investigated
5. Resolved
6. Reviewed for recurrence
Repeated quality failures should trigger supplier reassessment.
## Supplier Changes
Changes affecting an approved supply may require review before implementation.
Examples include:
- Material changes
- Manufacturing location changes
- Process changes
- Specification changes
- Packaging changes
- Subcontractor changes
- Significant formulation or composition changes
Changes should not be accepted solely because the supplier considers them equivalent.
## Supplier Development
Where strategically valuable, SAB’A may work with suppliers to improve:
- Quality
- Consistency
- Lead times
- Communication
- Packaging
- Production capability
- Cost efficiency
- Scalability
Supplier development should create measurable operational value.
## Risk Management
Supplier risks may include:
- Single-source dependency
- Capacity limitations
- Financial instability
- Quality inconsistency
- Long lead times
- Material shortages
- Geographic disruption
- Logistics disruption
- Regulatory issues
Critical risks should be identified and monitored.
## Alternative Suppliers
Where practical, SAB’A should identify alternative sources for critical materials or services.
Alternative suppliers should not be treated as fully interchangeable until their:
- Quality
- Specifications
- Capacity
- Commercial terms
- Lead times
- Compliance
have been appropriately evaluated.
## Supplier Performance Issues
When performance falls below requirements, SAB’A may apply:
- Corrective action
- Increased monitoring
- Supplier review
- Temporary restriction
- Requalification
- Alternative sourcing
- Termination
The response should reflect the severity and recurrence of the issue.
## Supplier Records
Relevant supplier documentation may include:
- Qualification records
- Supplier evaluations
- Quotations
- Agreements
- Specifications
- Certificates
- Purchase records
- Quality records
- Performance reviews
- Corrective actions
- Communication records
Records should remain organized and traceable.
## Supplier Relationship Management
SAB’A should build professional relationships with suppliers while maintaining appropriate commercial and operational discipline.
Strong relationships should be based on:
- Clear expectations
- Mutual respect
- Consistent communication
- Reliable commitments
- Fair commercial practices
- Accountability
Relationship quality should never replace performance requirements.
## Core Principle
> The strength of a supplier relationship is measured not by familiarity, but by the reliability it creates for SAB’A.
