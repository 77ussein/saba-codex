# Operations Standards
## Purpose
Operations Standards define the minimum requirements for operating SAB’A consistently, reliably, and with appropriate control.
They establish a common operational standard across procurement, suppliers, production, inventory, fulfillment, quality, documentation, and supporting activities.
## Core Standards
All SAB’A operations should prioritize:
1. **Quality**
2. **Accuracy**
3. **Reliability**
4. **Consistency**
5. **Traceability**
6. **Documentation**
7. **Efficiency**
8. **Customer Experience**
9. **Compliance**
10. **Continuous Improvement**
## Quality Standard
Operational activities must support the approved quality requirements of SAB’A.
Quality should be protected across:
- Materials
- Suppliers
- Production
- Packaging
- Storage
- Inventory
- Fulfillment
- Delivery
Operational efficiency must never justify knowingly reducing an established quality requirement.
## Accuracy Standard
Operational records and execution should accurately reflect reality.
This includes:
- Inventory quantities
- Product information
- Supplier information
- Purchase records
- Production records
- Batch information
- Order information
- Shipping information
Known discrepancies should be investigated and corrected appropriately.
## Consistency Standard
Processes that are repeated should be sufficiently standardized to produce consistent outcomes.
Where a process requires flexibility, the permitted variation should be clear.
Consistency should not prevent improvement, but improvements should be documented and approved where necessary.
## Documentation Standard
Critical operational activities should be documented.
Documentation should be:
- Clear
- Current
- Accessible
- Traceable
- Proportionate to the activity
Operational processes should not depend solely on individual memory or informal knowledge.
## Traceability Standard
Where appropriate, SAB’A should maintain traceability across:
- Materials
- Suppliers
- Batches
- Production
- Inventory
- Orders
- Fulfillment
The level of traceability should reflect product, operational, financial, and regulatory requirements.
## Supplier Standard
Suppliers should be evaluated according to their importance and risk.
Critical suppliers should have appropriate:
- Qualification
- Performance monitoring
- Documentation
- Communication
- Risk assessment
- Contingency planning
Supplier relationships should not remove the need for accountability.
## Production Standard
Production should only use approved:
- Materials
- Specifications
- Processes
- Equipment
- Packaging components
Production records should provide sufficient evidence that requirements were followed.
## Inventory Standard
Inventory should remain sufficiently accurate to support operational and commercial decisions.
Inventory should distinguish between relevant statuses such as:
- Available
- Reserved
- Quarantined
- Damaged
- Returned
- Expired
Restricted inventory must not be treated as available inventory.
## Fulfillment Standard
Orders should be fulfilled accurately and according to communicated expectations.
Fulfillment should verify:
- Product
- Quantity
- Customer information
- Packaging
- Shipping information
- Order-specific requirements
The fulfillment process should protect both the product and the intended customer experience.
## Storage Standard
Materials and products should be stored according to their relevant requirements.
Storage should protect against:
- Damage
- Contamination
- Environmental deterioration
- Unauthorized access
- Misidentification
- Unintended use
Storage conditions should be monitored where required.
## Exception Management
Operational exceptions should not be normalized.
Exceptions should be:
1. Identified
2. Documented
3. Assessed
4. Resolved
5. Reviewed when necessary
Repeated exceptions should be treated as signals of a process or system issue.
## Risk Management
Operational risks should be identified according to their potential impact.
Relevant risks may include:
- Supplier disruption
- Material shortages
- Production failure
- Quality issues
- Inventory inaccuracies
- Fulfillment failures
- Logistics disruption
- Capacity limitations
Critical risks should have appropriate controls or contingency plans.
## Change Management
Changes to important operational processes should be evaluated before implementation.
Changes may include:
- Supplier changes
- Material substitutions
- Production processes
- Packaging
- Systems
- Fulfillment methods
- Storage arrangements
Changes should be documented where they materially affect operations or approved standards.
## Performance Standard
Operational performance should be monitored using meaningful measures.
Possible indicators include:
- Supplier performance
- Lead times
- Production efficiency
- Quality issues
- Inventory accuracy
- Stock availability
- Fulfillment accuracy
- Delivery performance
- Operational cost
- Waste
Performance data should support decisions and improvement.
## Compliance
Operations must comply with applicable:
- Laws
- Regulations
- Safety requirements
- Product requirements
- Contractual obligations
- Internal standards
Compliance requirements should be identified according to the activity and operating market.
## Scalability Standard
Operational systems should be capable of supporting growth without unnecessary complexity.
As SAB’A grows, appropriate increases in:
- Documentation
- Automation
- Controls
- Capacity
- Supplier management
- Inventory planning
- Performance monitoring
should be introduced where needed.
## Continuous Improvement
Operational standards should evolve as SAB’A gains:
- Experience
- Data
- Customer insight
- Supplier knowledge
- Production knowledge
- Operational maturity
Improvements should preserve established quality and brand requirements.
## Core Principle
> SAB’A operations should make quality reliable, execution repeatable, and growth controlled.
