# Packaging Components

SAB’A packaging is composed of a defined set of physical and supporting elements that work together as one coherent system.

Each component must have a clear purpose, maintain the required level of quality, and contribute to the overall product and brand experience.

## Core Components

- **Primary Container** — The component that directly holds the product and provides its essential protection and presentation.
- **Closure System** — Caps, lids, pumps, seals, or other mechanisms that secure the product and support safe and intuitive use.
- **Secondary Box** — The main outer presentation structure that protects the primary container and establishes the first visual impression of the packaged product.
- **Internal Structure** — Trays, inserts, supports, or protective forms that secure the product within the secondary packaging.
- **Labels and Identification** — Labels and printed identification elements required for product information, recognition, and compliance.
- **Protective Elements** — Materials or structures used to prevent damage during handling, storage, and transportation.
- **Brand Elements** — Logos, typography, finishes, patterns, symbols, and other approved identity elements applied to packaging.
- **Information Elements** — Cards, product information, instructions, care information, or other relevant printed materials.
- **Seals and Closures** — Elements that provide security, authenticity, protection, or a sense of controlled opening.
- **Shipping Components** — Outer cartons, protective materials, and logistical elements required for safe delivery.

## Component Hierarchy

Packaging components should follow a clear hierarchy based on their role within the system.

Primary components establish the physical product experience. Secondary components frame and protect the product, while supporting components provide information, security, functionality, and additional experience.

No component should be added solely for decoration when it does not provide a meaningful functional, informational, or experiential purpose.

## Component Standards

Every packaging component should meet the following requirements:

- **Purpose** — Each component must have a clearly defined role.
- **Quality** — Materials and construction must reflect SAB’A standards.
- **Compatibility** — Components must work together structurally and visually.
- **Consistency** — Components should maintain a recognizable SAB’A language across the product portfolio.
- **Efficiency** — Components should consider material use, production, storage, and transportation.
- **Scalability** — The component system should support future products and packaging formats.
- **Experience** — Every customer-facing component should contribute positively to the overall interaction.

## Component Integration

Packaging components must be considered as parts of one system rather than isolated objects.

Their proportions, materials, finishes, typography, graphics, closures, and structural relationships should work together to create a consistent and refined experience.

Changes to one major component should be evaluated against the wider packaging system to ensure that function, aesthetics, production, and brand consistency remain aligned.

## Standard

SAB’A packaging components must be **purposeful, refined, compatible, and consistent**.

Every component should earn its place within the system by contributing to protection, functionality, information, identity, or experience.
