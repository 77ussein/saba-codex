# Packaging Development

Packaging development at SAB’A is a structured process that transforms a packaging concept into a refined, functional, manufacturable, and emotionally meaningful experience.

Development must balance brand identity, product requirements, customer experience, materials, production, and commercial realities without compromising the standards established by the SAB’A Codex.

## Development Principles

- **Purpose Before Decoration** — Every design decision must serve a clear functional, experiential, or brand purpose.
- **Experience Before Complexity** — The packaging should create value through the quality of the experience rather than unnecessary components or layers.
- **Design with Intent** — Form, proportion, materials, finishes, and interactions must be considered as one system.
- **Prototype Before Commitment** — Important packaging decisions should be tested through physical or functional prototypes before final approval.
- **Quality at Every Stage** — Quality must be evaluated throughout development rather than only at final production.
- **Scalability** — Packaging concepts should be capable of being adapted to future products, collections, and formats where appropriate.
- **Production Awareness** — Design decisions must consider real manufacturing capabilities, tolerances, supply, assembly, storage, and transportation.

## Development Stages

### 1. Concept

The development process begins by defining the purpose and intended experience of the packaging.

The concept should establish:

- Product and collection context
- Intended customer experience
- Brand and heritage relationship
- Functional requirements
- Initial structural direction
- Material direction
- Desired emotional response

The concept should answer a simple question:

**Why should this packaging exist in this form?**

### 2. Design Development

The selected concept is developed into a more defined packaging system.

This stage considers:

- Form and proportion
- Structural design
- Component relationships
- Materials
- Finishes
- Typography and visual identity
- Opening and closing experience
- Product presentation
- Protection requirements

All decisions should remain aligned with the approved SAB’A packaging standards.

### 3. Prototyping

Physical or functional prototypes should be produced when necessary to evaluate the proposed solution.

Prototypes should be used to assess:

- Scale and proportions
- Structural integrity
- Material behavior
- Product fit
- Opening sequence
- Tactile experience
- Visual appearance
- Assembly requirements

A packaging concept should not be considered final based on digital appearance alone when physical interaction is an important part of the experience.

### 4. Evaluation

The prototype should be evaluated against the complete packaging system.

Evaluation should consider:

- Brand alignment
- Product compatibility
- Customer experience
- Material quality
- Functionality
- Durability
- Manufacturing feasibility
- Cost efficiency
- Storage and transportation
- Emotional value

Any significant weakness should be addressed before approval.

### 5. Refinement

Feedback from testing and evaluation should be used to refine the packaging.

Refinement may involve changes to:

- Structure
- Materials
- Dimensions
- Finishes
- Components
- Opening mechanisms
- Protection
- Production methods

Changes should improve the overall system rather than introduce unnecessary complexity.

### 6. Approval

A packaging concept may proceed to production only after the required design, functional, material, and production requirements have been satisfied.

Approval should confirm that the packaging:

- Meets SAB’A standards
- Protects the product appropriately
- Delivers the intended experience
- Can be manufactured consistently
- Uses approved materials and finishes
- Maintains brand and product alignment

## Emotional Experience

Packaging development must consider the emotional journey of the customer.

The objective is not simply to create an attractive package, but to design a sequence of interactions that creates anticipation, discovery, appreciation, and connection.

The customer should feel that every interaction was intentional.

The packaging should create enough value and character that the customer may choose to keep it rather than immediately discard it.

## Development Documentation

Significant packaging projects should maintain appropriate documentation throughout development.

Documentation may include:

- Concept references
- Design iterations
- Material samples
- Prototype records
- Testing results
- Supplier information
- Technical specifications
- Approved artwork
- Production requirements
- Final approvals

Documentation should support consistency and provide a reliable reference for future production and development.

## Change Management

Any significant change to an approved packaging system should be evaluated before implementation.

Changes to materials, structure, components, finishes, suppliers, or production methods must be reviewed for their potential impact on:

- Quality
- Function
- Appearance
- Customer experience
- Brand consistency
- Product protection
- Production performance

A change should improve or maintain the approved standard rather than reduce it without clear justification.

## Development Standard

SAB’A packaging development must follow a process of **concept, design, prototype, evaluation, refinement, and approval**.

The final result should not simply be manufacturable.

It should be **purposeful, refined, functional, emotionally meaningful, and worthy of the SAB’A name**.
