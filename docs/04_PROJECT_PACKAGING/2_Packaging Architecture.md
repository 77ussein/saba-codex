# Packaging Architecture

The SAB’A packaging system is designed as a unified architecture in which every packaging element has a defined role, function, and relationship to the product and the wider brand experience.

The system should provide consistency across products while allowing flexibility for different product formats, collections, sizes, and future extensions.

## Packaging Layers

SAB’A packaging is organized into distinct layers:

- **Primary Packaging** — The packaging that directly contains and protects the product. It is the most immediate physical expression of the product and must meet both functional and aesthetic requirements.
- **Secondary Packaging** — The outer packaging that presents, protects, and frames the primary product. It carries a significant part of the visual and experiential identity.
- **Tertiary Packaging** — Packaging used for shipping, handling, storage, and logistical protection. It should prioritize durability, efficiency, and product safety while remaining aligned with SAB’A standards.
- **Supporting Elements** — Inserts, cards, labels, protective components, seals, and other elements that support the product experience and provide necessary information or functionality.

## System Relationships

Each packaging layer should operate as part of one coherent system.

Primary packaging establishes the product identity, secondary packaging reinforces the brand experience, and tertiary packaging ensures safe and efficient movement of the product.

Supporting elements should connect these layers without introducing unnecessary visual or functional complexity.

## Architectural Principles

- **Hierarchy** — Each packaging layer must have a clear purpose and visual priority.
- **Consistency** — Core packaging characteristics should remain recognizable across the SAB’A portfolio.
- **Flexibility** — The architecture should accommodate different products, formats, sizes, and collections without requiring a completely new system.
- **Scalability** — The system should support future product expansion while maintaining structural and visual coherence.
- **Efficiency** — Packaging decisions should consider material use, production requirements, storage, transportation, and operational practicality.
- **Integration** — Packaging should work seamlessly with product design, brand identity, customer experience, and operational requirements.

## Product and Brand Integration

Packaging architecture must connect the physical product to the broader SAB’A ecosystem.

The system should align with the principles established in the Brand and Product sections of the SAB’A Codex while supporting the customer experience from initial presentation through delivery and final use.

## Standard

SAB’A packaging architecture must remain **coherent, scalable, functional, and unmistakably connected to the brand**.

Every packaging layer should serve a clear purpose and contribute to one unified experience.
