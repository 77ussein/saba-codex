# Packaging Production

Packaging production is the stage in which the approved SAB’A packaging system is translated into physical products.

Production must preserve the approved design, materials, specifications, quality, functionality, and customer experience established throughout the packaging development process.

The objective is not simply to manufacture packaging.

The objective is to manufacture the **approved SAB’A experience consistently and reliably**.

## Production Principles

- **Specification First** — Production must follow the approved packaging specifications.
- **Consistency** — Every production unit should maintain the same approved standard.
- **Quality Before Volume** — Production volume must never justify a reduction in approved quality.
- **Controlled Production** — Significant production stages should be monitored and documented.
- **Approved Materials** — Only approved materials, components, finishes, and suppliers should be used unless an authorized change has been made.
- **Traceability** — Production batches and significant changes should be traceable.
- **Continuous Improvement** — Production issues should be documented and used to improve future packaging development.

## Production Readiness

Before production begins, the packaging system should be confirmed as production-ready.

Production readiness should verify:

- Approved design
- Approved materials
- Approved specifications
- Approved artwork
- Approved finishes
- Approved structural configuration
- Approved supplier or manufacturer
- Required production tooling
- Assembly requirements
- Quality requirements
- Reference samples
- Inspection criteria

No packaging should enter full production while critical requirements remain unresolved.

## Supplier and Manufacturer Approval

Packaging suppliers and manufacturers must be capable of meeting the approved SAB’A requirements.

Evaluation should consider:

- Manufacturing capability
- Material quality
- Production consistency
- Technical expertise
- Quality control systems
- Capacity
- Lead times
- Communication
- Reliability
- Ability to maintain approved specifications

Supplier selection should consider long-term consistency rather than cost alone.

## Pre-Production Sample

A pre-production sample should be reviewed before significant production begins when appropriate.

The sample should be evaluated against the approved specification and reference sample.

Review may include:

- Dimensions
- Materials
- Structure
- Color
- Printing
- Finishes
- Assembly
- Product fit
- Opening and closing
- Overall appearance
- Customer interaction

Production should proceed only after the required approval has been obtained.

## Production Control

During production, critical characteristics should be monitored to ensure consistency.

Quality control may include inspection of:

- Material quality
- Dimensions
- Structural integrity
- Color consistency
- Printing accuracy
- Surface finishes
- Component alignment
- Assembly
- Closure performance
- Product protection
- Overall appearance

Inspection frequency should be appropriate to the production scale and risk of the packaging system.

## Quality Control

Quality control should identify defects before packaging reaches the customer.

Defects may include:

- Structural damage
- Incorrect dimensions
- Material defects
- Printing errors
- Surface imperfections
- Incorrect finishes
- Misalignment
- Assembly errors
- Inconsistent components
- Product fit issues

Critical defects should prevent affected units from being approved for distribution.

## Batch Consistency

Production batches should maintain consistency with the approved reference.

Variations in materials, finishes, color, dimensions, or assembly should remain within the approved requirements.

Where variation exceeds the acceptable specification, the affected production should be reviewed before release.

## Non-Conformance

When production does not meet an approved requirement, the issue should be documented and evaluated.

Possible actions may include:

- Correction
- Rework
- Rejection
- Replacement
- Supplier corrective action
- Specification review
- Production adjustment

Non-conforming packaging should not be accepted simply because the deviation appears visually minor if it affects quality, function, protection, or customer experience.

## Final Inspection

Before release, finished packaging should undergo final inspection according to the approved quality requirements.

Final inspection should confirm:

- Correct components
- Correct materials
- Correct artwork
- Correct finishes
- Structural integrity
- Product fit
- Assembly quality
- Overall presentation
- Compliance with approved specifications

Only packaging that meets the required standard should be released for use.

## Packaging Assembly

Where packaging assembly is performed separately from manufacturing, assembly procedures should be documented and controlled.

Assembly must preserve:

- Component alignment
- Product positioning
- Protection
- Opening sequence
- Visual presentation
- Customer experience

The final assembled package should be evaluated as a complete experience rather than as individual components.

## Storage and Transportation

Finished packaging must be stored and transported in conditions appropriate to its materials and construction.

Storage and transportation requirements should consider:

- Temperature
- Humidity
- Pressure
- Stacking
- Handling
- Protection from contamination
- Protection from damage
- Protection of finishes and surfaces

Packaging should arrive at the point of use in the same approved condition in which it left production.

## Production Records

Production records should be maintained where appropriate.

Records may include:

- Production batch
- Supplier
- Production date
- Material references
- Approved specifications
- Inspection results
- Quality issues
- Corrective actions
- Approval records
- Reference samples
- Significant changes

Records provide traceability and support future production and improvement.

## Change Control

No significant production change should be introduced without appropriate review.

Changes may include:

- Supplier
- Material
- Manufacturing process
- Tooling
- Finish
- Component
- Assembly method
- Production location

Any approved change must be evaluated to ensure that it does not compromise the intended SAB’A standard.

## Continuous Improvement

Production is not considered a closed process.

Feedback from manufacturing, quality control, logistics, customer experience, and product teams should be used to identify opportunities for improvement.

Improvements should preserve the approved identity and experience while increasing:

- Quality
- Reliability
- Efficiency
- Durability
- Consistency
- Sustainability

## Production Standard

SAB’A packaging production must deliver the approved packaging system **accurately, consistently, and without compromising its intended quality or experience**.

The final production result must remain faithful to the approved relationship between:

**Brand — Product — Material — Structure — Function — Experience**

The production process succeeds when the customer receives the same quality and intention that were established at the beginning of development.

**What is designed must be what is produced.**

**What is produced must be worthy of SAB’A.**
