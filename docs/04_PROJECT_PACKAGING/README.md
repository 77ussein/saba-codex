# 04_PACKAGING

The SAB’A Packaging system defines how the brand’s identity, product, materials, functionality, and customer experience come together through packaging.

Packaging at SAB’A is not treated as disposable decoration or as a collection of separate components.

It is part of the product experience.

Every packaging decision should contribute to the perception, protection, presentation, and emotional experience of the product while remaining faithful to the identity and heritage of SAB’A.

## Purpose

The purpose of this section is to establish a complete framework for the development, specification, and production of SAB’A packaging.

It provides the principles and standards required to ensure that packaging remains:

- Intentional
- Refined
- Functional
- Consistent
- Emotionally meaningful
- Appropriate to the product
- Faithful to SAB’A identity
- Suitable for real-world production

## Packaging Philosophy

SAB’A does not measure luxury by the amount of packaging used.

Luxury is expressed through the quality of the materials, the precision of the construction, the restraint of the design, and the emotional value of the experience.

The objective is not to create more packaging.

The objective is to create **better packaging**.

Packaging should create a sense of anticipation, discovery, and connection.

It should feel valuable enough to be appreciated, reused, displayed, or kept rather than immediately discarded.

## Packaging System

The SAB’A packaging framework is organized into eight connected documents:

### 1. Packaging Philosophy

Defines the fundamental beliefs and principles behind SAB’A packaging.

### 2. Packaging Architecture

Defines the structure and hierarchy of the packaging system and how its elements work together.

### 3. Packaging Components

Defines the individual physical elements that form the complete packaging experience.

### 4. Packaging Materials

Defines the principles and considerations used when selecting materials for packaging.

### 5. Packaging Standards

Defines the minimum standards that every SAB’A packaging system must meet.

### 6. Packaging Development

Defines the process used to develop packaging from concept through testing, refinement, and approval.

### 7. Packaging Specifications

Defines the technical and physical requirements required to reproduce approved packaging consistently.

### 8. Packaging Production

Defines how approved packaging is manufactured, inspected, controlled, and released for use.

## Development Logic

The packaging system follows a continuous progression:

**Philosophy → Architecture → Components → Materials → Standards → Development → Specifications → Production**

Each document builds upon the previous one.

The result is a controlled system in which the original intention is preserved from the first concept through to the final customer experience.

## Customer Experience

Packaging is one of the first physical interactions between the customer and the product.

Therefore, every interaction should be intentional.

From the moment the package is received, through opening, discovery, handling, and continued use, the experience should communicate the character and quality of SAB’A.

The customer should feel that the packaging is part of what they received, not merely something that surrounded the product.

## Heritage and Identity

SAB’A packaging must remain connected to the identity and heritage of the brand.

Cultural influence should be expressed with intention, refinement, and authenticity rather than superficial decoration.

As SAB’A evolves across products, collections, digital experiences, and future developments, its packaging should evolve with it while maintaining the principles that define the brand.

## Standard

SAB’A packaging must protect the product, express its identity, support its function, and create meaningful value for the customer.

It should be designed to be **experienced, not simply opened and discarded**.
