# Packaging Materials

Materials are a fundamental part of the SAB’A packaging experience. Their quality, appearance, texture, weight, durability, and performance must support both the product and the wider brand identity.

Material selection should balance luxury, functionality, production requirements, and long-term consistency.

## Material Principles

- **Quality** — Materials must meet the level of quality expected from a premium product.
- **Material Integrity** — Each material should be selected for its inherent qualities rather than appearance alone.
- **Tactile Experience** — Texture, weight, finish, and physical interaction should contribute to the perceived value of the product.
- **Durability** — Materials must provide appropriate protection throughout handling, storage, transportation, and use.
- **Brand Alignment** — Materials should reinforce SAB’A’s visual and sensory identity.
- **Consistency** — Approved materials should remain consistent across products and production cycles where practical.
- **Efficiency** — Material choices should consider manufacturing, storage, transportation, and overall resource use.
- **Responsible Selection** — Materials should be evaluated with consideration for environmental impact, sourcing, recyclability, and responsible production.

## Primary Material Categories

### Glass

Glass may be used for primary product containers where its clarity, weight, durability, and premium tactile qualities support the product.

Selection should consider thickness, transparency, color, shape, compatibility, and manufacturing requirements.

### Paper and Board

Paper and board may be used for cartons, boxes, inserts, cards, labels, and other supporting elements.

They should provide the required structural strength while maintaining the intended visual and tactile quality.

### Metal

Metal may be used for closures, decorative components, structural elements, or other packaging applications where its durability, precision, weight, or finish contributes to the product experience.

### Wood

Wood may be considered for selected packaging applications where its natural character and tactile qualities support the intended product or collection.

Its use should remain purposeful and consistent with the wider SAB’A aesthetic.

### Textiles and Soft Materials

Textiles and soft materials may be used for protective pouches, wraps, inserts, or selected presentation elements where they provide meaningful functional or experiential value.

### Plastics and Synthetic Materials

Plastics and synthetic materials may be used where their performance, safety, compatibility, durability, or production requirements justify their use.

Selection should prioritize appropriate quality and responsible material choices.

## Material and Finish

Material selection should not be separated from finish selection.

Surface treatment, texture, coating, embossing, debossing, printing, metallic effects, and other finishes should enhance the inherent qualities of the material rather than conceal them.

Finishes should remain restrained and intentional.

## Material Compatibility

All materials must be evaluated for compatibility with the product, other packaging components, manufacturing processes, storage conditions, and transportation requirements.

Materials must not compromise product safety, stability, performance, or appearance.

## Material Evaluation

Before approval, materials should be evaluated according to:

- Quality and appearance
- Tactile experience
- Structural performance
- Product compatibility
- Manufacturing feasibility
- Durability
- Cost and efficiency
- Supply availability
- Environmental considerations
- Long-term consistency

## Material Standards

Approved packaging materials should be documented and maintained as part of the SAB’A packaging system.

Any significant material change should be evaluated against the original specification to ensure that the intended quality, function, appearance, and experience remain consistent.

## Standard

SAB’A packaging materials must be **intentional, refined, functional, durable, and aligned with the character of the brand**.

Material choice should always serve a clear purpose and contribute to the quality of the complete packaging experience.
