# Packaging Specifications

Packaging specifications define the approved technical and physical requirements for SAB’A packaging.

Specifications translate the approved packaging concept into clear requirements that can be manufactured, tested, documented, and reproduced consistently.

## Specification Principles

- **Clarity** — Specifications must be clear, measurable, and understandable to the parties responsible for production.
- **Consistency** — Approved specifications must preserve the intended packaging experience across production runs.
- **Purpose** — Every specification must support function, quality, protection, identity, or customer experience.
- **Precision** — Critical dimensions, materials, finishes, tolerances, and construction requirements must be documented accurately.
- **Adaptability** — Specifications should allow controlled development without compromising the core requirements of the packaging system.
- **Traceability** — Approved specifications and subsequent changes must be documented and traceable.

## Core Specification Areas

Each packaging specification should define the requirements relevant to the packaging system.

These may include:

- Dimensions and proportions
- Materials
- Material thickness
- Structural construction
- Component configuration
- Closures and opening mechanisms
- Internal protection
- Surface finishes
- Printing and artwork requirements
- Color requirements
- Typography and placement
- Branding elements
- Labels and information
- Assembly requirements
- Product fit
- Storage requirements
- Transportation requirements

## Dimensions and Tolerances

Critical dimensions must be documented together with acceptable manufacturing tolerances.

Tolerances should be established according to the function and importance of each component.

Where a dimension directly affects product fit, structural integrity, visual alignment, or customer interaction, the required tolerance should be clearly defined.

## Material Specifications

Approved materials must be identified by their relevant specifications rather than by general descriptions alone.

Where applicable, documentation should include:

- Material category
- Material grade
- Thickness
- Weight or density
- Surface characteristics
- Finish
- Color
- Supplier or approved source
- Performance requirements

Material substitutions should not be made without appropriate evaluation and approval.

## Structural Specifications

Structural packaging must be documented according to its intended function.

Specifications may define:

- Shape
- Dimensions
- Folding or forming requirements
- Assembly method
- Closure method
- Internal support
- Product positioning
- Protective requirements
- Opening sequence

The structure must maintain the intended experience while providing appropriate protection and stability.

## Finish Specifications

Finishes must be documented clearly enough to reproduce the approved result.

Where applicable, specifications should identify:

- Surface finish
- Coating
- Texture
- Embossing
- Debossing
- Foiling
- Printing method
- Gloss or matte requirements
- Other approved surface treatments

Finishes should remain consistent with the intended character of SAB’A and should not be introduced without a clear purpose.

## Brand Specifications

Packaging must follow the approved SAB’A brand system.

Specifications should define the correct application of:

- Logo
- Typography
- Colors
- Graphic elements
- Product naming
- Information hierarchy
- Placement and proportions

Brand elements must remain consistent even when packaging formats evolve.

## Product Fit and Protection

The packaging must securely accommodate the intended product.

Specifications should establish the required relationship between the product and its packaging, including:

- Product positioning
- Movement limitations
- Protection from impact
- Stability
- Removal and placement
- Compatibility with closures and internal structures

The product should feel intentionally presented rather than simply contained.

## Customer Interaction

Where customer interaction is a defining part of the packaging, specifications should document the intended experience.

This may include:

- Opening force
- Opening sequence
- Closure behavior
- Product reveal
- Tactile interaction
- Reusability
- Storage or continued use

Technical specifications should protect the emotional and experiential qualities established during development.

## Quality Requirements

Every approved packaging specification should include appropriate quality requirements.

Evaluation may cover:

- Dimensional accuracy
- Material consistency
- Surface quality
- Print quality
- Structural integrity
- Component alignment
- Closure performance
- Product protection
- Overall appearance

Quality requirements should be established before production rather than defined after defects occur.

## Production and Supplier Requirements

Specifications should provide sufficient information for approved suppliers and manufacturers to reproduce the packaging accurately.

Production documentation may include:

- Technical drawings
- Material references
- Artwork files
- Assembly instructions
- Quality requirements
- Approved samples
- Inspection criteria

Supplier interpretation should not replace documented SAB’A requirements.

## Specification Control

Approved specifications must be treated as controlled documentation.

Any significant change to:

- Material
- Dimension
- Structure
- Finish
- Supplier
- Manufacturing method
- Component
- Brand application

should be reviewed and approved before implementation.

Changes must be documented to maintain traceability between the original specification and the current approved version.

## Reference Sample

Where appropriate, an approved physical sample should be maintained as a reference for production quality.

The reference sample should represent the approved combination of:

**Material — Structure — Finish — Branding — Assembly — Experience**

Physical reference samples should be protected and replaced when an officially approved revision supersedes them.

## Specification Standard

SAB’A packaging specifications must be **clear, precise, controlled, reproducible, and aligned with the approved customer experience**.

Specifications exist not to restrict creativity, but to protect the integrity of an approved idea as it moves from design into production.

The objective is simple:

**What SAB’A approves must be what SAB’A produces.**
