# Packaging Standards

SAB’A packaging must meet a consistent standard of quality, functionality, refinement, and emotional value across every product and customer interaction.

Packaging is not treated as disposable decoration. It is part of the product experience and must reflect the value, identity, and heritage of SAB’A.

## Core Standards

- **Quality** — Every packaging element must meet a level of quality appropriate to the product and the SAB’A brand.
- **Purpose** — Every component must serve a clear functional, informational, protective, or experiential purpose.
- **Refinement** — Packaging must communicate luxury through proportion, material quality, construction, and restraint rather than unnecessary decoration.
- **Consistency** — Packaging must remain recognizably SAB’A across products, collections, and future developments.
- **Functionality** — Packaging must protect the product, support practical use, and provide a clear and intuitive experience.
- **Durability** — Packaging must maintain its intended condition throughout handling, transportation, storage, and reasonable customer use.
- **Material Integrity** — Materials and finishes must reflect their intended quality and must not create a perception that is inconsistent with the product.
- **Cohesion** — All packaging elements must work together as one complete system rather than as disconnected layers.

## Emotional Value

SAB’A packaging should create an emotional response before the product is even experienced.

The objective is not simply to deliver a product safely, but to create a moment of anticipation, discovery, and connection.

Packaging should feel valuable enough to be appreciated, reused, displayed, or kept rather than immediately discarded.

The customer should feel that the packaging itself is part of what they have received.

## Packaging as Part of the Product

The packaging must be considered an extension of the product rather than a separate disposable layer.

The relationship between product, packaging, identity, materials, and presentation should create one complete experience.

A successful SAB’A package should communicate the character of the product before it is opened and reinforce that character after it has been revealed.

## Restraint

SAB’A does not equate luxury with excessive packaging.

Additional layers, materials, decorations, or components should only be introduced when they provide meaningful protection, functionality, information, identity, or emotional value.

Unnecessary packaging should be avoided.

The goal is not to maximize the number of components, but to maximize the value of the experience.

## Heritage and Identity

Packaging must reflect the heritage and identity of SAB’A in a refined and contemporary manner.

Cultural references should be intentional and integrated into the overall design rather than applied as superficial decoration.

The packaging should feel connected to its origin while remaining relevant to a contemporary international audience.

## Product Hierarchy

Packaging standards should remain appropriate to the product, collection, and intended positioning.

Differences between product levels may be expressed through materials, construction, finishes, scale, detailing, and presentation.

However, every level must remain recognizably SAB’A and must meet the minimum standards of quality and experience established by the brand.

## Customer Experience

Packaging must be evaluated from the customer's perspective.

The experience should consider:

- First visual impression
- Physical interaction
- Opening sequence
- Material and tactile experience
- Product presentation
- Information clarity
- Ease of use
- Protection
- Final impression

Every stage should feel deliberate and consistent.

## Production Standards

Packaging must be designed with real production requirements in mind.

Approved packaging should be evaluated for:

- Manufacturing feasibility
- Material availability
- Structural integrity
- Quality consistency
- Assembly requirements
- Storage requirements
- Transportation requirements
- Cost efficiency
- Long-term supply reliability

## Approval Standard

No packaging system should be approved solely because it looks visually appealing.

Approval requires alignment across:

**Brand — Product — Function — Material — Experience — Production**

A packaging concept should only move forward when these areas work together as one system.

## Standard

SAB’A packaging must be **intentional, refined, functional, emotionally meaningful, and worthy of being kept**.

The standard is not to create more packaging.

The standard is to create **better packaging**.
