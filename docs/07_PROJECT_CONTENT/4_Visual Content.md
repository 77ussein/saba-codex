# Visual Content

## Purpose

This document defines how SAB’A is visually expressed through digital and physical content.

Visual content should strengthen the identity of SAB’A and create a recognisable visual world across every channel.

---

## Visual Philosophy

SAB’A visual content should feel:

- Refined
- Distinct
- Intentional
- Atmospheric
- Sophisticated
- Human
- Timeless

Visuals should communicate quality without relying on excess.

---

## Visual Identity

Every visual should remain consistent with the established SAB’A:

- Color System
- Typography System
- Imagery Guidelines
- Logo Guidelines
- Brand Principles

Content should extend the visual identity, not create a separate one.

---

## Photography

Photography should prioritise:

- Composition
- Light
- Texture
- Material
- Detail
- Atmosphere
- Authenticity

Product photography should communicate the actual product accurately while maintaining the SAB’A visual character.

---

## Product Photography

Product imagery should clearly communicate:

- Product form
- Scale
- Materials
- Details
- Packaging
- Variants where applicable

Product images must not misrepresent the product through excessive editing or manipulation.

---

## Editorial Photography

Editorial imagery may explore:

- Atmosphere
- Places
- Objects
- Materials
- People
- Craft
- Lifestyle
- Cultural references

Editorial photography should expand the world of SAB’A rather than simply display products.

---

## Video

Video may be used for:

- Storytelling
- Product discovery
- Education
- Craft
- Behind-the-scenes
- Launches
- Social content
- Brand experiences

Movement should have purpose.

Video should not rely on excessive effects simply to create attention.

---

## Short-Form Content

Instagram Reels, TikTok, and similar formats may use faster and more dynamic storytelling.

However, platform behaviour must not override SAB’A's identity.

The content may adapt to the platform while maintaining:

**SAB’A Identity → Visual Quality → Platform Relevance**

---

## People

When people appear in SAB’A content, they should feel natural and authentic.

Representation should support:

- Human connection
- Diversity of experience
- Cultural relevance
- Real interaction

People should feel like part of the world, not simply models placed into it.

---

## Materials & Texture

Materials are an important visual language for SAB’A.

Content may highlight:

- Glass
- Paper
- Wood
- Metal
- Fabric
- Natural materials
- Fragrance materials
- Packaging details

Texture should create sensory depth and encourage closer attention.

---

## Composition

Composition should favour:

- Balance
- Space
- Hierarchy
- Detail
- Intentional framing

Visuals should avoid unnecessary visual noise.

Negative space may be used deliberately to create calm and emphasis.

---

## Colour

Visual content must follow the established SAB’A colour system.

Colour may be used to:

- Create atmosphere
- Establish hierarchy
- Support storytelling
- Differentiate collections
- Reinforce recognition

Colour should never become disconnected from the wider brand system.

---

## Typography in Visual Content

Typography should follow the established SAB’A typography system.

Text placed within images or videos should be:

- Clear
- Legible
- Minimal
- Consistent
- Purposeful

Typography should support the visual rather than compete with it.

---

## Motion

Motion should feel intentional and controlled.

Transitions, animation, and effects should support:

- Story
- Focus
- Rhythm
- Emotion
- Product understanding

Motion should never exist purely because it is technically possible.

---

## Social Visuals

Social content may become more immediate and expressive while maintaining SAB’A's visual language.

Each platform may adapt:

- Composition
- Duration
- Format
- Cropping
- Motion
- Pace

The underlying visual identity must remain recognisable.

---

## AI-Generated Visuals

AI may assist with:

- Concept development
- Mood exploration
- Visual ideation
- Early creative exploration

AI-generated visuals must not misrepresent real products, materials, people, or events.

Final public-facing visuals must meet SAB’A visual standards.

---

## Visual Consistency

A customer should recognise SAB’A across:

**Website → Ecommerce → Email → Instagram → TikTok → X → WhatsApp → Packaging**

The format may change.

The visual character should not.

---

## Visual Quality

Before publication, important visual content should be reviewed for:

- Brand alignment
- Composition
- Lighting
- Accuracy
- Quality
- Legibility
- Platform suitability
- Technical quality

---

## Core Principle

SAB’A visual content should create recognition before explanation.

It should make the audience pause, look closer, and discover something.
