# Pricing

## Purpose

The SAB’A pricing system defines how every creation is valued, presented, discounted, promoted, and purchased across the ecommerce ecosystem.

Pricing is not merely a commercial calculation.

It is part of the customer's perception of:

- Value
- Quality
- Positioning
- Trust
- Exclusivity
- Experience

The objective is to create a pricing system that is commercially sustainable while remaining worthy of SAB’A.

The governing principle is:

**Price communicates value. It must never undermine it.**

---

# Pricing Philosophy

SAB’A should not compete primarily through low prices or constant promotions.

The customer should understand that the value of a creation comes from:

**Creation + Craft + Materials + Story + Heritage + Experience + Service**

rather than from the size of a discount.

Pricing should therefore feel:

- Intentional
- Confident
- Consistent
- Transparent
- Justified
- Premium

---

# Price Architecture

Every sellable product must have a defined commercial price.

The system should support:

- Base price
- Variant price
- Promotional price
- Discount
- Coupon
- Loyalty reward
- Bundle pricing
- Gift-related pricing where applicable

The final customer-facing amount must always be clear.

---

# Base Price

The base price represents the standard selling price of a product before temporary promotions or eligible customer benefits.

It should remain stable enough to establish a reliable perception of value.

Frequent unexplained changes should be avoided.

---

# Variant Pricing

Different variants may have different prices when their:

- Size
- Volume
- Materials
- Format
- Edition
- Contents

justify the difference.

The customer must clearly understand when selecting a variant that the price changes.

---

# Price Presentation

Prices must be presented clearly.

Customers should be able to understand:

- Product price
- Quantity
- Subtotal
- Discount where applicable
- Shipping cost
- Taxes where applicable
- Final total

No important cost should appear unexpectedly at the final step.

---

# Currency

The ecommerce system should support the appropriate currency for each active market.

The displayed currency must remain consistent throughout the customer journey.

A customer should not discover a different currency only after reaching checkout.

Future market expansion may introduce:

- Additional currencies
- Market-specific pricing
- Local tax treatment
- Local promotional rules

---

# Market Pricing

If SAB’A expands into multiple markets, pricing may differ because of:

- Taxes
- Duties
- Shipping
- Local operating costs
- Currency
- Market positioning
- Regulatory requirements

Market-specific pricing must remain intentional and controlled.

---

# Pricing Consistency

The same product should not appear with contradictory pricing across SAB’A-controlled channels without a clear reason.

This includes:

- Website
- Customer account
- Email
- WhatsApp
- Campaign landing pages
- Social commerce where applicable
- Customer care

The ecommerce system should remain the authoritative source for current pricing.

---

# Promotions

Promotions should be used selectively.

Appropriate uses may include:

- Product launches
- Seasonal campaigns
- Special events
- Customer acquisition
- Loyalty rewards
- Limited campaigns
- Competitions

Promotions should have a defined beginning and end.

---

# Discount Philosophy

Discounts are a tool, not the identity of SAB’A.

The brand should avoid creating an expectation that products are regularly discounted.

Constant discounting can weaken:

- Perceived value
- Exclusivity
- Trust
- Price integrity
- Long-term positioning

The preferred hierarchy is:

**Value → Recognition → Access → Reward → Discount**

rather than:

**Discount → Purchase**

---

# Promotional Pricing

When a product is temporarily discounted, the customer should clearly understand:

- Original price
- Promotional price
- Amount or percentage saved where relevant
- Promotion conditions
- Expiration where applicable

Promotional language must remain honest.

---

# Coupons

Coupons are controlled promotional instruments.

They may be issued through:

- Campaigns
- Loyalty
- Customer service
- Competitions
- Special occasions
- Acquisition campaigns

Each coupon should have defined rules.

---

# Coupon Rules

The system should support conditions such as:

- Expiration date
- Minimum order value
- Eligible products
- Eligible collections
- Customer eligibility
- Usage limit
- One-time use
- Market restrictions
- Combination restrictions

The customer should receive clear feedback when a coupon is accepted or rejected.

---

# Coupon Errors

When a coupon cannot be applied, the customer should understand why.

Examples:

- Coupon expired
- Product not eligible
- Minimum spend not reached
- Coupon already used
- Customer not eligible

The explanation should be clear and non-technical.

---

# Loyalty Pricing

Loyalty should reward the relationship rather than train customers to wait for discounts.

Possible benefits include:

- Points
- Reward products
- Exclusive access
- Early releases
- Complimentary items
- Selected coupons
- Special experiences

Where discounts are used, they should remain controlled.

---

# Points

If SAB’A uses a points system, the customer should be able to understand:

- Points earned
- Points available
- Points redeemed
- Points required for rewards
- Expiration rules where applicable

The system should make points feel like recognition, not accounting.

---

# Reward Thresholds

Rewards may be triggered by:

- Purchase value
- Number of purchases
- Product milestones
- Loyalty level
- Campaign participation
- Other meaningful customer actions

Reward rules must be transparent.

---

# Loyalty Card

The loyalty system may eventually connect to digital wallet experiences such as:

- Apple Wallet
- Google Wallet
- Samsung Wallet

The underlying pricing and loyalty logic must remain controlled by SAB’A's ecommerce system.

The wallet is an access point, not the source of truth.

---

# Bundles

Bundles may be priced to create meaningful value.

Examples may include:

- Discovery sets
- Gift sets
- Seasonal sets
- Collection sets
- Multi-product offers

Bundle pricing should remain understandable.

The customer should be able to see what they receive and, where relevant, what value the bundle provides.

---

# "Buy More" Offers

Promotions such as:

**Buy five, receive the sixth complimentary**

may be used where commercially appropriate.

Such mechanics must:

- Have clear eligibility
- State the qualifying quantity
- Clearly identify the reward
- Avoid misleading conditions
- Work correctly at checkout

These offers should be used strategically rather than continuously.

---

# Large Discounts

Large discounts may occasionally be appropriate for:

- Special campaigns
- Clearance
- Customer recovery
- Competitions
- Exceptional promotional events

However, significant discounts should not become a recurring expectation.

A premium house should retain pricing confidence.

---

# Complimentary Products

Complimentary products may be used as an alternative to direct price reduction.

Examples include:

- Discovery samples
- Small gifts
- Complimentary products
- Exclusive packaging
- Limited accessories

This may preserve perceived value more effectively than repeated percentage discounts.

---

# Gift Cards

If gift cards are introduced, the pricing system should support:

- Fixed denominations
- Customer-selected values where appropriate
- Expiration rules where legally permitted
- Redemption rules
- Balance tracking
- Refund handling

Gift cards should feel like an extension of the SAB’A gifting experience.

---

# Shipping and Pricing

Shipping costs must be integrated clearly into the total price experience.

The customer should understand:

- Product subtotal
- Shipping
- Taxes where applicable
- Discounts
- Final total

Free shipping may be used as a benefit when commercially justified.

It should not be presented in a misleading way.

---

# Tax and Regulatory Pricing

Where applicable, prices must comply with the requirements of each market.

The system should correctly handle:

- Applicable taxes
- Tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive presentation
- Required disclosures
- Invoices
- Receipts

Legal compliance takes priority over visual minimalism.

---

# Price Changes

Price changes should be controlled.

Before changing a live product price, verify:

- Current campaigns
- Active coupons
- Loyalty rules
- Existing carts
- Customer communication
- Scheduled campaigns
- Market-specific pricing

Customers should not experience unexpected pricing changes during an active purchase journey.

---

# Price Integrity

Price manipulation is prohibited.

The system must not create false impressions through:

- Fake original prices
- Artificial countdowns
- False scarcity
- Misleading discount percentages
- Hidden conditions

Trust is more valuable than a short-term conversion increase.

---

# Pricing and Customer Care

Customer care must be able to understand the pricing context of an order.

Relevant information may include:

- Product price
- Applied discount
- Coupon
- Loyalty reward
- Shipping charge
- Final amount

This allows customer care to resolve pricing questions accurately.

---

# Pricing and AI

SAB’A AI may answer pricing questions only using verified ecommerce data.

It may communicate:

- Current price
- Eligible promotions
- Coupon conditions
- Loyalty benefits
- General pricing information

It must never invent a price or promise a discount without system confirmation.

---

# Pricing and Email

Marketing and transactional emails must use current pricing information.

Campaign emails should clearly communicate:

- Offer
- Eligibility
- Duration
- Conditions
- Final price where relevant

Transactional emails must reflect the actual price paid.

---

# Pricing and Social Media

Prices communicated through:

- Instagram
- TikTok
- X

must remain accurate at the time of publication.

Where prices may change, campaigns should avoid creating long-term confusion.

Social promotions should link to the appropriate ecommerce destination.

---

# Pricing Psychology

SAB’A may use pricing psychology carefully.

However, pricing should remain consistent with the character of the house.

Avoid:

- Excessive urgency
- Constant countdowns
- Aggressive red discount badges
- Repeated "SALE" messaging
- Manipulative scarcity

The customer should feel invited, not chased.

---

# Premium Pricing

A premium price should be supported by a premium experience.

That means the customer should receive consistency across:

- Product quality
- Photography
- Storytelling
- Website
- Checkout
- Packaging
- Delivery
- Customer care
- After-purchase experience

The price creates an expectation.

The entire house must fulfil it.

---

# Pricing Governance

Pricing changes should be controlled by authorised business roles.

A pricing change should have:

- Clear owner
- Defined reason
- Effective date
- Market scope
- Product scope
- Promotion interaction
- Review process

No uncontrolled pricing changes should reach production.

---

# Pricing Audit

Before launching or changing a price, verify:

1. **Is the base price correct?**
2. **Are all variants priced correctly?**
3. **Is the displayed currency correct?**
4. **Are taxes handled correctly?**
5. **Are promotions configured correctly?**
6. **Are coupons behaving correctly?**
7. **Are loyalty benefits correct?**
8. **Is the final checkout amount correct?**
9. **Are email and social communications accurate?**
10. **Can customer care explain the price clearly?**
11. **Does the offer protect SAB’A's premium positioning?**
12. **Is the promotion genuinely valuable rather than artificially urgent?**

---

# What Pricing Must Never Become

SAB’A pricing must never become:

- A permanent sale
- A race to the lowest price
- A confusing system
- A hidden-fee structure
- A manipulation engine
- A source of contradictory information
- A substitute for product value

The customer should trust the price.

---

# Core Standard

The ideal SAB’A price experience is:

**Clear enough to trust.  
Premium enough to respect.  
Flexible enough to support the business.  
Controlled enough to protect the brand.**

---

# Core Statement

Pricing is part of the SAB’A experience.

It communicates value before the product arrives.

Therefore:

**Price must be intentional.**

**Discounts must be meaningful.**

**Rewards must feel earned.**

**Promotions must remain controlled.**

**Conditions must remain transparent.**

And above all:

**SAB’A should never need to become cheaper to become more desirable.**
