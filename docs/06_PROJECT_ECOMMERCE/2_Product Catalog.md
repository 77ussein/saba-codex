# Product Catalog

## Purpose

The SAB’A product catalogue is the structured commercial representation of every creation offered by the house.

It is not simply a database of products.

It is the foundation that connects:

**Product Identity → Story → Presentation → Availability → Price → Purchase → Ownership**

Every product must be represented accurately, consistently, and at a level worthy of SAB’A.

---

# Catalogue Philosophy

The catalogue must make products easy to understand without reducing them to technical entries.

Every creation should retain:

- Identity
- Character
- Story
- Materiality
- Purpose
- Commercial clarity

The system must support both:

**Discovery**

and

**Direct Purchase**

without compromising either.

---

# Product Hierarchy

Products should exist within a clear hierarchy.

The catalogue may be organised through:

- Collections
- Categories
- Subcategories
- Individual Products
- Product Variants

The hierarchy should remain understandable to customers and scalable for the business.

It must not become unnecessarily deep.

---

# Product Identity

Every product must have a consistent identity.

Core information may include:

- Product name
- Product ID
- SKU
- Collection
- Category
- Short description
- Full description
- Product status
- Availability
- Price
- Variants

Product naming must remain consistent across:

- Website
- Customer account
- Checkout
- Email
- WhatsApp
- Customer care
- Social campaigns

---

# Product Naming

Product names should feel unmistakably SAB’A.

Names must be:

- Memorable
- Clear
- Consistent
- Distinct
- Appropriate to the house

Avoid unnecessary technical naming conventions in customer-facing interfaces.

Internal identifiers may remain technical.

Customer-facing names should remain human.

---

# SKU and Internal Identification

Every product and relevant variant must have a unique internal identifier.

The system should support:

- SKU
- Product ID
- Variant ID
- Inventory reference

These identifiers should remain stable and should not be casually changed after products enter the commerce system.

---

# Collections

Collections should provide meaningful context.

A collection may represent:

- A creative concept
- A seasonal release
- A product family
- A specific story
- A cultural or editorial theme

Collections should not exist merely to divide products.

Each collection should have a reason to exist.

---

# Categories

Categories should help customers discover products naturally.

Categories should remain:

- Clear
- Relevant
- Stable
- Easy to understand

The category structure should avoid excessive fragmentation.

If a customer needs to understand the internal business structure before finding a product, the catalogue is too complicated.

---

# Product Variants

Where a creation has multiple variants, the system should represent them under the appropriate parent product.

Variants may include:

- Size
- Volume
- Format
- Colour where relevant
- Material where relevant
- Edition
- Other meaningful differences

Variants must clearly communicate what changes between them.

---

# Variant Experience

Customers should immediately understand:

- Which variant is selected
- What each option means
- Whether it changes the price
- Whether it changes availability

The system must prevent accidental purchases caused by unclear variant selection.

---

# Product Status

Products should have controlled lifecycle states.

Possible states include:

- Draft
- Preview
- Coming Soon
- Available
- Low Stock
- Out of Stock
- Temporarily Unavailable
- Archived

The customer-facing experience must use clear language rather than internal system terminology.

---

# Inventory

Inventory information must remain accurate.

The system should support:

- Available quantity
- Inventory status
- Variant-level inventory
- Low-stock thresholds
- Out-of-stock behaviour

Inventory information should update consistently across the ecommerce ecosystem.

---

# Scarcity

Scarcity may be communicated when it is genuine.

Examples:

- Limited edition
- Low stock
- Limited release

Artificial scarcity must not be created.

SAB’A should create desire through the quality and meaning of the creation, not misleading pressure.

---

# Product Information

Each product should contain the information necessary for confident purchase.

Depending on the product, this may include:

- Description
- Story
- Materials
- Ingredients
- Dimensions
- Weight
- Size
- Volume
- Usage
- Care
- Origin
- Production information
- Shipping information
- Return eligibility

Only relevant information should be shown.

---

# Product Story

Products should have a meaningful narrative where appropriate.

The story may communicate:

- Inspiration
- Heritage
- Craft
- Material
- Place
- Ritual
- Meaning

Storytelling should enhance understanding.

It should never replace factual product information.

---

# Heritage Information

Heritage-related product information must be accurate and responsible.

Historical or cultural references should be:

- Researched
- Contextual
- Authentic
- Clearly differentiated from creative interpretation

The catalogue should protect the integrity of SAB’A's cultural foundation.

---

# Product Imagery

Every product should have imagery appropriate to its importance.

Images may include:

- Primary product image
- Secondary views
- Detail photography
- Scale references where useful
- Packaging
- Lifestyle imagery
- Editorial imagery

Images must represent the actual product accurately.

---

# Product Video

Video may be included where it adds meaningful value.

It may communicate:

- Movement
- Texture
- Craft
- Scale
- Ritual
- Atmosphere

Video must not be required to understand essential product information.

---

# Product Media Standards

Media should maintain consistency in:

- Aspect ratio
- Cropping
- Lighting
- Background treatment
- Resolution
- Quality

The product catalogue should prevent inconsistent media from appearing across product pages.

---

# Product Relationships

Products should be capable of being related to:

- Similar creations
- Complementary products
- Same collection
- Same category
- Frequently purchased products
- Editorial stories
- Campaigns

Relationships should support discovery rather than aggressive upselling.

---

# Bundles and Sets

The catalogue may support:

- Discovery sets
- Gift sets
- Product bundles
- Limited sets
- Seasonal sets

Each bundle should have its own commercial identity while maintaining links to the underlying products.

---

# Gift Products

Where applicable, the catalogue may support gifting.

Gift-related products or services may include:

- Gift sets
- Gift packaging
- Gift cards
- Gift messages

The system should clearly distinguish gift functionality from ordinary products.

---

# Product Availability by Market

If SAB’A expands into multiple markets, products may have different:

- Availability
- Pricing
- Currency
- Shipping eligibility
- Legal requirements

The catalogue must support market-level configuration without duplicating the entire product system unnecessarily.

---

# Product Data Integrity

Product information must have one authoritative source.

Changes should propagate consistently to:

- Website
- Product pages
- Checkout
- Customer account
- Email
- Customer care
- AI
- Relevant social commerce experiences

Conflicting product information must be treated as a system failure.

---

# AI and Product Data

SAB’A AI may use catalogue information to answer customer questions.

AI responses should rely on verified catalogue data.

The AI must never invent:

- Product specifications
- Ingredients
- Materials
- Availability
- Pricing
- Product history
- Benefits
- Shipping eligibility

If information is unavailable, the AI should say so and route the customer appropriately.

---

# Search and Discovery

Catalogue data should support effective search.

Search should be able to recognise relevant:

- Product names
- Categories
- Collections
- Attributes
- Keywords

Search results should remain accurate and useful.

---

# Filtering

Where product volume requires filtering, filters should be meaningful.

Possible filters may include:

- Category
- Collection
- Size
- Format
- Availability
- Price
- Relevant product attributes

Filtering should simplify discovery rather than overwhelm the customer.

---

# Product SEO

Products should have structured information supporting discoverability where appropriate.

This may include:

- Product title
- Description
- Metadata
- Structured product data
- Search-friendly URLs
- Accurate availability
- Accurate pricing

SEO must never distort the SAB’A voice or product storytelling.

---

# Product URLs

Product URLs should be:

- Stable
- Clear
- Human-readable
- Consistent

URLs should avoid unnecessary technical parameters where possible.

Product URL changes should be carefully managed to prevent broken customer journeys.

---

# Product Reviews

If product reviews are introduced, they must be authentic and responsibly managed.

The system should support:

- Verified purchases where possible
- Clear review context
- Appropriate moderation
- Honest representation

Reviews should build trust rather than become artificial marketing content.

---

# Product Compliance

Where applicable, product information must satisfy relevant legal and regulatory requirements.

Required information should never be removed simply to maintain visual minimalism.

Luxury presentation and compliance must coexist.

---

# Product Lifecycle

Products should move through a controlled lifecycle:

**Concept → Draft → Review → Preview → Launch → Active → Limited / Low Stock → Unavailable → Archived**

Archived products should remain accessible internally for historical, reporting, and customer-service purposes where appropriate.

---

# Launching a Product

Before a product becomes publicly available, verify:

- Product identity
- Naming
- SKU
- Description
- Story
- Images
- Video where applicable
- Price
- Variants
- Inventory
- Shipping eligibility
- Return eligibility
- Catalogue relationships
- SEO information
- Customer-care information

No product should launch with incomplete critical information.

---

# Product Updates

Changes to live products must be controlled.

Updates may include:

- Price
- Availability
- Media
- Description
- Variants
- Shipping information

Changes must be reflected consistently across all relevant systems.

---

# Archived Products

When a product is discontinued, the system should determine whether it should:

- Be removed from discovery
- Remain visible as an archive
- Redirect to a successor
- Become part of the house history

Discontinuation should not automatically destroy useful historical information.

---

# Catalogue Quality Audit

Before approving a product, ask:

1. **Is the product accurately represented?**
2. **Is the name correct?**
3. **Is the SKU correct?**
4. **Is the price correct?**
5. **Is availability correct?**
6. **Are variants clear?**
7. **Are images high quality?**
8. **Is the story meaningful and accurate?**
9. **Is all required information present?**
10. **Can customers understand the product without assistance?**
11. **Can AI answer questions using verified data?**
12. **Does the product belong naturally within its collection or category?**
13. **Does the product meet the SAB’A standard?**

---

# What the Catalogue Must Never Become

The catalogue must never become:

- A messy product database
- A collection of duplicated information
- A collection of inconsistent names
- A discount-driven product list
- A technically correct but emotionally empty system

The catalogue should preserve both:

**Commercial Precision**

and

**Brand Meaning**

---

# Core Standard

Every product is a representative of the house.

Therefore:

**Every product must be accurate.  
Every product must be understandable.  
Every product must be discoverable.  
Every product must be beautifully represented.  
Every product must belong to the same world.**

---

# Core Statement

The SAB’A product catalogue is where the creations of the house become structured for the digital world.

It connects:

**Creation → Identity → Story → Information → Availability → Commerce**

without reducing the product to a database entry.

The catalogue should make the business scalable while keeping every creation recognisably SAB’A.

**Structure creates clarity.**

**Clarity creates trust.**

**Trust allows desire to become purchase.**
