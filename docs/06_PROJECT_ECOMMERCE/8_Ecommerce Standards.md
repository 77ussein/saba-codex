# Ecommerce Standards

## Purpose

The SAB’A ecommerce standards define the minimum level of quality required across the entire digital commerce ecosystem.

These standards apply to:

- Product catalogue
- Pricing
- Checkout
- Payments
- Shipping
- Returns and exchanges
- Customer account
- Customer care
- Ecommerce communication
- Future commerce capabilities

The objective is simple:

**Every commercial interaction must feel unmistakably SAB’A.**

---

# The Ecommerce Standard

SAB’A ecommerce must balance two things at all times:

**Commercial Excellence**

and

**Brand Experience**

Neither may sacrifice the other.

The system must be commercially effective without becoming aggressive.

It must be technically robust without feeling technical.

It must be luxurious without becoming impractical.

---

# Core Principles

Every ecommerce experience must follow these principles:

1. **Clarity before complexity**
2. **Trust before conversion**
3. **Experience before transaction**
4. **Consistency across channels**
5. **Accuracy across systems**
6. **Technology in service of the customer**
7. **Luxury through restraint**
8. **Continuous improvement without losing identity**

---

# One Commerce Environment

The ecommerce ecosystem must operate as one connected environment.

The customer should experience continuity between:

**Website → Product → Account → Checkout → Payment → Shipping → Customer Care → Ownership**

These should never feel like unrelated systems.

---

# Source of Truth

Every critical commercial data point must have an authoritative source.

This includes:

- Product information
- Product availability
- Price
- Discount
- Coupon
- Order status
- Payment status
- Shipping status
- Return status
- Loyalty status

Different channels must not independently invent or maintain conflicting versions of the same information.

---

# Data Consistency

The same order should remain the same order everywhere.

Information shown through:

- Website
- Customer account
- Email
- WhatsApp
- AI
- Customer care

must remain consistent with the central ecommerce system.

Contradictory information is considered a system failure.

---

# Product Accuracy

Every product must be represented accurately.

This includes:

- Name
- Description
- Images
- Price
- Variants
- Availability
- Materials or ingredients where relevant
- Dimensions where relevant
- Shipping eligibility
- Return eligibility

Customers must receive enough accurate information to make a confident decision.

---

# Pricing Integrity

Pricing must be:

- Accurate
- Transparent
- Consistent
- Controlled

SAB’A must avoid:

- Fake discounts
- Artificial scarcity
- Hidden costs
- Misleading original prices
- Unclear promotional conditions

Price should communicate value rather than manipulate behaviour.

---

# Checkout Standard

Checkout should be:

**Fast. Clear. Secure. Minimal.**

Customers should not be forced through unnecessary steps.

The final amount must always be clear before the order is submitted.

---

# Payment Standard

Payment must be secure and reliable.

Supported methods may include:

- Cards
- Apple Pay
- Samsung Pay
- Tabby
- Tamara
- Approved local payment methods

Payment methods must remain connected to the central order system.

A payment should never be considered successful without verified confirmation.

---

# Shipping Standard

Shipping must be treated as part of the customer experience.

The customer should understand:

- Shipping cost
- Delivery estimate
- Shipping method
- Tracking
- Delivery status

Packaging and physical delivery must represent the same standard established digitally.

---

# Returns Standard

Returns and exchanges must be:

- Clear
- Fair
- Accessible
- Traceable
- Consistent

A customer should never feel punished for using a legitimate return process.

---

# Customer Account Standard

The customer account should become the customer's personal space within SAB’A.

It may contain:

- Personal information
- Addresses
- Orders
- Tracking
- Returns
- Wishlist
- Loyalty
- Points
- Rewards
- Coupons
- Membership status
- Preferences

The account should become more useful as the relationship grows.

---

# Loyalty Standard

Loyalty should reward relationship and recognition.

Possible mechanisms include:

- Points
- Rewards
- Exclusive access
- Early releases
- Complimentary products
- Special offers
- Shipping benefits

Loyalty must not turn SAB’A into a discount-driven business.

---

# Digital Loyalty Card

Where technically and commercially appropriate, SAB’A may introduce a digital loyalty card connected to:

- Apple Wallet
- Google Wallet
- Samsung Wallet

The card may communicate:

- Membership
- Points
- Rewards
- Offers
- Customer recognition

The loyalty platform remains the source of truth.

The wallet is the customer's access point.

---

# Customer Communication

Ecommerce communication should operate through clear channels.

Primary communication channels may include:

- Website
- Email
- WhatsApp
- Customer account
- AI
- Social platforms

Each channel should have a defined role.

---

# Email Architecture

SAB’A should distinguish between different types of email communication.

### Transactional Email

Used for:

- Receipts
- Order confirmation
- Payment confirmation
- Shipping updates
- Delivery confirmation
- Refunds
- Return updates

### Marketing and Editorial Email

Used for:

- New products
- Collections
- Stories
- Announcements
- Offers
- Campaigns
- Newsletters

### Customer Care Email

Used for:

- Complaints
- Questions
- Return assistance
- Order problems
- Customer support

These functions should remain operationally distinct even if they share the same overall brand identity.

---

# WhatsApp Standard

WhatsApp may serve as a direct customer communication and support channel.

It may support:

- Order updates
- Delivery updates
- Customer care
- AI assistance
- General questions
- Campaign communication where appropriate

WhatsApp must not become a replacement for the central ecommerce system.

It is a communication layer.

---

# AI Customer Care

SAB’A AI may become an important customer-service interface.

It may assist with:

- Product discovery
- Product questions
- Order questions
- Shipping information
- Return guidance
- General policies
- Account assistance where integrated

AI must use verified information.

It must never invent:

- Prices
- Availability
- Delivery dates
- Discounts
- Payment status
- Refund status
- Product specifications

When the issue requires human judgement or authority, the conversation should be escalated.

---

# Social Commerce Continuity

SAB’A's social presence should connect naturally to ecommerce.

Relevant channels may include:

- Instagram
- TikTok
- X

Social platforms may support:

- Product discovery
- Storytelling
- Campaigns
- Community
- Competitions
- Launches
- Traffic to ecommerce

Social content should never contradict the current ecommerce information.

---

# X Competitions

X may be used periodically for direct community engagement.

Examples may include:

- Competitions
- Campaign challenges
- Limited coupons
- Product-related activities
- Special rewards

Competitions must have:

- Clear rules
- Defined duration
- Transparent eligibility
- Clear winner selection
- Appropriate fulfilment

The purpose should be community and engagement, not constant discounting.

---

# Promotional Discipline

Promotions must be intentional.

The ecommerce system should support:

- Coupons
- Percentage discounts
- Fixed discounts
- Bundles
- Complimentary products
- Buy-more offers
- Loyalty rewards
- Shipping benefits

Promotions must never become the permanent language of the brand.

---

# Performance Standard

Ecommerce must perform efficiently across devices.

Priority areas include:

- Fast loading
- Stable rendering
- Responsive interaction
- Fast checkout
- Reliable payment
- Efficient mobile experience

Performance is part of luxury.

A slow experience is an inconsistent experience.

---

# Mobile Standard

Mobile is not a secondary experience.

Every core ecommerce journey must be designed for mobile.

This includes:

- Product discovery
- Product pages
- Account
- Cart
- Checkout
- Payment
- Tracking
- Returns
- Customer care

Touch interactions must be comfortable and clear.

---

# Accessibility Standard

Ecommerce must be accessible to as many customers as reasonably possible.

This includes:

- Clear typography
- Sufficient contrast
- Keyboard accessibility
- Screen-reader support
- Logical navigation
- Clear labels
- Understandable errors
- Appropriate touch targets

Accessibility should be integrated into the system rather than added later.

---

# Security Standard

Security must be considered across the entire ecommerce ecosystem.

Areas include:

- Customer accounts
- Authentication
- Payments
- Personal information
- Orders
- Loyalty
- Coupons
- APIs
- Third-party integrations

Security must not rely on the customer understanding technical risks.

The system should protect them by design.

---

# Privacy Standard

Only necessary customer information should be collected.

Customer information must be handled responsibly across:

- Website
- Ecommerce
- Customer account
- Email
- WhatsApp
- AI
- Customer care

Marketing consent and transactional communication must remain conceptually distinct.

---

# AI Safety and Trust

AI must operate within defined boundaries.

AI should:

- Be helpful
- Be accurate
- Be transparent
- Use verified information
- Escalate when necessary

AI should not:

- Guess
- Invent
- Manipulate
- Promise unsupported outcomes
- Request unnecessary sensitive information

Trust in SAB’A must never depend on an AI making something up.

---

# Third-Party Integrations

Third-party services may support:

- Payments
- Shipping
- Analytics
- Customer communication
- Loyalty
- Authentication
- Other ecommerce functions

Every integration must be evaluated according to:

- Reliability
- Security
- Data handling
- Scalability
- Customer experience
- Brand impact
- Exit strategy

A third-party tool must never dictate the identity of the SAB’A experience.

---

# Technology Principle

The ecommerce architecture should remain technology-agnostic where practical.

Technology may change.

The principles should not.

The system should be capable of replacing or upgrading individual services without destroying the broader experience.

---

# Scalability

The ecommerce system must support growth in:

- Products
- Collections
- Customers
- Orders
- Markets
- Currencies
- Payment methods
- Shipping providers
- Loyalty capabilities
- Customer communication

Growth must not require sacrificing coherence.

---

# International Expansion

Future market expansion should account for:

- Language
- Currency
- Taxes
- Payments
- Shipping
- Returns
- Legal requirements
- Customer expectations

Each market may require local adaptation.

The underlying SAB’A experience must remain recognisable.

---

# Analytics

Analytics should be used to understand and improve the customer journey.

Relevant measurements may include:

- Product engagement
- Add-to-cart rate
- Checkout initiation
- Checkout completion
- Payment success
- Cart abandonment
- Return rate
- Customer retention
- Loyalty engagement
- Customer support volume

Analytics should inform decisions without replacing judgement.

---

# Customer Experience Measurement

The business should monitor more than conversion.

Important indicators may include:

- Customer satisfaction
- Repeat purchase
- Return reasons
- Customer complaints
- Support resolution
- Delivery satisfaction
- Product satisfaction
- Loyalty participation

The objective is not simply:

**More orders**

It is:

**Better relationships.**

---

# Operational Resilience

Critical ecommerce functions should have appropriate recovery plans.

The system should be prepared for:

- Payment provider failure
- Shipping provider failure
- Inventory errors
- Website outages
- Third-party outages
- Data inconsistencies

Customers should experience as little disruption as possible.

---

# Quality Assurance

Every significant ecommerce change should be tested before release.

Testing should cover:

- Functionality
- Mobile
- Desktop
- Accessibility
- Payments
- Checkout
- Shipping
- Returns
- Customer account
- Email
- WhatsApp
- AI
- Integrations

No feature should be considered complete merely because it works technically.

---

# Launch Standard

Before launching a new ecommerce feature, confirm:

1. **Does it solve a real customer or business need?**
2. **Is the experience clear?**
3. **Is the information accurate?**
4. **Is the feature secure?**
5. **Does it work on mobile?**
6. **Is it accessible?**
7. **Does it integrate with the existing ecosystem?**
8. **Does customer care understand it?**
9. **Can AI explain it accurately where relevant?**
10. **Are email and WhatsApp flows considered?**
11. **Are failures and edge cases handled?**
12. **Can the system scale?**
13. **Does it maintain the SAB’A standard?**

---

# Design Standard

Ecommerce design must reflect the broader SAB’A visual language.

Use:

- Space
- Hierarchy
- Restraint
- Typography
- Rhythm
- Consistency
- Purposeful motion

Avoid:

- Visual clutter
- Excessive badges
- Aggressive promotional elements
- Unnecessary animation
- Generic ecommerce patterns
- Interface noise

---

# Motion Standard

Motion should communicate:

- Transition
- Continuity
- Feedback
- Hierarchy

Motion must never exist merely to demonstrate technology.

It should be:

**Purposeful → Controlled → Quiet**

---

# Content Standard

Every customer-facing ecommerce message should be:

- Clear
- Concise
- Human
- Accurate
- Consistent with SAB’A

Avoid unnecessary technical language.

Avoid exaggerated promises.

Avoid generic ecommerce clichés.

---

# Error Standard

Errors should be treated as part of the experience.

A good error message should communicate:

**What happened → What it means → What the customer can do**

The customer should never be blamed for a system error.

---

# Empty States

Empty states should remain useful.

Examples include:

- Empty cart
- No orders
- No wishlist items
- No rewards
- No available coupons

The interface should explain the state and provide an appropriate next step where useful.

---

# Search Standard

Search should help customers discover products and information quickly.

It should support relevant:

- Product names
- Categories
- Collections
- Attributes
- Keywords

Poor search should not become a barrier to discovery.

---

# Customer Trust Standard

Trust must be visible throughout the experience.

Customers should understand:

- What they are buying
- What it costs
- How they will pay
- When it will arrive
- What happens if something goes wrong
- How to contact SAB’A

Trust is not created by one page.

It is created through consistency.

---

# Brand Standard

Every ecommerce interaction must answer one question:

**Does this feel like SAB’A?**

This includes the moments that are often overlooked:

- Error messages
- Payment failures
- Delivery delays
- Refund confirmations
- Return instructions
- Empty carts
- Customer support
- Transactional emails

The brand exists in the details.

---

# What Ecommerce Must Never Become

SAB’A ecommerce must never become:

- A generic online store
- A discount machine
- A collection of disconnected tools
- A technically impressive but emotionally empty system
- A maze of unnecessary steps
- A source of contradictory information
- A platform controlled by third-party limitations
- An experience that forgets the customer after payment

---

# Ecommerce Governance

The ecommerce ecosystem should have clear ownership.

Responsibilities should be defined for:

- Product data
- Pricing
- Promotions
- Orders
- Payments
- Shipping
- Returns
- Customer account
- Loyalty
- Customer communication
- AI
- Technical infrastructure

No critical commercial function should exist without a clear owner.

---

# Continuous Improvement

The ecommerce system should evolve continuously.

Improvements should be based on:

- Customer feedback
- Analytics
- Operational data
- Support conversations
- Return reasons
- Technical performance
- Business needs

However:

**Evolution must never become identity drift.**

Every improvement must preserve the foundations of SAB’A.

---

# Final Ecommerce Checklist

Before approving any ecommerce experience, ask:

### Customer

- Is it clear?
- Is it easy?
- Is it trustworthy?
- Is it useful?

### Commercial

- Is the pricing accurate?
- Is the transaction reliable?
- Is the system scalable?

### Technical

- Is it fast?
- Is it secure?
- Is it resilient?
- Is it maintainable?

### Brand

- Does it feel like SAB’A?
- Is it restrained?
- Is it consistent?
- Does it protect the house's positioning?

### Relationship

- Does it strengthen trust?
- Does it support the customer after purchase?
- Does it contribute to long-term loyalty?

If the answer is no to any critical question, the experience is not ready.

---

# Core Standard

SAB’A ecommerce should be:

**Commercially strong.  
Technically reliable.  
Operationally disciplined.  
Emotionally coherent.  
Humanly considerate.**

The customer should never need to understand the complexity behind the system.

They should simply experience:

**Clarity.  
Confidence.  
Desire.  
Ease.  
Care.**

---

# Core Statement

The SAB’A ecommerce ecosystem is not a collection of commercial tools.

It is one continuous environment through which the customer:

**Discovers → Understands → Desires → Purchases → Receives → Experiences → Returns → Reconnects**

Every system must support that journey.

Every channel must reinforce it.

Every transaction must protect it.

And every future feature must earn its place within it.

**Technology serves the experience.**

**Commerce serves the relationship.**

**The relationship serves the house.**

That is the SAB’A ecommerce standard.
