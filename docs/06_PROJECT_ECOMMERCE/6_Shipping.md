# Shipping

## Purpose

The SAB’A shipping system defines how creations move from the house to the customer.

Shipping is not merely a logistics function.

It is the physical continuation of the digital experience.

The journey should feel like:

**Purchase → Anticipation → Movement → Arrival → Ownership**

The governing principle is:

**The experience does not end when the order leaves the house.**

---

# Shipping Philosophy

Shipping must balance:

- Reliability
- Speed
- Protection
- Transparency
- Flexibility
- Service
- Brand experience

The customer should always understand what is happening with their order.

No unnecessary uncertainty should exist between payment and arrival.

---

# Shipping Journey

The standard journey is:

**Order Confirmed → Processing → Prepared → Shipped → In Transit → Out for Delivery → Delivered**

Additional states may exist where required.

The status shown to the customer should remain simple and understandable.

---

# Shipping Architecture

The shipping system connects:

- Ecommerce
- Inventory
- Fulfilment
- Delivery partners
- Customer account
- Email
- WhatsApp
- Customer care
- Returns

The central ecommerce system should remain the source of truth for the customer's order status.

---

# Shipping Availability

Shipping availability should be defined by:

- Country
- Region
- City
- Postal or address eligibility
- Product requirements
- Inventory location
- Delivery partner coverage

The customer should know whether their destination is supported before completing the purchase.

---

# Delivery Zones

SAB’A may define delivery zones according to operational requirements.

These may include:

- Local delivery
- Domestic delivery
- Regional delivery
- International delivery

Each zone may have different:

- Delivery times
- Costs
- Services
- Restrictions
- Customs requirements

---

# Delivery Options

Where commercially appropriate, customers may be offered different delivery options.

Examples may include:

- Standard delivery
- Express delivery
- Scheduled delivery
- Other approved services

Each option should clearly communicate:

- Estimated delivery time
- Cost
- Availability

---

# Delivery Estimates

Delivery estimates should be realistic.

The system should distinguish between:

**Processing Time**

and

**Transit Time**

Where appropriate, the customer should understand both.

Delivery estimates must not be presented as guaranteed unless the service genuinely provides a guarantee.

---

# Shipping Cost

Shipping costs must be clearly communicated before payment.

The customer should understand:

- Shipping fee
- Free-shipping eligibility
- Any minimum order requirement
- Applicable additional charges

Unexpected shipping fees must not appear after payment.

---

# Free Shipping

SAB’A may offer free shipping when commercially appropriate.

Possible conditions may include:

- Minimum order value
- Selected campaigns
- Loyalty benefits
- Specific collections
- Specific markets

Conditions must remain clear.

Free shipping should be treated as a benefit, not a permanent discount expectation.

---

# Shipping Calculation

Shipping calculations may depend on:

- Destination
- Order size
- Product type
- Weight
- Delivery method
- Market
- Current shipping rules

The customer-facing result must remain simple even when the underlying calculation is complex.

---

# Product Shipping Eligibility

Some products may have specific shipping requirements.

These may relate to:

- Size
- Weight
- Fragility
- Temperature
- Materials
- Regulatory requirements
- International restrictions

Such restrictions must be communicated before purchase.

---

# Packaging

Packaging is part of the physical SAB’A experience.

The package should communicate:

- Care
- Protection
- Precision
- Identity
- Restraint

Packaging should feel considered without unnecessary excess.

---

# Product Protection

Products must be packaged appropriately to prevent:

- Damage
- Leakage where applicable
- Breakage
- Contamination
- Presentation damage

The physical condition of the product upon arrival must meet the expected SAB’A standard.

---

# Packaging Identity

Where appropriate, packaging should maintain consistency with:

- SAB’A visual identity
- Product identity
- Collection
- Gifting experience

The package should feel like a continuation of the house rather than a generic shipping box.

---

# Gift Shipping

Where gifting is supported, shipping may include:

- Gift packaging
- Gift message
- Recipient details
- Gift-specific presentation

The customer should be able to distinguish between the delivery address and the purchaser's information where applicable.

---

# Order Processing

After successful payment, the order enters fulfilment.

The customer should receive confirmation that the order has been accepted.

The internal process may include:

**Payment Confirmed → Inventory Reserved → Order Prepared → Quality Check → Packed → Handover**

---

# Inventory Reservation

Once an order is confirmed, inventory should be reserved according to the ecommerce system's rules.

The system must reduce the risk of selling the same available unit to multiple customers.

---

# Order Preparation

Before shipment, the fulfilment process should verify:

- Correct product
- Correct variant
- Correct quantity
- Correct packaging
- Correct destination
- Order condition

A final quality check should occur before handover where operationally appropriate.

---

# Shipping Confirmation

When an order leaves SAB’A, the customer should receive a shipping confirmation.

It should communicate:

- Order number
- Shipment status
- Tracking information
- Delivery estimate where available
- Customer care access

---

# Tracking

Customers should be able to track their order through appropriate channels.

Tracking may be available through:

- Customer account
- Website
- Email
- WhatsApp where enabled
- Delivery provider

The information should remain consistent across channels.

---

# Tracking Experience

Tracking should translate logistics into simple customer language.

Instead of exposing unnecessary technical events, the customer should primarily see:

**Where the order is.  
What has happened.  
What happens next.**

---

# Customer Account

The customer account should provide access to:

- Order status
- Tracking
- Delivery address
- Order details
- Shipping information
- Customer care

Tracking should remain accessible after the initial confirmation email.

---

# Email Communication

Shipping-related email may include:

- Order confirmation
- Shipment confirmation
- Tracking information
- Delivery notification
- Delay notification
- Delivery issue notification

Transactional communication should remain functional while maintaining SAB’A's identity.

---

# WhatsApp Communication

Where enabled, WhatsApp may provide useful shipping communication.

This may include:

- Shipment updates
- Tracking
- Delivery notifications
- Delivery questions
- Customer support

WhatsApp should complement the central order system.

It should never create conflicting information.

---

# Customer Care

Customer care should be able to access relevant shipping information.

This may include:

- Order status
- Tracking number
- Delivery partner
- Estimated delivery
- Shipping address
- Delivery issues

This allows customer care to resolve questions without repeatedly asking the customer for information already available in the system.

---

# SAB’A AI and Shipping

SAB’A AI may help customers understand:

- Shipping options
- Delivery estimates
- Tracking
- General shipping policies
- Delivery status

AI must rely on verified ecommerce and logistics data.

It must never invent:

- Tracking information
- Delivery dates
- Shipping fees
- Delivery guarantees
- Customs outcomes

---

# Delivery Delay

If a shipment is delayed, the customer should be informed when the delay becomes relevant.

The communication should explain:

- That there is a delay
- Updated expectation where available
- What the customer can do
- How to contact support

The system should avoid vague or misleading reassurance.

---

# Failed Delivery

If delivery cannot be completed, the customer should receive clear information.

Possible causes may include:

- Incorrect address
- Customer unavailable
- Delivery restriction
- Courier issue
- Customs issue

The customer should receive an appropriate next step.

---

# Address Changes

Address changes should be supported where operationally possible.

Once an order reaches a certain fulfilment stage, changes may no longer be possible.

The customer should be informed clearly rather than promised an action the system cannot perform.

---

# Delivery Confirmation

After successful delivery, the order should transition to:

**Delivered**

The customer may receive a delivery confirmation through:

- Email
- WhatsApp
- Customer account

The confirmation should remain simple.

---

# Delivery Experience

The moment of arrival should feel like the physical conclusion of the digital journey.

Where appropriate, the customer experience may continue through:

- Product care information
- Storytelling
- Loyalty recognition
- Customer account
- Follow-up communication

The goal is to transition naturally from:

**Delivery → Ownership**

---

# International Shipping

International shipping may be introduced as SAB’A expands.

The system should account for:

- Destination eligibility
- Customs
- Duties
- Taxes
- Import restrictions
- Delivery partners
- Local regulations

International delivery information must remain transparent.

---

# Customs and Duties

Where applicable, customers should understand whether customs duties or import charges:

- Are included
- Are calculated at checkout
- Are payable upon delivery

SAB’A should never promise that customs charges will not apply unless the business can guarantee that outcome.

---

# Shipping Restrictions

Certain products or destinations may have restrictions.

The system should identify restrictions before checkout wherever possible.

Customers should not discover basic shipping incompatibility only after payment.

---

# Shipping Partners

SAB’A may work with one or more logistics providers.

Partners should be evaluated according to:

- Reliability
- Coverage
- Tracking
- Delivery performance
- Customer experience
- Handling quality
- Support
- Integration capability

The customer experience must remain SAB’A regardless of which provider performs the delivery.

---

# Shipping Performance

The business should monitor:

- Delivery time
- On-time delivery rate
- Failed deliveries
- Damaged orders
- Lost shipments
- Customer complaints
- Shipping cost
- Return-to-sender rate

Shipping performance should be continuously improved.

---

# Damaged Orders

If a product arrives damaged, the customer should have a clear route to customer care.

The process should support:

- Reporting
- Evidence where appropriate
- Investigation
- Replacement or refund according to policy

The customer should not be forced to navigate multiple unrelated systems.

---

# Lost Shipments

If a shipment is confirmed lost, SAB’A should provide a clear resolution process.

Possible outcomes may include:

- Replacement
- Refund
- Investigation
- Other approved resolution

The outcome should follow the applicable policy.

---

# Shipping and Returns

Shipping must integrate with the returns process.

Where a return is approved, the system should determine:

- Return method
- Return shipping responsibility
- Tracking
- Receipt
- Refund process

Detailed rules belong in:

**7_Returns and Exchanges.md**

---

# Shipping and Loyalty

Where applicable, loyalty benefits may include:

- Free shipping
- Preferred shipping
- Early access
- Special delivery services

Such benefits must be clearly defined within the loyalty system.

---

# Shipping and Promotions

Promotional campaigns may include shipping-related benefits.

Examples:

- Free shipping
- Reduced shipping
- Express shipping
- Limited delivery campaigns

Conditions must remain transparent.

---

# Shipping Data

Shipping information must remain consistent across:

- Ecommerce
- Customer account
- Email
- WhatsApp
- AI
- Customer care

The system should avoid contradictory delivery information.

---

# Shipping Security

Customer delivery information must be handled securely.

Sensitive information should only be accessible to authorised systems and personnel.

Delivery information should not be unnecessarily exposed through customer communications.

---

# Shipping Scalability

The shipping architecture should support future expansion into:

- New cities
- New regions
- New countries
- New delivery providers
- New fulfilment centres
- New delivery services

Expansion should not require rebuilding the core ecommerce architecture.

---

# Shipping Quality Audit

Before launching or changing a shipping service, verify:

1. **Is the destination supported?**
2. **Is the shipping price correct?**
3. **Is the delivery estimate realistic?**
4. **Is inventory correctly connected?**
5. **Is packaging appropriate?**
6. **Is tracking available?**
7. **Are email notifications working?**
8. **Is WhatsApp communication accurate where enabled?**
9. **Can customer care access shipment information?**
10. **Can AI provide verified shipping information?**
11. **Are delays handled clearly?**
12. **Are damaged and lost shipments supported?**
13. **Are international restrictions communicated?**
14. **Does the physical delivery meet the SAB’A standard?**

---

# What Shipping Must Never Become

Shipping must never feel like:

- A black box
- An afterthought
- A source of surprise fees
- A confusing tracking system
- A generic logistics experience
- A disconnected third-party interaction

The customer should always know what is happening.

---

# Core Standard

The ideal SAB’A shipping experience is:

**Reliable enough to trust.  
Clear enough to understand.  
Careful enough to protect the product.  
Refined enough to represent the house.**

---

# Core Statement

Shipping is the bridge between the digital purchase and physical ownership.

The customer begins with:

**Discovery**

Then:

**Desire → Purchase → Anticipation**

And finally:

**Arrival → Ownership**

SAB’A must protect the quality of that entire journey.

**The package is not merely something that carries the product.**

**It is the final physical expression of the experience the customer entered online.**
