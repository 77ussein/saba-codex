# Costing

## Purpose

Costing defines how SAB’A identifies, calculates, documents, and reviews the costs associated with its products and commercial activities.

The objective is to establish a reliable understanding of product economics so that decisions regarding pricing, production, inventory, and growth are based on realistic costs.

## Costing Principles

1. **Complete Cost Visibility**
2. **Accuracy before Simplicity**
3. **Consistent Methodology**
4. **Traceable Assumptions**
5. **Current Supplier Information**
6. **Unit-Level Understanding**
7. **Review after Material Changes**
8. **Cost Awareness without Compromising Quality**

## Cost Structure

Product costing may include:

- Raw Materials
- Fragrance Materials
- Packaging Components
- Manufacturing
- Filling and Assembly
- Product Testing
- Quality Control
- Labels and Printing
- Product-Specific Logistics
- Storage
- Fulfillment
- Payment Fees
- Applicable Duties or Taxes
- Other Relevant Direct or Allocated Costs

The exact cost structure should reflect the actual product and operating model.

## Direct Costs

Direct costs are costs that can be reasonably associated with producing or delivering a specific product.

Examples include:

- Ingredients
- Fragrance
- Bottles
- Caps
- Boxes
- Labels
- Manufacturing
- Product-Specific Testing

Direct costs should be recorded using the most reliable available information.

## Indirect Costs

Indirect costs support the broader business and may not be directly attributable to a single product.

Examples may include:

- General Administration
- Software
- Professional Services
- General Marketing
- Office Expenses
- General Storage

Indirect costs should only be allocated to products when the allocation method is meaningful and consistently applied.

## Unit Cost

The unit cost should represent the relevant cost required to produce or deliver one unit under the defined costing methodology.

A basic calculation may be:

Unit Cost = Total Relevant Product Costs ÷ Number of Finished Units

The methodology used should be documented so that unit costs remain comparable over time.

## Packaging Costing

Packaging should be costed at the appropriate component level where practical.

Components may include:

- Primary Container
- Closure
- Label
- Carton
- Inserts
- Protective Materials
- Shipping Packaging

Packaging changes should trigger a costing review when they materially affect unit economics.

## Manufacturing Costing

Manufacturing costs may include:

- Filling
- Assembly
- Labor
- Production Services
- Setup
- Tooling where applicable
- Quality Control
- Production Waste

Manufacturing assumptions should reflect the actual production arrangement.

## Waste and Yield

Costing should account for material waste or production yield where material to the economics of the product.

Relevant considerations may include:

- Production Loss
- Filling Loss
- Material Waste
- Damaged Units
- Rejects
- Minimum Production Quantities

Yield assumptions should be based on actual production data when available.

## Minimum Order Quantities

Supplier minimum order quantities may affect the effective cost of a product.

Costing should distinguish between:

- Unit Cost
- Minimum Purchase Commitment
- Total Cash Requirement

A lower unit price may still create an undesirable financial position if it requires excessive inventory or cash commitment.

## Supplier Pricing

Supplier prices should be based on current and reliable information.

Records should include, where relevant:

- Supplier
- Item
- Unit Price
- Currency
- Minimum Order Quantity
- Price Breaks
- Lead Time
- Effective Date
- Quotation Reference

Material supplier price changes should trigger a costing review.

## Currency

Where costs are incurred in different currencies, the costing methodology should clearly identify:

- Original Currency
- Conversion Rate
- Conversion Date
- Converted Cost

Foreign exchange assumptions should be reviewed when currency movements materially affect product economics.

## Landed Cost

Where applicable, SAB’A may calculate landed cost to reflect the cost of bringing a product or material into its intended location.

This may include:

- Purchase Cost
- Freight
- Insurance
- Duties
- Customs Charges
- Import Fees
- Other Direct Import Costs

The components included in landed cost should be clearly defined.

## Fulfillment and Delivery

Depending on the costing purpose, SAB’A may evaluate fulfillment and delivery costs separately from product cost or include them in a broader contribution-cost model.

Relevant costs may include:

- Pick and Pack
- Shipping
- Packaging for Shipment
- Payment Processing
- Delivery Services
- Returns

The treatment should remain consistent across financial analysis.

## Contribution Cost

For commercial decision-making, SAB’A may calculate a broader contribution cost beyond the basic product cost.

A contribution model may consider:

Selling Price  
− Product Cost  
− Variable Selling Costs  
= Contribution

Relevant variable costs may include:

- Payment Fees
- Fulfillment
- Shipping
- Transaction Costs
- Other Sales-Dependent Costs

## Costing and Pricing

Costing provides an input to pricing but does not determine the final price by itself.

Pricing should also consider:

- Brand Positioning
- Customer Value
- Market Context
- Product Strategy
- Desired Margin
- Channel Economics

The relationship between costing and pricing should be maintained in the Pricing Model.

## Costing and Inventory

Inventory should be evaluated using an appropriate and consistent costing method.

Cost records should support visibility into:

- Inventory Value
- Unit Cost
- Purchase Commitments
- Production Costs
- Slow-Moving Inventory
- Obsolete or Damaged Inventory

Accounting treatment should follow the applicable accounting framework.

## Cost Changes

A costing review should be considered when there are material changes to:

- Supplier Prices
- Raw Materials
- Fragrance
- Packaging
- Manufacturing
- Shipping
- Duties
- Production Yield
- Product Specifications

Cost changes should be documented with their effective date.

## Costing Versions

Where product costs change over time, previous costing records should be retained.

Each material costing version should identify:

- Product
- Version
- Effective Date
- Cost Assumptions
- Supplier Inputs
- Total Unit Cost
- Approval Status

Historical costing should not replace the current approved costing.

## Costing Review

Product costing should be reviewed:

- During Product Development
- Before Production
- Before Launch
- After Material Supplier Changes
- After Packaging Changes
- After Manufacturing Changes
- During Periodic Financial Review

The frequency should reflect the volatility and importance of the product.

## Costing Accuracy

Costing should distinguish between:

- Confirmed Costs
- Estimated Costs
- Assumed Costs
- One-Time Costs
- Recurring Costs

Unconfirmed assumptions should be clearly identified rather than presented as final costs.

## Costing Record

A standard costing record may include:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Product | Product being costed |
| SKU | Product identifier |
| Version | Costing version |
| Effective Date | Date the costing applies |
| Supplier Inputs | Relevant supplier information |
| Material Costs | Raw material and fragrance costs |
| Packaging Costs | Packaging components |
| Manufacturing | Production costs |
| Other Direct Costs | Relevant additional costs |
| Unit Cost | Calculated unit cost |
| Assumptions | Important assumptions |
| Status | Draft / Approved / Superseded |
| Owner | Responsible person |

## Professional Financial Advice

Accounting treatment, inventory valuation, tax treatment, and other specialized financial matters should be reviewed with appropriately qualified professionals where required.

This document establishes an internal costing framework and does not replace professional accounting or financial advice.

## Core Principle

> SAB’A should know what a product truly costs before deciding what it should cost the business to make, sell, and grow.
