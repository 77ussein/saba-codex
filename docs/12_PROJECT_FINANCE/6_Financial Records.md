# Financial Records

## Purpose

The Financial Records document defines how SAB’A creates, maintains, organizes, and protects the financial records required to understand and manage the business.

The objective is to ensure that financial information is accurate, traceable, accessible, consistent, and sufficiently documented to support sound decision-making and appropriate financial reporting.

## Financial Records Principles

1. **Accuracy**
2. **Completeness**
3. **Traceability**
4. **Consistency**
5. **Timeliness**
6. **Controlled Access**
7. **Documented Evidence**
8. **Appropriate Retention**

## Financial Records Philosophy

Financial records should provide a reliable representation of SAB’A’s financial activity.

Records should allow the business to understand:

- What was received
- What was paid
- What was purchased
- What was sold
- What is owed
- What is owned
- What is committed
- Why a financial transaction occurred

Financial records should support both day-to-day management and longer-term financial analysis.

## Record Categories

Financial records may include:

- Sales Records
- Purchase Records
- Supplier Invoices
- Customer Invoices
- Receipts
- Payment Records
- Bank Records
- Expense Records
- Payroll Records where applicable
- Tax Records
- Inventory Records
- Asset Records
- Loan or Financing Records
- Contracts with Financial Implications
- Financial Reports
- Budgets
- Forecasts
- Costing Records
- Pricing Records

## Transaction Records

Each material financial transaction should have sufficient supporting information.

Where relevant, records should identify:

- Date
- Description
- Amount
- Currency
- Supplier or Customer
- Category
- Payment Method
- Reference Number
- Supporting Document
- Approval where required

## Sales Records

Sales records should provide sufficient information to understand revenue activity.

Relevant information may include:

- Order Date
- Product
- SKU
- Quantity
- Selling Price
- Discount
- Taxes where applicable
- Shipping Charges
- Payment Status
- Refunds
- Net Sales

Sales records should remain consistent with the applicable accounting and reporting system.

## Purchase Records

Purchase records should document material business purchases.

Relevant information may include:

- Supplier
- Purchase Date
- Item or Service
- Quantity
- Unit Price
- Total Cost
- Currency
- Payment Terms
- Payment Status
- Supporting Invoice
- Related Product or Project where applicable

## Expense Records

Business expenses should be categorized consistently.

Each material expense should have:

- Business Purpose
- Amount
- Date
- Supplier or Recipient
- Category
- Supporting Evidence
- Approval where required

Personal or non-business expenses should not be recorded as business expenses unless appropriately permitted and documented under the applicable financial framework.

## Bank Records

Bank activity should be reconciled against internal financial records.

Reconciliation should identify and investigate differences between:

- Bank Statements
- Recorded Transactions
- Outstanding Payments
- Outstanding Receipts

Bank reconciliations should be performed at an appropriate frequency.

## Accounts Payable

Where SAB’A has outstanding supplier obligations, records should provide visibility into:

- Supplier
- Invoice
- Amount
- Due Date
- Payment Status
- Payment Reference

Outstanding obligations should be incorporated into cash flow planning.

## Accounts Receivable

Where SAB’A is owed money by customers or commercial partners, records should provide visibility into:

- Customer
- Invoice
- Amount
- Due Date
- Payment Status
- Collection Date

Outstanding receivables should be monitored appropriately.

## Inventory Records

Inventory records should support visibility into:

- Product
- SKU
- Quantity
- Unit Cost
- Total Value
- Location
- Purchase Date
- Production Date where relevant
- Movement
- Damaged or Obsolete Stock

Inventory records should remain consistent with the applicable inventory valuation method.

## Asset Records

Material business assets should be documented appropriately.

Records may include:

- Asset Description
- Acquisition Date
- Acquisition Cost
- Location
- Ownership
- Useful Life where applicable
- Depreciation where applicable
- Disposal Date

Accounting treatment should follow the applicable accounting framework.

## Financial Reporting

Financial records should support the preparation and review of relevant financial reports.

These may include:

- Profit and Loss
- Balance Sheet
- Cash Flow Statement
- Sales Reports
- Expense Reports
- Inventory Reports
- Management Reports

The appropriate reporting structure should reflect the size and needs of SAB’A.

## Documentation Standards

Financial documentation should be:

- Legible
- Complete
- Correct
- Properly dated
- Traceable to the relevant transaction
- Stored in an organized manner

Where digital records are used, the system should preserve sufficient information to identify the original transaction and supporting evidence.

## Record Organization

Financial records should follow a consistent structure.

A record structure may include:

- Financial Year
- Month
- Category
- Supplier or Customer
- Transaction Type
- Supporting Document

The exact structure may evolve as SAB’A grows, provided consistency is maintained.

## Record Retention

Financial records should be retained for the period required by applicable law, regulation, accounting requirements, and business needs.

Retention requirements should be reviewed periodically with qualified professional advice where necessary.

Records subject to legal, tax, audit, or contractual requirements should not be deleted prematurely.

## Access Control

Access to financial records should be limited according to responsibility.

Access levels may include:

- View
- Create
- Edit
- Approve
- Export
- Administrative Access

Sensitive financial information should not be accessible to unauthorized individuals.

## Financial Approvals

Material financial transactions should follow appropriate approval requirements.

Depending on the transaction, approval may be required for:

- Purchases
- Supplier Payments
- Refunds
- Discounts
- Capital Expenditure
- Contracts
- Financing
- Exceptional Expenses

Approval thresholds should be defined separately where appropriate.

## Corrections and Adjustments

Errors should be corrected transparently.

Financial records should not be altered in a way that removes the ability to understand the original transaction or the reason for the correction.

Material adjustments should include:

- Date
- Reason
- Amount
- Supporting Evidence
- Approver where required

## Audit Trail

Where systems support audit trails, SAB’A should preserve sufficient history to identify:

- Who created a record
- Who changed a record
- When it was changed
- What was changed
- Why it was changed where relevant

The level of auditability should be proportionate to the financial importance of the record.

## Data Security

Financial records should be protected against:

- Unauthorized Access
- Accidental Deletion
- Data Loss
- Unauthorized Modification
- Theft
- Uncontrolled Sharing

Appropriate backup and security practices should be maintained.

## Financial Data Accuracy

Financial records should be reviewed for:

- Duplicate Transactions
- Missing Transactions
- Incorrect Amounts
- Incorrect Categories
- Currency Errors
- Unreconciled Balances
- Missing Supporting Documents

Material discrepancies should be investigated and resolved.

## Confidentiality

Financial information should be treated as confidential business information unless disclosure is authorized or legally required.

Financial information should only be shared with:

- Authorized Team Members
- Approved Advisors
- Relevant Financial Institutions
- Regulators or Authorities where required
- Other Authorized Parties

## Monthly Financial Review

Where appropriate, SAB’A should conduct a periodic financial records review covering:

- Revenue
- Expenses
- Cash
- Accounts Payable
- Accounts Receivable
- Inventory
- Major Commitments
- Material Variances

The frequency may increase as transaction volume or financial complexity increases.

## Financial Records and Decision-Making

Financial records should provide reliable inputs for:

- Costing
- Pricing
- Budgeting
- Cash Flow
- Forecasting
- Investment Decisions
- Product Decisions
- Strategic Planning

Poor records create poor financial visibility and increase decision-making risk.

## Relationship with Other Financial Documents

Financial Records should work together with:

- Financial Framework
- Budget
- Costing
- Pricing Model
- Cash Flow

Financial Records provide the underlying evidence.

Financial Framework defines the financial management principles.

Costing defines product economics.

Pricing Model defines commercial pricing.

Cash Flow defines the timing of cash movement.

## Professional Financial Advice

Accounting records, tax records, statutory reporting, audit requirements, and other specialized financial matters should be managed in accordance with applicable laws and professional accounting advice.

This document establishes an internal financial records framework and does not replace professional accounting or legal advice.

## Core Principle

> SAB’A should be able to trace every material financial number back to reliable evidence and understand exactly what it represents.
