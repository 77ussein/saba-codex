# Budget

## Purpose

The Budget defines how SAB’A plans, allocates, monitors, and reviews financial resources across the business.

The objective is to ensure that spending supports business priorities, remains controlled, and reflects the financial capacity and strategic direction of SAB’A.

## Budget Principles

1. **Purpose before Spending**
2. **Planning before Commitment**
3. **Priorities before Distribution**
4. **Visibility before Optimization**
5. **Discipline without Unnecessary Restriction**
6. **Actual Performance over Assumption**
7. **Flexibility with Control**
8. **Financial Sustainability**

## Budget Structure

The SAB’A budget may be organized across major business areas, including:

- Product Development
- Fragrance
- Packaging
- Production
- Inventory
- Website and Ecommerce
- Marketing
- Content
- Operations
- Customer Experience
- Legal
- Technology
- Professional Services
- Administration
- Contingency

The exact structure may evolve with the size and complexity of the business.

## Budget Periods

Budgets may be prepared across different planning periods.

These may include:

- Annual Budget
- Quarterly Budget
- Monthly Budget
- Project Budget
- Product Launch Budget
- Campaign Budget

The appropriate planning period should depend on the nature of the expense and the level of uncertainty.

## Budget Allocation

Budget allocation should reflect:

- Strategic Priorities
- Expected Business Impact
- Cash Availability
- Operational Requirements
- Product Priorities
- Growth Plans
- Risk
- Timing

Budget allocation should not be based solely on historical spending.

## Budget Ownership

Each significant budget category should have an identifiable owner.

The budget owner is responsible for:

- Understanding the approved allocation
- Monitoring spending
- Identifying expected variances
- Requesting changes when required
- Avoiding unnecessary commitments
- Reporting material issues

Budget ownership does not necessarily mean the owner personally approves every payment.

## Budget Approval

Significant spending should follow an appropriate approval process.

Approval requirements may depend on:

- Amount
- Category
- Risk
- Contractual commitment
- Strategic importance
- Budget availability

No expense should be considered approved solely because funds appear to be available.

## Variance Management

A variance occurs when actual or expected spending differs materially from the approved budget.

Variances should be assessed according to:

- Amount
- Percentage
- Cause
- Timing
- Business Impact
- Cash Impact

Not every variance requires corrective action, but material or recurring variances should be investigated.

## Budget Changes

Budgets may require revision when significant assumptions change.

Examples include:

- Product Delays
- Cost Increases
- Supplier Changes
- Market Expansion
- New Product Development
- Unexpected Expenses
- Changes in Sales Expectations
- Major Strategic Decisions

Material changes should be documented and appropriately approved.

## Contingency

Where appropriate, SAB’A may maintain a contingency allocation for unexpected but legitimate business requirements.

Contingency funds should not be treated as unrestricted spending.

Use of contingency should be documented and reviewed.

## Capital Expenditure

Major purchases or investments should be distinguished from routine operating expenses where appropriate.

Examples may include:

- Equipment
- Technology Infrastructure
- Production Assets
- Major Website Development
- Other Long-Term Assets

Capital expenditure should be evaluated based on:

- Cost
- Expected Useful Life
- Business Value
- Cash Impact
- Risk
- Expected Return

Accounting treatment should follow the applicable accounting framework.

## Marketing Budget

Marketing budgets should be connected to the broader marketing strategy.

Spending may be evaluated against:

- Reach
- Qualified Traffic
- Engagement
- Customer Acquisition
- Conversion
- Revenue
- Retention
- Return on Marketing Investment

Marketing spend should not be increased simply to generate activity without meaningful business value.

## Product Launch Budget

Each significant product launch may have a dedicated budget covering:

- Development
- Samples
- Production
- Packaging
- Content
- Marketing
- Ecommerce
- Fulfillment
- Customer Experience

Launch budgets should account for the complete expected cost rather than only the visible marketing expense.

## Cash Consideration

Budget approval should consider actual cash requirements and payment timing.

A project may be affordable in total but still create short-term cash pressure.

Before significant commitments, SAB’A should consider:

- Payment Schedule
- Supplier Deposits
- Inventory Commitments
- Expected Revenue Timing
- Existing Obligations
- Available Cash

## Budget Discipline

SAB’A should avoid:

- Spending simply because a budget remains
- Artificially inflating budgets
- Hiding costs in unrelated categories
- Repeatedly exceeding budgets without review
- Treating budget availability as proof of necessity

Unused budget is not automatically a problem.

## Budget Review

Budget performance should be reviewed at an appropriate frequency.

Reviews may consider:

- Spending to Date
- Remaining Budget
- Material Variances
- Upcoming Commitments
- Cash Requirements
- Changes in Business Priorities

The review frequency should reflect the scale and financial complexity of SAB’A.

## Budget Records

Budget records should maintain a clear history of:

- Original Budget
- Approved Revisions
- Actual Spending
- Material Commitments
- Variances
- Explanations
- Final Outcome

Previous budgets should be retained where useful for planning, analysis, or historical reference.

## Relationship with Other Financial Documents

The Budget should work together with:

- Financial Framework
- Costing
- Pricing Model
- Cash Flow
- Financial Records

The Budget defines planned allocation, while these documents provide the broader financial context and actual financial record.

## Core Principle

> A budget is not a target for spending. It is a deliberate allocation of resources toward what SAB’A has decided matters.
