# Service Standards

## Purpose

Service Standards define how SAB’A provides customer service across relevant channels and situations.

The objective is to ensure that every customer interaction is handled with clarity, respect, consistency, and appropriate care.

## Service Principles

1. **Respect before Resolution**
2. **Clarity before Complexity**
3. **Understanding before Response**
4. **Ownership before Deflection**
5. **Accuracy before Speed**
6. **Humanity before Script**
7. **Resolution before Closure**
8. **Consistency across Channels**

## Service Character

SAB’A customer service should feel:

- Human
- Calm
- Respectful
- Clear
- Knowledgeable
- Responsive
- Considered
- Professional

Service should never feel:

- Aggressive
- Dismissive
- Robotic
- Defensive
- Manipulative
- Unnecessarily formal
- Overly scripted

## Customer Understanding

Before responding, the service representative should understand:

- What the customer needs
- What has happened
- What information is available
- What action is required
- Whether another team is involved

The goal is to address the actual need rather than respond only to the wording of the message.

## Communication

Customer communication should be:

- Clear
- Accurate
- Concise
- Relevant
- Respectful
- Appropriate to the situation

Avoid unnecessary jargon, vague promises, or information that may create confusion.

## Response Standards

Service responses should be provided within the response times established for the relevant channel and service level.

Where an immediate resolution is not possible, the customer should receive:

- Confirmation that the issue is understood
- Relevant information
- The next expected step
- A realistic timeframe where possible

Customers should not be left without meaningful communication when an issue remains unresolved.

## Accuracy

Customer service must not provide information that has not been verified.

This includes information related to:

- Products
- Fragrance
- Pricing
- Availability
- Orders
- Shipping
- Returns
- Exchanges
- Policies
- Delivery timelines

When information is uncertain, it should be verified before being communicated.

## Ownership

When SAB’A is responsible for an issue, the service interaction should demonstrate appropriate ownership.

Ownership means:

- Acknowledging the issue
- Taking the necessary action
- Coordinating internally when required
- Keeping the customer informed
- Following through until the appropriate resolution

The customer should not be unnecessarily transferred between teams.

## Problem Resolution

Customer issues should generally follow:

**Understand**  
→ **Verify**  
→ **Resolve**  
→ **Confirm**  
→ **Learn**

The appropriate resolution depends on the issue, applicable policy, and customer circumstances.

## Complaints

Complaints should be treated as meaningful customer feedback.

The response should:

- Remain calm
- Avoid defensiveness
- Acknowledge the concern
- Establish the facts
- Explain the available resolution
- Escalate when necessary

The objective is to resolve the issue while protecting trust.

## Difficult Interactions

When a customer is frustrated or dissatisfied, SAB’A should maintain professionalism.

Service representatives should:

- Avoid arguments
- Avoid blame
- Avoid sarcasm
- Avoid dismissive language
- Avoid unnecessary escalation

The customer's tone should not determine SAB’A's standard of conduct.

## Escalation

Issues should be escalated when they exceed the authority, knowledge, or responsibility of the initial service representative.

Examples may include:

- Serious product issues
- Repeated delivery failures
- Financial disputes
- Legal concerns
- Safety-related concerns
- Sensitive customer matters
- Significant service failures

Escalation should include enough context to prevent the customer from repeatedly explaining the same issue.

## Returns and Exchanges

Returns and exchanges should be handled according to the approved SAB’A policy.

Communication should clearly explain:

- Eligibility
- Required steps
- Relevant timelines
- Customer responsibilities
- Expected resolution

The service experience should remain respectful regardless of the outcome.

## Product Questions

Product questions should be answered using approved and accurate product information.

Where appropriate, service representatives may help customers understand:

- Product characteristics
- Fragrance profiles
- Usage
- Packaging
- Availability
- Relevant product differences

No unsupported claims should be made.

## Personalization

Personalization should be used when it improves relevance or demonstrates genuine attention.

It should not become intrusive or create an uncomfortable sense of surveillance.

## Service Across Channels

SAB’A may provide service through channels such as:

- Email
- WhatsApp
- Social Media
- Website
- Ecommerce
- Other approved customer communication channels

The format may change by channel, but the underlying service standard should remain consistent.

## Privacy and Customer Information

Customer information should be handled according to SAB’A privacy requirements and applicable laws.

Service personnel should only access or share information necessary for the relevant service activity.

## Service Records

Relevant interactions may be documented to support:

- Resolution
- Follow-up
- Customer history
- Internal coordination
- Issue analysis
- Continuous improvement

Records should be handled according to applicable privacy and data requirements.

## Service Quality

Service quality may be evaluated through:

- Response time
- Resolution time
- First-contact resolution
- Customer satisfaction
- Complaint frequency
- Escalation frequency
- Repeat issues
- Customer feedback

Metrics should support service improvement rather than encourage artificial speed or scripted behavior.

## Training

Anyone responsible for customer service should understand:

- SAB’A brand principles
- Customer experience philosophy
- Product knowledge
- Communication standards
- Relevant policies
- Escalation procedures
- Privacy requirements

Training should be updated when products, policies, or service processes materially change.

## Continuous Improvement

Service standards should evolve through:

- Customer feedback
- Repeated service issues
- Operational learning
- Product changes
- New communication channels
- Service performance data

Recurring problems should be addressed at their source whenever possible.

## Core Principle

> Good service is not simply answering the customer. It is making the customer feel understood, respected, and appropriately cared for.
