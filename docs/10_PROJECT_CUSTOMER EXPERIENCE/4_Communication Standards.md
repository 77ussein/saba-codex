# Communication Standards

## Purpose

Communication Standards define how SAB’A communicates with customers across all relevant channels and interactions.

The objective is to ensure that every communication is clear, accurate, human, consistent, and aligned with the character of SAB’A.

## Communication Principles

1. **Clarity over Complexity**
2. **Humanity over Automation**
3. **Accuracy over Assumption**
4. **Relevance over Repetition**
5. **Respect over Pressure**
6. **Meaning over Noise**
7. **Consistency over Imitation**
8. **Context before Response**

## Communication Character

SAB’A communication should feel:

- Intelligent
- Refined
- Human
- Calm
- Clear
- Considered
- Respectful
- Distinctive

Communication should avoid sounding:

- Robotic
- Aggressive
- Excessively promotional
- Artificially luxurious
- Overly familiar
- Dismissive
- Unnecessarily complicated

## Brand Voice

Customer communication should reflect the established SAB’A Brand Voice.

The tone may adapt according to context, but the underlying character should remain recognizable.

Different situations may require different levels of:

- Warmth
- Formality
- Detail
- Directness
- Urgency

Adaptation should not change the identity of SAB’A.

## Clarity

Customer-facing information should be easy to understand.

Important information should be:

- Direct
- Specific
- Accurate
- Appropriately detailed
- Easy to locate

Avoid unnecessary wording, vague statements, and language that creates uncertainty.

## Accuracy

Communication must be based on verified information.

This applies to:

- Product information
- Fragrance descriptions
- Pricing
- Availability
- Shipping
- Delivery
- Returns
- Exchanges
- Promotions
- Policies
- Customer orders

Unsupported claims or assumptions should not be communicated as facts.

## Written Communication

Written communication should be structured according to the situation.

Where appropriate, messages should include:

1. A clear opening
2. The relevant information
3. The required action or next step
4. Additional context only when useful
5. A clear closing

The customer should not have to interpret what SAB’A expects them to do.

## Response Style

Responses should be proportionate to the customer's need.

Simple questions should receive simple answers.

Complex situations may require:

- Additional explanation
- Context
- Step-by-step guidance
- Follow-up

The goal is not maximum detail.

The goal is sufficient clarity.

## Personalization

Personalization may be used when it improves the relevance of the interaction.

Examples may include:

- Using the customer's name where appropriate
- Referring to the relevant order
- Recognizing previous context
- Recommending relevant products

Personalization should remain natural and respectful.

## Automation

Automation may be used to improve:

- Speed
- Availability
- Accuracy
- Operational efficiency
- Consistency

Automated communication should still reflect SAB’A standards.

Automation should not create unnecessary repetition or make the customer feel that a human concern has been reduced to a process.

## Channel Adaptation

SAB’A may communicate through:

- Email
- WhatsApp
- Social Media
- Website
- Ecommerce
- SMS
- Other approved channels

Each channel may require different formatting and response behavior.

The underlying principles should remain consistent.

## Email

Email communication should be:

- Clear
- Structured
- Professional
- Appropriately concise

Subject lines should communicate the purpose of the message where relevant.

Important actions or information should be easy to identify.

## WhatsApp and Direct Messaging

Direct communication channels may allow a more conversational tone while maintaining SAB’A's standards.

Messages should remain:

- Clear
- Respectful
- Human
- Context-aware

Excessive promotional messaging should be avoided.

## Social Media Communication

Social media responses should reflect the public nature of the channel.

Responses should:

- Remain respectful
- Avoid unnecessary arguments
- Protect customer privacy
- Provide useful information
- Move sensitive matters to a private channel where appropriate

Public communication should never expose private customer information.

## Promotional Communication

Promotional communication should provide genuine value or relevance.

It should not rely excessively on:

- Artificial urgency
- Fear of missing out
- Misleading scarcity
- Exaggerated claims
- Constant discounting
- Emotional pressure

SAB’A should communicate reasons to discover rather than manufacture reasons to buy.

## Product Communication

Product communication should be grounded in approved product information.

Descriptions may communicate:

- Product identity
- Fragrance character
- Materials
- Craft
- Design
- Experience
- Intended use

Claims should never exceed what SAB’A can substantiate.

## Sensitive Communication

Sensitive matters should be handled with additional care.

Examples include:

- Complaints
- Product concerns
- Safety matters
- Financial disputes
- Privacy issues
- Legal matters
- Serious delivery problems

Communication should remain factual, respectful, and appropriately escalated.

## Response Time

Customer communications should be answered within the service levels established for the relevant channel.

When resolution requires additional time, the customer should receive an appropriate update rather than being left without context.

## Internal Communication

Internal communication supporting customer-facing activities should also be:

- Clear
- Accurate
- Documented where necessary
- Action-oriented

Internal ambiguity should not become customer-facing confusion.

## Translation and Localization

Where SAB’A communicates in multiple languages, translations should preserve:

- Meaning
- Tone
- Accuracy
- Cultural appropriateness
- Brand character

Translation should communicate the intended idea rather than mechanically reproduce wording.

## Prohibited Communication Practices

SAB’A communication should not intentionally use:

- False claims
- Misleading information
- Unsupported product benefits
- Artificial scarcity
- Manipulative pressure
- Offensive language
- Discriminatory language
- Customer shaming
- Unnecessary confrontation

## Communication Review

Important customer-facing communication should be reviewed when it:

- Introduces a new product
- Communicates a major policy
- Makes a significant claim
- Addresses a sensitive issue
- Represents a major campaign
- Creates a new recurring communication format

Review requirements should remain proportionate to the importance of the communication.

## Communication Quality

Communication quality may be evaluated through:

- Accuracy
- Clarity
- Relevance
- Response time
- Customer feedback
- Complaint patterns
- Conversion where applicable
- Engagement where applicable

Performance should not be measured through engagement or speed alone.

## Core Principle

> SAB’A should communicate in a way that respects the intelligence of the customer and leaves no unnecessary distance between the brand and the person experiencing it.
