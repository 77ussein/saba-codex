# Customer Journey
## Purpose
Customer Journey defines the stages and touchpoints through which a customer interacts with SAB’A, from initial discovery to long-term relationship.
The objective is to understand the customer experience as a connected journey rather than a series of isolated transactions.
## Journey Architecture
The SAB’A customer journey may be understood through:
**Discover**  
→ **Explore**  
→ **Understand**  
→ **Consider**  
→ **Purchase**  
→ **Receive**  
→ **Experience**  
→ **Return**  
→ **Recommend**
Not every customer will follow every stage in the same order.
## 1. Discover
The customer becomes aware of SAB’A for the first time.
Possible touchpoints include:
- Social Media
- Search
- Creators
- Advertising
- PR
- Partnerships
- Events
- Word of Mouth
- Website
The objective is not to force immediate conversion, but to create enough relevance and curiosity to encourage exploration.
## 2. Explore
The customer begins interacting with the world of SAB’A.
They may:
- Visit the website
- Explore products
- Read brand stories
- View visual content
- Explore fragrance information
- Discover the brand philosophy
- Follow SAB’A on relevant channels
The experience should make exploration natural and rewarding.
## 3. Understand
The customer develops a clearer understanding of:
- What SAB’A is
- What the products represent
- How fragrances differ
- What makes the brand distinctive
- Why a particular product may be relevant to them
Information should be clear without overwhelming the customer.
## 4. Consider
The customer evaluates whether to purchase.
Considerations may include:
- Product relevance
- Fragrance profile
- Product details
- Price
- Reviews or social proof where applicable
- Brand perception
- Availability
- Shipping
- Returns
SAB’A should support informed consideration rather than manufacture pressure.
## 5. Purchase
The customer completes the purchase.
The purchase experience should provide:
- Clear product information
- Transparent pricing
- Simple navigation
- Smooth checkout
- Appropriate payment options
- Clear shipping information
- Order confirmation
Unnecessary friction should be identified and reduced.
## 6. Receive
The customer receives the order.
This stage includes:
- Shipping communication
- Delivery
- Packaging
- Unboxing
- Product presentation
The transition from digital purchase to physical product should feel coherent with the SAB’A experience.
## 7. Experience
The customer begins using and experiencing the product.
Relevant touchpoints may include:
- Product presentation
- Fragrance discovery
- Product education
- Usage guidance
- Customer support
- Follow-up communication
The product experience should fulfill the expectations established before purchase.
## 8. Return
A satisfied customer may return to SAB’A for:
- Another purchase
- A different fragrance
- A new collection
- Product discovery
- Brand content
- New experiences
Retention should be built through relevance and relationship rather than constant promotion.
## 9. Recommend
A customer may voluntarily recommend SAB’A to others.
This may occur through:
- Word of mouth
- Reviews
- Social sharing
- Personal recommendations
- Community participation
- Creator or customer content
Advocacy should be earned through the quality of the product and experience.
## Customer Needs by Stage
| Stage | Primary Customer Need |
|---|---|
| Discover | Relevance |
| Explore | Curiosity |
| Understand | Clarity |
| Consider | Confidence |
| Purchase | Ease |
| Receive | Reliability |
| Experience | Satisfaction |
| Return | Continued relevance |
| Recommend | Trust |
## Touchpoint Consistency
The following should remain aligned throughout the journey:
- Brand Identity
- Product Information
- Visual Language
- Tone of Voice
- Pricing
- Customer Service
- Packaging
- Fulfillment
- After-Sales
A disconnect between touchpoints can weaken trust even when individual interactions are strong.
## Friction Points
Potential friction should be monitored across:
- Navigation
- Product discovery
- Product understanding
- Checkout
- Payment
- Shipping
- Delivery
- Returns
- Customer service
Friction should be reduced when it does not provide meaningful value or necessary control.
## Personalization
Personalization may be used where it creates genuine relevance.
Examples include:
- Product recommendations
- Relevant product education
- Purchase history
- Customer preferences
- Relevant communication
Personalization should remain respectful and should not feel intrusive.
## Journey Feedback
Customer feedback should be connected to the relevant stage of the journey.
Feedback may help identify:
- Confusion
- Friction
- Product issues
- Service issues
- Unmet expectations
- Opportunities for improvement
Significant patterns should be shared with the responsible function.
## Journey Measurement
Journey performance may be evaluated through:
- Qualified traffic
- Engagement
- Conversion
- Cart abandonment
- Order completion
- Delivery performance
- Customer satisfaction
- Repeat purchase
- Retention
- Customer lifetime value
- Referral or advocacy
Quantitative metrics should be considered alongside qualitative feedback.
## Continuous Improvement
The customer journey should be reviewed as SAB’A evolves.
Changes in:
- Products
- Channels
- Technology
- Operations
- Customer expectations
- Markets
may require updates to the journey.
## Core Principle
> The SAB’A customer journey should feel like one continuous relationship, not a collection of disconnected transactions.
