# Packaging Experience

## Purpose

Packaging Experience defines how SAB’A uses packaging as part of the customer experience rather than as a purely functional layer.

The objective is to ensure that packaging protects the product while also supporting discovery, presentation, coherence, and the overall SAB’A experience.

## Experience Principles

1. **Protection before Presentation**
2. **Purpose before Excess**
3. **Detail before Decoration**
4. **Coherence before Complexity**
5. **Experience without Waste**
6. **Quality without Unnecessary Excess**
7. **Discovery without Instruction**
8. **Consistency with the SAB’A Identity**

## Role of Packaging

Packaging serves several functions:

- Protect the product
- Preserve product condition
- Present the product
- Communicate essential information
- Support the brand identity
- Create a meaningful unboxing experience

Packaging should perform these functions without allowing presentation to compromise practicality.

## Packaging Journey

The packaging experience may be understood through:

**Arrival**  
→ **First Impression**  
→ **Opening**  
→ **Discovery**  
→ **Product Reveal**  
→ **Product Use**

Each stage should feel connected and intentional.

## First Impression

The first interaction with the package should establish a sense of:

- Care
- Quality
- Restraint
- Identity
- Anticipation

The experience should create curiosity without relying on excessive visual or verbal messaging.

## Unboxing

Unboxing should feel natural and considered.

Where appropriate, the experience may include:

- Outer packaging
- Protective layers
- Brand elements
- Product presentation
- Inserts
- Product information

Each element should have a clear purpose.

Unnecessary layers should be avoided when they do not meaningfully improve protection or experience.

## Product Reveal

The product should remain the central focus of the experience.

Packaging should guide attention toward the product rather than compete with it.

The reveal may use:

- Sequence
- Material contrast
- Typography
- Space
- Structure
- Tactile details

These elements should remain consistent with approved packaging standards.

## Information

Information included within packaging should be:

- Relevant
- Accurate
- Clear
- Appropriately concise

Possible information may include:

- Product details
- Usage guidance
- Care instructions
- Brand information
- Safety information where required
- Contact or support information

Packaging should not become overloaded with unnecessary information.

## Materials

Packaging materials should be selected according to:

- Product protection
- Quality
- Appearance
- Tactile experience
- Durability
- Operational requirements
- Sustainability considerations where applicable

Material choices should align with approved packaging specifications.

## Visual Consistency

Packaging should remain consistent with:

- Brand Identity
- Color System
- Typography
- Imagery
- Product Architecture
- Packaging Architecture

Different products may have distinct expressions while remaining recognizably SAB’A.

## Tactile Experience

Where relevant, physical interaction with packaging may contribute to the experience through:

- Texture
- Weight
- Opening mechanism
- Material quality
- Finishing
- Structural detail

Tactile elements should serve the experience rather than exist purely as decoration.

## Sustainability

Packaging decisions should consider environmental impact where practical.

Considerations may include:

- Material efficiency
- Recyclability
- Reusability
- Avoidance of unnecessary components
- Responsible sourcing
- Shipping efficiency

Sustainability claims must remain accurate and supportable.

## Protection

Packaging must adequately protect products throughout:

- Storage
- Handling
- Fulfillment
- Transportation
- Delivery

A visually strong package that fails to protect the product does not meet the SAB’A standard.

## Personalization

Where appropriate, packaging may support personalized experiences.

Examples may include:

- Customer notes
- Occasion-specific elements
- Approved inserts
- Gift presentation

Personalization should remain refined and should not compromise consistency or operational reliability.

## Inserts and Supporting Materials

Inserts should only be included when they provide meaningful value.

Possible purposes include:

- Product education
- Usage guidance
- Care information
- Brand discovery
- Customer support
- Relevant recommendations

Every insert should have a defined purpose.

## Packaging and Customer Expectations

Packaging should deliver on the expectations created by SAB’A before purchase.

The relationship should remain coherent:

**Brand Promise**  
→ **Product Presentation**  
→ **Physical Interaction**  
→ **Product Experience**

Packaging should not create expectations that the product itself cannot support.

## Damaged Packaging

Packaging damage should be assessed according to its effect on:

- Product protection
- Product condition
- Presentation
- Customer experience

Significant damage should be handled through the relevant fulfillment and customer service procedures.

## Continuous Improvement

Packaging experience should be reviewed through:

- Customer feedback
- Damage rates
- Unboxing observations
- Operational performance
- Material performance
- Cost
- Sustainability considerations

Improvements should preserve the core SAB’A experience while addressing genuine opportunities.

## Core Principle

> Packaging should not simply contain the product. It should create the space in which the product is discovered.
