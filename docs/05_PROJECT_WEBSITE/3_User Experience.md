# User Experience

## Purpose

The SAB’A website is designed around the experience of the person entering the house.

User Experience is not limited to usability, navigation, or conversion.

It defines how the visitor:

- Feels
- Understands
- Explores
- Trusts
- Desires
- Interacts
- Purchases
- Remembers SAB’A

The experience must transform a digital visit into a meaningful encounter with the house.

The governing sequence is:

**Identity → Curiosity → Emotion → Trust → Desire → Experience → Purchase → Memory**

This sequence guides the experience without forcing every visitor through the exact same path.

---

# Experience Philosophy

## The Visitor Is Entering a World

The website should never feel like a collection of screens designed to move products.

The visitor should feel that they are entering a distinct world with its own:

- Character
- Rhythm
- Language
- Atmosphere
- Standards
- Meaning

Every interaction should reinforce this sense of place.

---

# Emotion Before Transaction

The first objective is not to obtain the customer's money.

The first objective is to earn the customer's attention.

Then:

**Attention → Curiosity → Understanding → Appreciation → Trust → Desire → Purchase**

The commercial action should feel like the natural conclusion of an experience rather than the purpose of every interaction.

The website must never pressure the visitor into purchasing before sufficient meaning and confidence have been established.

---

# First Impression

The first moments of the experience carry disproportionate importance.

The visitor should immediately understand that SAB’A is:

- Distinct
- Refined
- Rooted
- Contemporary
- Intentional

The opening experience should create curiosity without explaining everything immediately.

The visitor should feel:

**“There is something here worth discovering.”**

The first interaction should therefore prioritise:

- Atmosphere
- Identity
- Visual confidence
- Clarity
- Controlled pacing

It should avoid:

- Immediate visual clutter
- Excessive promotional messaging
- Aggressive calls to action
- Too many choices
- Generic e-commerce patterns

---

# Progressive Revelation

SAB’A should reveal itself progressively.

Not every piece of information needs to be presented immediately.

The experience should move from:

**Introduction → Discovery → Depth**

The visitor receives enough information to understand the current moment, while deeper layers remain available for those who want to explore.

This creates curiosity without creating confusion.

Progressive revelation may apply to:

- Brand stories
- Heritage
- Product information
- Collections
- Visual narratives
- Supporting content
- Product details

The visitor should never feel overwhelmed by information.

---

# Experience Rhythm

The website should have rhythm.

Not every section should have the same:

- Density
- Speed
- Scale
- Visual intensity
- Amount of information

Moments of discovery should alternate with moments of silence.

Moments of visual intensity should be balanced with restraint.

Moments of information should be balanced with space.

The experience should feel composed rather than continuous noise.

---

# Attention Management

The website must respect the visitor's attention.

Every element competes for attention.

Therefore:

- Primary elements must be obvious.
- Secondary elements must remain subordinate.
- Supporting information must not compete with the main message.
- Motion must not distract from meaning.
- Calls to action must not dominate every section.

The interface should guide attention through hierarchy rather than force.

---

# User Intent

Not every visitor arrives with the same intention.

The experience must support multiple visitor states.

### 1. The Curious Visitor

They do not know SAB’A yet.

They need:

- Atmosphere
- Identity
- Context
- Discovery
- A reason to continue

### 2. The Explorer

They are interested in the world of SAB’A.

They need:

- Collections
- Stories
- Heritage
- Product discovery
- Deeper information

### 3. The Intentional Shopper

They already know what they want.

They need:

- Fast access
- Clear product information
- Availability
- Price
- Purchase path
- Confidence

### 4. The Returning Customer

They already have a relationship with SAB’A.

They need:

- Recognition
- Convenience
- Order access
- Product information
- New discoveries
- Continuity

The website must support these different intentions without creating separate personalities.

---

# Discovery

Discovery should feel rewarding.

The visitor should be encouraged to explore because each step reveals something meaningful.

Discovery may occur through:

- Collections
- Product relationships
- Editorial content
- Heritage
- Visual storytelling
- Recommendations
- Related creations
- Experiences

Recommendations must feel curated rather than algorithmically aggressive.

The website should suggest:

**“You may appreciate this.”**

Not:

**“Buy this too.”**

---

# Product Experience

A product should never appear disconnected from the world that created it.

The product experience should answer, progressively:

- What is this?
- What makes it distinctive?
- What does it feel like?
- Why does it belong to SAB’A?
- Why should I care?
- Why should I choose it?
- How do I own it?

The product page should move the visitor from:

**Observation → Understanding → Appreciation → Desire → Decision**

Product information should be available without destroying the emotional experience.

Detailed product requirements are defined in:

**5_Product Pages.md**

---

# Trust

Luxury requires trust.

The website must communicate confidence without making exaggerated claims.

Trust is built through:

- Clear information
- Consistent presentation
- Authentic storytelling
- Product transparency
- Professional service
- Secure transactions
- Clear policies
- Reliable performance
- Consistent details

The visitor should never have to question whether the experience is legitimate, reliable, or professionally managed.

---

# Interaction Principles

Interactions should feel natural and deliberate.

Every interaction must answer:

**“What just happened?”**

and:

**“What can I do next?”**

The interface should provide appropriate feedback for:

- Clicks
- Taps
- Hover
- Focus
- Selection
- Loading
- Success
- Errors
- Cart changes
- Form submission

The visitor should never feel lost inside an interaction.

---

# Friction

Not all friction is bad.

Some friction can create:

- Anticipation
- Deliberation
- Discovery
- Emotional depth

But functional friction should be minimised.

### Remove friction from:

- Navigation
- Search
- Product discovery
- Checkout
- Forms
- Payment
- Essential information

### Preserve intentional friction where it creates:

- Atmosphere
- Storytelling
- Anticipation
- Meaningful discovery

The distinction is:

**Functional friction should disappear.  
Experiential pacing should remain.**

---

# Navigation Experience

Navigation should allow the visitor to move confidently without constantly demanding attention.

The visitor should always understand:

- Where they are
- Where they came from
- Where they can go
- How to return
- What the primary action is

Navigation should remain consistent throughout the experience.

Detailed navigation rules are defined in:

**6_Navigation.md**

---

# Search Experience

Search should serve visitors with clear intent.

A visitor searching for a known product should be able to reach it efficiently.

Search should provide:

- Clear input
- Relevant results
- Useful filtering where necessary
- Helpful empty states
- Fast feedback
- Mobile usability

Search should not feel like a separate technical system.

It should belong to the SAB’A environment.

---

# Mobile Experience

Mobile is a primary experience, not an afterthought.

The mobile visitor must receive the same:

- Brand character
- Emotional quality
- Clarity
- Product confidence
- Navigation logic
- Standard of detail

The experience may be structurally different from desktop, but it must remain recognisably SAB’A.

Detailed requirements are defined in:

**7_Mobile Experience.md**

---

# Motion and Pacing

Motion should support the emotional rhythm of the website.

It may be used to:

- Reveal
- Transition
- Guide
- Emphasise
- Connect
- Create continuity

Motion should never become a source of cognitive burden.

Visitors should be able to navigate and purchase without waiting for decorative animations.

Motion must respect:

- Performance
- Accessibility
- User control
- Device capability

---

# Loading Experience

Loading is part of the experience.

A premium website must not leave visitors staring at blank screens without context.

Loading states should:

- Communicate progress where appropriate
- Preserve visual continuity
- Avoid unnecessary waiting
- Maintain brand character
- Transition smoothly into the content

Performance should be treated as part of hospitality.

The visitor should feel welcomed, not made to wait.

---

# Error Experience

Errors are moments of truth.

When something fails, the website must remain calm and respectful.

Error states should:

- Explain what happened
- Avoid technical jargon
- Provide a clear next action
- Preserve the visual language
- Never blame the visitor

An error should feel like a temporary interruption, not a collapse of the experience.

---

# Transaction Experience

The purchase journey should be:

**Clear → Calm → Confident → Efficient**

The visitor should always know:

- What they are purchasing
- How much it costs
- What happens next
- What information is required
- How the transaction is completed

The transaction environment should preserve the SAB’A standard without introducing unnecessary visual storytelling that interferes with completion.

The visitor should never feel that the brand disappeared when they reached checkout.

---

# Checkout

Checkout should minimise cognitive load.

The visitor should not be forced through unnecessary steps.

The system should provide:

- Clear order summary
- Transparent pricing
- Clear delivery information
- Appropriate payment options
- Strong validation
- Error recovery
- Confirmation

The final purchase moment should feel like a continuation of the relationship.

Not an abrupt transition into a generic commerce platform.

---

# Post-Purchase Experience

The relationship with the customer does not end at payment.

After purchase, SAB’A should continue to provide value.

This may include:

- Order confirmation
- Delivery information
- Product care
- Ownership guidance
- Product education
- Related experiences
- Future discovery

The customer should feel:

**“I have entered into a relationship with this house.”**

Not:

**“I completed a transaction with this store.”**

---

# Memory

The final stage of the experience is memory.

A successful digital experience should leave the visitor with something beyond a completed transaction.

They should remember:

- The atmosphere
- The story
- The product
- The feeling
- The distinctiveness of SAB’A

The objective is not merely:

**“Did the customer buy?”**

It is also:

**“What will the customer remember?”**

---

# Emotional Continuity

Every stage must connect to the next.

The experience should feel like:

**One Journey**

rather than:

**A Collection of Pages**

The transition between:

- Homepage
- Brand
- Collection
- Product
- Cart
- Checkout
- Post-purchase

must preserve the same underlying character.

---

# Personalisation

Personalisation may be introduced where it creates genuine value.

It should never make the experience feel invasive or overly commercial.

Useful personalisation may include:

- Remembering preferences
- Relevant recommendations
- Returning customer recognition
- Recently viewed products
- Order-related information

Personalisation should feel like hospitality.

Not surveillance.

---

# Accessibility

A refined experience must remain accessible.

Accessibility should be considered throughout the experience, including:

- Typography
- Contrast
- Keyboard interaction
- Touch targets
- Screen readers
- Motion sensitivity
- Alternative text
- Error messaging
- Focus states

Accessibility is not separate from luxury.

It is part of the standard of care provided to the visitor.

---

# Experience Quality Standard

Every major experience should be evaluated against five dimensions:

### 1. Clarity

Does the visitor understand what is happening?

### 2. Emotion

Does the experience create the intended feeling?

### 3. Confidence

Does the visitor trust SAB’A?

### 4. Desire

Does the experience increase appreciation and desire naturally?

### 5. Ease

Can the visitor accomplish their goal without unnecessary friction?

A visually beautiful experience that fails any of these dimensions requires reconsideration.

---

# Experience Test

Before approving a major experience, ask:

1. **What should the visitor feel here?**
2. **What should the visitor understand here?**
3. **What should the visitor be able to do here?**
4. **Why does this interaction exist?**
5. **Does it strengthen the SAB’A world?**
6. **Does it create unnecessary friction?**
7. **Does it preserve luxury through restraint?**
8. **Would the experience still feel correct without the visual effects?**

If the answer depends entirely on visual effects, the underlying experience is not strong enough.

---

# Core Standard

The SAB’A experience should never force the visitor to choose between beauty and usability.

It should provide both.

It should feel:

**Beautiful without being difficult.**

**Luxurious without being excessive.**

**Emotional without being confusing.**

**Immersive without being slow.**

**Commercial without feeling commercialised.**

**Rooted in heritage without becoming historical decoration.**

---

# Core Statement

SAB’A does not simply want visitors to browse a website.

It wants them to enter a world.

To become curious.

To discover.

To understand.

To appreciate.

To trust.

To desire.

To experience.

To purchase.

And ultimately:

**To remember.**

The website succeeds when the visitor leaves with more than a product in their cart.

They leave with an impression of SAB’A.
