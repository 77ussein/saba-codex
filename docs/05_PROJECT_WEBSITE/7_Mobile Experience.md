# Mobile Experience

## Purpose

The mobile experience is not a reduced version of the SAB’A website.

It is a complete expression of the house, designed specifically for the way people discover, explore, purchase, and interact through a smaller screen.

The objective is to preserve:

- Luxury
- Atmosphere
- Clarity
- Emotional depth
- Performance
- Ease
- Brand consistency

without sacrificing usability.

The governing principle is:

**Small Screen. Full Experience.**

---

# Mobile Philosophy

SAB’A must feel like SAB’A whether the visitor is using:

- A phone
- A tablet
- A desktop
- A large display

The expression may change.

The identity does not.

Mobile design should therefore adapt the experience rather than simply shrink the desktop layout.

---

# Mobile as a Primary Experience

Mobile must be treated as a first-class environment.

Every important journey must be designed and tested specifically for mobile, including:

- Brand discovery
- Collection exploration
- Product discovery
- Product evaluation
- Purchase
- Account management
- Loyalty
- Customer care
- AI assistance

The mobile experience must never depend on the visitor having access to a larger screen.

---

# First Impression

The first screen on mobile carries significant responsibility.

It should immediately communicate:

- SAB’A
- Atmosphere
- Distinction
- Purpose

The opening should not be overloaded.

The visitor should understand where they are without being presented with too many competing elements.

The first impression should feel:

**Quiet. Intentional. Premium.**

---

# Mobile Navigation

Mobile navigation must remain extremely clear.

Primary access should provide convenient entry to:

- Collections
- Creations
- The House
- Search
- Account
- Bag

Secondary destinations may remain within the navigation system without overwhelming the primary interface.

The mobile menu should feel spacious and deliberate rather than compressed.

Detailed navigation principles are defined in:

**6_Navigation.md**

---

# Touch First

Mobile interaction is fundamentally touch-based.

All interactive elements must provide comfortable touch targets.

Buttons and controls should be:

- Easy to tap
- Clearly separated
- Visually understandable
- Resistant to accidental activation

The interface should never require precision that is unrealistic on a phone.

---

# One-Handed Use

Important actions should remain accessible during natural one-handed use where practical.

This is especially important for:

- Navigation
- Search
- Product selection
- Add to Bag
- Bag access
- Checkout
- Account access

The experience should minimise unnecessary reaching, repeated scrolling, or precision interaction.

---

# Typography

Typography must remain elegant while being highly readable.

Mobile typography should prioritise:

- Legibility
- Appropriate scale
- Comfortable line height
- Clear hierarchy
- Short readable sections

Large display typography may be used, but it must never become so large that it overwhelms the screen.

Luxury should come from proportion and composition, not simply oversized text.

---

# Content Density

Mobile screens require stronger content discipline.

Long information should be structured through:

- Short paragraphs
- Sections
- Progressive disclosure
- Accordions where appropriate
- Clear hierarchy
- Visual pauses

The visitor should never encounter a wall of text without meaningful structure.

The content itself remains complete.

The presentation becomes more focused.

---

# Images

Images are central to the SAB’A experience.

On mobile, they must retain:

- Detail
- Materiality
- Atmosphere
- Product accuracy

Images should be optimised without visibly compromising quality.

Cropping must be intentional.

The mobile crop should not accidentally remove the most important visual information.

---

# Product Photography

Product images must remain immediately recognisable.

The visitor should be able to understand:

- Shape
- Scale
- Material
- Packaging
- Detail

without needing to zoom excessively.

Where zoom is useful, it should be smooth and intuitive.

---

# Cinematic Content

Video and cinematic sequences may be used to establish atmosphere.

However, mobile video must be:

- Fast to load
- Appropriately compressed
- Responsive
- Muted by default where necessary
- Respectful of data usage
- Respectful of motion preferences

Video should enhance the experience rather than delay it.

If the cinematic experience does not load immediately, the visitor should still receive a complete and beautiful experience.

---

# Motion

Motion should be subtle and purposeful.

It may communicate:

- Transition
- Hierarchy
- Product reveal
- Continuity
- Interaction feedback

It must never create:

- Confusion
- Delay
- Motion sickness
- Accidental interaction
- Excessive scrolling

The mobile experience should feel alive, not busy.

---

# Performance

Performance is part of luxury.

A beautiful page that takes too long to load is not a premium experience.

The website should prioritise:

- Fast initial rendering
- Optimised images
- Efficient video
- Minimal layout shift
- Responsive interaction
- Efficient code
- Progressive loading

The visitor should feel that SAB’A responds immediately.

---

# Loading Experience

Loading states should remain consistent with the brand.

Where loading is unavoidable, the experience should provide calm and intentional feedback.

Avoid generic loading experiences that feel disconnected from SAB’A.

However, visual elegance must never become an excuse for hiding slow performance.

The goal is to reduce waiting, not decorate it.

---

# Product Pages on Mobile

Product pages must retain the full product experience.

The visitor should be able to access:

- Product identity
- Images
- Story
- Sensory information
- Product details
- Price
- Options
- Availability
- Purchase action
- Shipping information
- Returns
- Related products

without unnecessary navigation.

The mobile product page should maintain the same emotional sequence as desktop:

**Identity → Emotion → Understanding → Trust → Desire → Purchase**

---

# Mobile Purchase Action

The primary purchase action must remain easy to access.

Where appropriate, a contextual sticky purchase area may be used.

It should provide:

- Product name or essential context
- Price where appropriate
- Primary action
- Selected option where relevant

It must not permanently cover important content.

The rule is:

**Accessible, not intrusive.**

---

# Product Options

Options such as:

- Size
- Volume
- Variant
- Format

must be easy to select with touch.

The selected option must always be obvious.

The visitor should not need to open multiple layers of controls to understand what they are buying.

---

# Mobile Checkout

Checkout should be especially focused on mobile.

The visitor should be able to complete the transaction with minimal friction.

Priorities include:

- Clear order summary
- Simple information entry
- Easy address selection
- Appropriate payment methods
- Clear delivery information
- Error prevention
- Fast confirmation

Where supported, mobile-native payment experiences should be considered.

---

# Customer Account

The mobile customer account should function as the customer's private space within SAB’A.

It may include:

- Profile
- Orders
- Order tracking
- Wishlist
- Points
- Loyalty status
- Rewards
- Coupons
- Purchase history
- Communication preferences
- Newsletter preferences
- Customer care
- AI assistance

The interface should remain simple despite the depth of functionality.

---

# Loyalty on Mobile

The loyalty experience should be immediately understandable.

The customer should be able to see:

- Current points
- Progress
- Rewards
- Available coupons
- Purchase-related benefits
- Membership status

The experience should feel rewarding without turning the account into a conventional points dashboard.

---

# Digital Loyalty Card

The core loyalty experience should work within the SAB’A account first.

Future integrations may include:

- Apple Wallet
- Google Wallet
- Samsung Wallet

These integrations should be treated as extensions of the loyalty system rather than prerequisites for launch.

The website should remain fully functional without them.

---

# Wishlist

Wishlist must be simple to use on mobile.

Customers should be able to:

- Save a product
- Remove a product
- Return to saved products
- Understand availability

The wishlist should support discovery and future purchasing without aggressive reminders.

---

# Search on Mobile

Search must be easy to access.

The search experience should provide:

- Immediate input
- Useful suggestions
- Product results
- Collection results
- Relevant content
- Clear no-result states

Search should reduce effort rather than create another navigation layer.

---

# Customer Care

Mobile users should have easy access to support.

Relevant channels may include:

- WhatsApp
- Customer care email
- AI assistance
- Help content

WhatsApp may serve as the fast communication channel.

Email may handle more detailed cases, complaints, and situations requiring structured follow-up.

The visitor should always understand which channel is appropriate.

---

# SAB’A AI

SAB’A AI may be accessible within the mobile website.

It can assist with:

- Product discovery
- Product questions
- Brand information
- Frequently asked questions
- Order guidance
- Customer care routing

AI should remain consistent with the SAB’A voice.

When a situation requires human judgement, the system should allow escalation to human customer care.

---

# Email and Mobile Continuity

Mobile interactions should connect naturally with email communication.

For example:

A customer purchases through mobile.

They may then receive:

**Order Confirmation → Receipt → Shipping Update → Delivery Confirmation**

through the transactional email system.

Marketing and editorial communication may separately include:

- New creations
- Stories
- Launches
- Selected offers
- Editorial content

The customer should experience one coherent SAB’A relationship across channels.

---

# Social Media Continuity

Mobile visitors frequently arrive through social platforms.

The experience should therefore support smooth movement between:

**Instagram → SAB’A Website**

**TikTok → SAB’A Website**

**X → SAB’A Website**

Campaign links should lead directly to relevant destinations whenever possible.

A visitor arriving from a social post should not have to search for the product or story they were promised.

---

# Mobile Campaigns

Campaigns and competitions promoted through social channels should have mobile-optimised landing experiences.

This is particularly important for:

- X competitions
- Launch announcements
- Limited campaigns
- Seasonal moments
- New collection releases

The visitor should be able to move from campaign discovery to meaningful action with minimal friction.

---

# Forms

Forms must be short and intentional.

Avoid asking for information that is not necessary.

Inputs should provide:

- Clear labels
- Appropriate keyboards
- Helpful validation
- Understandable errors
- Easy correction

The customer should never have to fight the interface to submit basic information.

---

# Error States

Errors must remain human and clear.

Avoid technical language.

Instead of exposing system terminology, explain:

- What happened
- What the customer can do
- What happens next

Errors should never feel like punishment.

---

# Accessibility

Mobile accessibility is mandatory.

The experience must consider:

- Text size
- Contrast
- Touch targets
- Screen readers
- Keyboard and assistive technologies where applicable
- Reduced motion
- Focus states
- Alternative text
- Clear labels

Luxury is not exclusive to users without accessibility needs.

---

# Device Adaptation

The experience must work across a broad range of:

- Screen sizes
- Aspect ratios
- Operating systems
- Browsers
- Device capabilities

The design should adapt fluidly.

It should not depend on one specific device model.

---

# Orientation

The experience should remain functional when device orientation changes where appropriate.

Important content and actions should not become inaccessible because the screen changes shape.

---

# Mobile Footer

The footer should provide access to important supporting destinations without becoming excessively long.

It may include:

- Customer Care
- Contact
- Policies
- Shipping
- Returns
- Social channels
- Newsletter
- Legal information

The footer should remain structured and easy to scan.

---

# Mobile Content Quality Test

Before approving a mobile page, ask:

1. **Does the first screen immediately feel like SAB’A?**
2. **Can the visitor understand where they are?**
3. **Can they navigate comfortably with touch?**
4. **Can they find a product quickly?**
5. **Can they understand the product without a desktop?**
6. **Can they purchase without unnecessary friction?**
7. **Are images and videos beautiful without slowing the experience?**
8. **Are account, loyalty, and rewards easy to access?**
9. **Can the customer reach support quickly?**
10. **Does AI assistance remain useful without becoming intrusive?**
11. **Does the experience remain accessible?**
12. **Does the site feel premium even under real mobile conditions?**

---

# What Mobile Must Never Become

The SAB’A mobile experience must never feel like:

- A compressed desktop website
- A generic mobile storefront
- A collection of oversized animations
- A slow cinematic experiment
- A cluttered menu
- An endless scrolling page
- A difficult checkout
- A dashboard disguised as a luxury experience

Mobile must remain beautiful **because it is disciplined**, not because it contains more effects.

---

# Core Standard

The mobile experience should preserve the essence of SAB’A under constraint.

Less screen.

Less space.

Less attention.

Yet the same:

**Identity → Emotion → Trust → Desire → Experience → Purchase**

The limitations of mobile should sharpen the design rather than weaken it.

---

# Core Statement

SAB’A mobile is not the smaller version of the house.

It is another entrance to the same house.

It must load quickly.

Respond immediately.

Feel effortless.

Look refined.

Communicate clearly.

And preserve the emotional character of SAB’A from the first touch to the final purchase.

**The objective is simple:**

**Make the smallest screen feel worthy of the house.**
