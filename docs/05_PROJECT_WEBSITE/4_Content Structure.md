# Content Structure

## Purpose

The content of SAB’A is not simply information placed inside a website.

It is one of the primary tools through which the house communicates its identity, heritage, products, values, and worldview.

Content must therefore be structured as part of the experience.

Its role is to:

- Establish identity
- Create curiosity
- Build emotional connection
- Explain meaning
- Build trust
- Create desire
- Support decision-making
- Complete the purchase
- Extend the relationship after purchase

The governing content sequence is:

**Identity → Curiosity → Emotion → Trust → Desire → Decision → Purchase → Memory**

Content should support this progression without forcing every visitor through the same narrative.

---

# Content Philosophy

## Content Is Part of the House

Every piece of content belongs to the same world as the product, packaging, photography, interface, and physical experience.

The visitor should feel that SAB’A is speaking with one voice across every touchpoint.

Content must therefore feel:

- Authentic
- Refined
- Confident
- Clear
- Intelligent
- Emotional
- Rooted
- Contemporary

It must never feel like generic luxury marketing.

---

# Meaning Before Marketing

SAB’A should not begin with:

**“Buy this.”**

It should begin with:

**“Understand what this is.”**

Then:

**“Understand why it matters.”**

Then:

**“Experience its character.”**

Then:

**“Decide whether it belongs to you.”**

Commercial language should emerge naturally from meaning.

The website should create desire through understanding and appreciation rather than aggressive persuasion.

---

# The Content Hierarchy

Content should be revealed progressively.

The preferred hierarchy is:

### Level 1 — Presence

The visitor immediately understands:

- Where they are
- What SAB’A represents
- What is important in this moment

### Level 2 — Context

The visitor receives enough information to understand the subject.

### Level 3 — Meaning

The content explains why the subject matters.

### Level 4 — Depth

Additional information is available for visitors who want to explore further.

### Level 5 — Action

The visitor receives a clear next step when appropriate.

This creates depth without overwhelming the visitor.

---

# Content Types

The SAB’A website should distinguish between different types of content.

## 1. Brand Content

Communicates:

- Identity
- Heritage
- Philosophy
- Worldview
- Character
- Standards

Its role is to build meaning and recognition.

---

## 2. Heritage Content

Communicates the cultural and historical foundations that influence SAB’A.

Heritage content should be:

- Accurate
- Respectful
- Relevant
- Well contextualised
- Contemporary in presentation

Heritage should not become decoration.

It should explain the foundation from which SAB’A emerges.

---

## 3. Collection Content

Communicates the character and logic of a collection.

It should help visitors understand:

- Why the collection exists
- What connects its creations
- What distinguishes it
- What experience it represents

A collection should feel like a coherent chapter of the SAB’A world rather than a product category.

---

## 4. Product Content

Communicates the individual creation.

It should progressively answer:

- What is it?
- What does it contain?
- What does it feel like?
- What makes it distinctive?
- What inspired it?
- How is it experienced?
- Why does it belong to SAB’A?

Product content must balance emotional storytelling with practical information.

---

## 5. Experience Content

Communicates the lived relationship with SAB’A.

This may include:

- Ritual
- Usage
- Product care
- Presentation
- Ownership
- Sensory experience
- Cultural context

Its purpose is to help the visitor imagine the product beyond the screen.

---

## 6. Commercial Content

Communicates information required to make a purchase.

This includes:

- Price
- Availability
- Size
- Variants
- Shipping
- Returns
- Payment
- Delivery information

Commercial content must be clear and transparent.

It should never hide essential information behind luxury language.

---

## 7. Supporting Content

Includes functional information such as:

- FAQs
- Customer service
- Policies
- Contact information
- Account information
- Order information

Supporting content must remain at the same standard as the rest of the house.

---

# Content Architecture by Intent

Content should respond to visitor intent rather than forcing a single narrative.

### Visitor Who Is Discovering SAB’A

Prioritise:

**Identity → Atmosphere → Heritage → Curiosity**

### Visitor Exploring the House

Prioritise:

**World → Collections → Stories → Products**

### Visitor Evaluating a Product

Prioritise:

**Product → Character → Information → Trust → Decision**

### Visitor Ready to Purchase

Prioritise:

**Clarity → Confidence → Transaction**

### Existing Customer

Prioritise:

**Recognition → Convenience → Ownership → Continuity**

The content architecture must support all of these states.

---

# Progressive Revelation

SAB’A should not explain everything at once.

Content should unfold in layers.

A typical progression may be:

**Statement → Image → Short Context → Deeper Story → Detailed Information**

The visitor should be able to stop when they have enough information.

Those who want depth should always have somewhere meaningful to go.

This principle prevents long walls of text while preserving intellectual depth.

---

# Writing Principles

The writing should feel like SAB’A.

It should be:

### Precise

Say what needs to be said.

Avoid unnecessary words.

### Confident

Do not apologise for the brand or over-explain its value.

### Emotional

Allow the reader to feel the meaning behind the product.

### Intelligent

Respect the visitor's ability to understand complexity.

### Restrained

Avoid exaggerated claims and excessive adjectives.

### Human

The language should feel authored, not generated from a marketing template.

---

# What the Writing Should Avoid

Avoid language that feels:

- Generic
- Overly promotional
- Aggressive
- Repetitive
- Artificially luxurious
- Excessively poetic
- Historically exaggerated
- Filled with empty superlatives

Avoid repeatedly using words such as:

- Ultimate
- Unparalleled
- Extraordinary
- Unmatched
- Exquisite
- World-class

unless a specific context genuinely justifies them.

Luxury is stronger when it does not constantly announce itself.

---

# Voice and Tone

The SAB’A voice should communicate:

**Quiet Confidence.**

It does not need to shout to establish authority.

The tone may change according to context while preserving the same underlying character.

### Brand Story

More emotional and atmospheric.

### Heritage

More informative, respectful, and grounded.

### Product

Sensory, precise, and informative.

### Commerce

Clear, direct, and reassuring.

### Customer Service

Warm, professional, and human.

The voice changes according to purpose.

The identity does not.

---

# Heritage Storytelling

SAB’A's connection to Sabaean heritage must be communicated with depth and responsibility.

Heritage should answer:

- What is the connection?
- Why does it matter?
- How does it influence SAB’A?
- What principles have been carried forward?
- How are those principles expressed today?

The goal is not to reproduce the past.

The goal is to demonstrate how the past informs the present.

The visitor should leave with the feeling:

**Ancient foundation. Contemporary expression.**

---

# Cultural Integrity

Heritage content must not make unsupported historical claims.

When historical information is presented, it should be:

- Researched
- Contextualised
- Respectful
- Clearly distinguished from brand interpretation

SAB’A may interpret its heritage creatively, but interpretation should not be presented as historical fact.

This distinction protects both the credibility of the house and the integrity of the culture it represents.

---

# Product Storytelling

A product story should not exist simply to make the product sound expensive.

It should help the visitor understand the creation.

A strong product narrative can connect:

**Origin → Inspiration → Composition → Character → Experience → Ownership**

Not every product requires every layer.

The depth should match the importance and complexity of the creation.

---

# Sensory Language

Because SAB’A operates in a sensory category, language should help the visitor imagine the experience.

Sensory descriptions should be:

- Specific
- Controlled
- Believable
- Meaningful

Avoid vague descriptions that could apply to any luxury fragrance.

Instead of simply saying:

**“A luxurious and unforgettable fragrance.”**

The content should communicate what makes the experience distinctive.

The visitor should be able to build an internal picture of the product.

---

# Product Information

Emotional storytelling must never replace essential information.

Where relevant, product pages should provide clear access to:

- Product name
- Product type
- Size
- Price
- Availability
- Ingredients or composition
- Fragrance notes
- Usage
- Care
- Shipping
- Returns
- Other legally or commercially required information

The visitor should never have to decode poetic language to understand what they are purchasing.

---

# Content and Imagery

Text and imagery should work together.

Neither should exist merely to fill a layout.

Content may:

- Introduce an image
- Explain an image
- Contrast with an image
- Expand the meaning of an image
- Allow the image to speak without text

The relationship should be intentional.

A strong image may require only a few words.

A complex story may require deeper editorial treatment.

---

# Content and Motion

Motion should reveal or reinforce content.

It should never be used to hide weak content.

Examples:

- Text may appear progressively as a story unfolds.
- Images may transition to reveal materiality.
- Product details may emerge through interaction.
- Cinematic sequences may establish atmosphere before information appears.

The content remains meaningful even when motion is removed.

---

# Headlines

Headlines should be concise and purposeful.

A headline should create one of three outcomes:

- Orientation
- Curiosity
- Emotional response

It should not attempt to explain an entire section.

Good hierarchy:

**Headline → Supporting statement → Detail**

rather than:

**Headline → Paragraph → Paragraph → Paragraph**

---

# Microcopy

Small words carry significant responsibility.

Microcopy includes:

- Buttons
- Labels
- Filters
- Form instructions
- Error messages
- Notifications
- Checkout text
- Empty states

Microcopy must remain:

- Clear
- Calm
- Human
- Consistent

A luxury interface can still say:

**Add to Bag**

when that is the clearest expression.

There is no need to replace functional language with unnecessary poetic language.

---

# Calls to Action

Calls to action should express the next meaningful step.

Examples may include:

- Explore
- Discover
- View Collection
- Discover the Creation
- Learn More
- Add to Bag
- Continue
- Complete Purchase

The wording should match the user's current intent.

Do not use aggressive commercial language when the visitor is still discovering the brand.

---

# Content Density

Content density should vary according to purpose.

### Brand and Editorial Areas

May allow greater emotional depth and visual space.

### Product Areas

Require a balanced combination of emotional and functional information.

### Checkout

Should be highly focused and concise.

### Supporting Information

Should be comprehensive but organised for easy scanning.

There is no single ideal content density for the entire website.

---

# Content Reuse

Content should be structured so that it can be reused consistently across the digital ecosystem.

Where appropriate, the same underlying information may appear in:

- Product pages
- Collection pages
- Search
- Recommendations
- Editorial content
- Campaigns
- Customer communications

However, reuse should not result in repetitive or identical experiences everywhere.

The underlying truth remains consistent.

The presentation adapts to context.

---

# Content Governance

Every important piece of content should have:

- A clear purpose
- An intended audience
- An appropriate location
- An owner
- A defined source of truth

Content should not be duplicated across the system without a reason.

Changes to core brand information should propagate consistently.

---

# Content Quality Standard

Before publishing content, ask:

1. **Why does this content exist?**
2. **Who needs it?**
3. **What should the visitor feel or understand after reading it?**
4. **Is it accurate?**
5. **Is it necessary?**
6. **Does it sound like SAB’A?**
7. **Does it support the current stage of the journey?**
8. **Does it create clarity or unnecessary complexity?**
9. **Does it respect the heritage it references?**
10. **Would the experience be stronger if this content were removed?**

If content does not have a meaningful role, it should be reconsidered.

---

# Content Integrity

The quality of SAB’A's content must remain consistent with the quality of its products and physical experiences.

The website must never make claims that the product, service, or brand experience cannot support.

The standard is:

**Say less. Mean more.**

---

# Core Content Standard

SAB’A content should never feel like words placed around products.

It should feel like part of the house.

Every sentence should either:

- Build the world
- Create understanding
- Establish trust
- Increase appreciation
- Support the experience
- Enable action

Nothing should exist merely to fill space.

---

# Core Statement

SAB’A does not use content simply to sell products.

It uses content to communicate meaning.

It introduces the visitor to a world.

It reveals the history behind the identity.

It gives products context.

It creates understanding before desire.

It provides clarity before purchase.

And it leaves the visitor with something worth remembering.

**The objective is not to say more.**

**The objective is to make every word matter.**
