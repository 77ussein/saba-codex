# Product Pages

## Purpose

The SAB’A product page is not a conventional e-commerce product listing.

It is the dedicated digital space for a creation within the house.

Its purpose is to transform a visitor's understanding of a product from:

**Object → Meaning → Experience → Desire → Decision → Ownership**

The product page must provide both emotional depth and practical clarity.

It should allow the visitor to understand the creation, appreciate its character, trust the house, and purchase with confidence.

The governing principle is:

**The product is not presented outside the world of SAB’A. It is presented as a part of it.**

---

# Product Experience Philosophy

Every product should feel like it has earned its place within SAB’A.

The visitor should never feel that they have left the brand world and entered a generic product template.

The product page must preserve the same:

- Atmosphere
- Restraint
- Visual quality
- Typography
- Spatial discipline
- Emotional depth
- Interaction quality

established across the wider website.

The commercial function is essential, but it must exist within the experience rather than dominate it.

---

# Product Journey

The preferred product experience follows:

**First Impression → Identity → Sensory Understanding → Story → Trust → Desire → Decision → Purchase → Ownership**

This is not a rigid sequence for every product.

Different products may require different levels of depth.

The architecture should adapt to the creation while preserving the same underlying logic.

---

# First Impression

The first view of the product must immediately establish:

- Product identity
- Visual character
- SAB’A presence
- Quality
- Confidence

The visitor should understand what they are looking at without being overwhelmed by information.

The opening should create a feeling of:

**“This deserves my attention.”**

The first screen should avoid excessive:

- Text
- Promotional messaging
- Badges
- Discounts
- Technical information
- Multiple competing calls to action

The product should be given room to breathe.

---

# Product Hero

The hero area is the primary introduction to the creation.

It may combine:

- Product photography
- Cinematic video
- Product name
- Short statement
- Product type
- Price
- Primary action

The exact composition may change by product.

However, the hierarchy must remain clear.

The visitor should immediately understand:

**What is this?**

**Why should I care?**

**What can I do next?**

---

# Product Identity

The product identity should be unmistakable.

At minimum, the visitor should be able to identify:

- Product name
- Product category
- Collection where relevant
- Size or format
- Price
- Availability

Supporting information may be progressively revealed.

Product naming must follow the approved SAB’A naming system.

The product page must not introduce alternative names or inconsistent terminology.

---

# Product Story

Every important creation should have a reason for existing.

Where appropriate, the product story should communicate:

- Inspiration
- Origin
- Cultural relationship
- Creative intention
- Character
- Relationship to its collection
- Relationship to SAB’A

The story should explain meaning rather than simply decorate the product with poetic language.

The visitor should understand:

**Why this creation exists.**

---

# Sensory Experience

For fragrance and other sensory products, the website must compensate for the obvious limitation of digital commerce:

**The visitor cannot physically experience the product through the screen.**

The product page should therefore help the visitor build a mental and emotional representation.

This may be achieved through:

- Sensory language
- Visual atmosphere
- Material imagery
- Cinematic content
- Fragrance structure
- Context
- Usage scenarios
- Supporting storytelling

The objective is not to pretend the screen can reproduce the physical experience.

The objective is to make the visitor understand what they may encounter when they experience the product themselves.

---

# Fragrance Information

Where applicable, fragrance products should communicate their structure clearly.

This may include:

- Opening notes
- Heart notes
- Base notes
- Key ingredients
- Character
- Intensity
- Longevity where supported by appropriate information
- Recommended context or ritual

Information should remain accurate and should never promise an experience that cannot reasonably be supported.

Sensory storytelling and factual information should complement one another.

---

# Product Composition

Where relevant, the product page should provide clear information about:

- Ingredients
- Materials
- Concentration
- Size
- Origin
- Manufacturing
- Certifications
- Care

The visitor should never need to choose between emotional storytelling and factual transparency.

Both belong in the experience.

---

# Visual Storytelling

The visual system should communicate the product beyond a single packshot.

Depending on the creation, imagery may include:

### Product Image

Clear representation of the actual product.

### Detail Image

Highlights:

- Materials
- Cap
- Bottle
- Texture
- Craftsmanship
- Packaging

### Atmospheric Image

Communicates the emotional world surrounding the creation.

### Contextual Image

Shows the product within a relevant environment or ritual.

### Cinematic Content

Creates movement, atmosphere, or progressive revelation.

Every visual must have a purpose.

More images do not automatically create a better experience.

---

# Product Gallery

The gallery should provide enough visual information to understand the creation without becoming a catalogue of repetitive photographs.

Images should be ordered intentionally.

The sequence may move from:

**Recognition → Detail → Materiality → Atmosphere → Context**

The visitor should progressively discover the creation rather than repeatedly seeing the same object from different angles without purpose.

---

# Price

Price must be clear.

Luxury does not require hiding commercial information.

The price should be presented with confidence and appropriate visual hierarchy.

It should never dominate the emotional introduction to the product.

Avoid:

- Artificial urgency
- Aggressive discount treatment
- Excessive promotional badges
- Constant price emphasis

The product should establish value through the overall experience.

---

# Purchase Action

The primary purchase action must be immediately understandable.

The visitor should never wonder:

**“How do I buy this?”**

The primary action should be:

- Clearly visible
- Easy to interact with
- Consistent
- Accessible
- Appropriately prioritised

The language should remain direct.

For example:

**Add to Bag**

may be preferable to a decorative phrase that obscures the action.

Clarity is part of luxury.

---

# Product Options

Where a product has multiple:

- Sizes
- Volumes
- Variants
- Formats
- Concentrations
- Configurations

the options must be presented clearly.

The interface should make differences easy to understand.

Selection should not require unnecessary navigation or guesswork.

The selected state must always be obvious.

---

# Availability

Availability must be communicated clearly.

Possible states include:

- Available
- Limited availability
- Out of stock
- Coming soon
- Pre-order where applicable

Availability messaging should be truthful and calm.

Artificial scarcity should never be used to manipulate the visitor.

If a product is unavailable, the page should remain valuable rather than becoming a dead end.

---

# Out-of-Stock Experience

An unavailable product should continue to communicate its identity and story.

Where appropriate, the page may offer:

- Restock notification
- Related creations
- Collection discovery
- Product alternatives
- Deeper brand content

The visitor should remain inside the SAB’A world.

---

# Trust

The product page must provide enough information to support confident decision-making.

Trust may be strengthened through:

- Clear product information
- Authentic imagery
- Accurate descriptions
- Transparent pricing
- Shipping information
- Return information
- Secure transaction indicators where appropriate
- Customer service access
- Consistent brand presentation

Trust should feel natural rather than visually advertised.

---

# Social Proof

Social proof may be used when it genuinely helps the visitor make a decision.

Potential forms include:

- Verified reviews
- Customer experiences
- Ratings
- Editorial recognition
- Relevant testimonials

Social proof must not overpower the brand environment.

The page should never become a review marketplace.

Quality of evidence matters more than quantity.

---

# Recommendations

Related products should be presented as curated discovery.

The system may suggest:

- Complementary creations
- Products from the same collection
- Related fragrances
- Products with similar characteristics
- Products commonly explored together

Recommendations should feel like:

**“You may appreciate this.”**

rather than:

**“Buy more.”**

The visitor should remain free to continue exploring without feeling pressured.

---

# Product Relationships

Products should exist within a meaningful network.

A visitor should be able to move naturally between:

**Product → Collection → Related Product → SAB’A World**

This strengthens the sense that each creation belongs to a larger house.

Product relationships should be intentional rather than based solely on technical categorisation.

---

# Product Education

Where a product requires explanation, educational content should be available.

This may include:

- How to use the product
- How to apply fragrance
- How to store it
- How to care for it
- What to expect
- How to understand its composition
- Relevant cultural or historical context

Education should increase confidence rather than make the product feel complicated.

---

# Ownership

The product experience should anticipate the moment after purchase.

Where relevant, the page may introduce:

- Product care
- Usage guidance
- Storage
- Ritual
- Refill information
- Longevity
- Related experiences

The visitor should begin to imagine ownership before purchasing.

---

# Packaging and Unboxing

Where packaging is an important part of the SAB’A experience, the product page may communicate it.

The packaging should be presented as part of the creation rather than as an afterthought.

Content may communicate:

- Presentation
- Materials
- Opening ritual
- Layers
- Craftsmanship
- Reusability
- Emotional value

The physical arrival of the product should feel like a continuation of the digital experience.

---

# Content Hierarchy

Product information should generally follow:

**Identity → Emotion → Understanding → Detail → Trust → Purchase**

Not every product requires the same amount of content.

The page should remain proportionate to the significance of the creation.

A hero creation may justify a deeper narrative.

A simple supporting product may require a more focused presentation.

---

# Product Page Layout

The exact layout may evolve over time.

However, the architecture should generally provide access to:

1. Product Introduction
2. Product Identity
3. Purchase Information
4. Sensory / Product Experience
5. Story
6. Composition / Details
7. Supporting Visuals
8. Trust Information
9. Related Discovery
10. Ownership / Care where relevant

The order may change when the product requires a different narrative.

The experience should follow meaning rather than a rigid template.

---

# Mobile Product Experience

The mobile product page must remain complete and premium.

It must not simply compress the desktop page.

Mobile priorities should include:

- Product recognition
- Image quality
- Clear product information
- Easy option selection
- Accessible purchase action
- Readable content
- Controlled motion
- Fast performance

The primary commercial action should remain easy to reach without destroying the immersive experience.

Detailed mobile principles are defined in:

**7_Mobile Experience.md**

---

# Motion on Product Pages

Motion may be used to create:

- Product reveal
- Material transitions
- Image progression
- Storytelling
- Visual continuity
- Emotional pacing

Motion must never interfere with:

- Product recognition
- Reading
- Selection
- Purchase
- Accessibility
- Performance

The visitor must remain in control.

---

# Performance

Product pages may contain high-quality imagery, video, and interactive content.

However, product quality must never be used as an excuse for poor performance.

The page must prioritise:

- Optimised images
- Efficient video
- Progressive loading
- Responsive rendering
- Stable layout
- Fast interaction

The visitor should experience quality immediately.

---

# Accessibility

Every product page must remain usable for visitors with different abilities.

This includes appropriate consideration for:

- Text contrast
- Alternative text
- Keyboard navigation
- Focus states
- Touch targets
- Screen readers
- Motion preferences
- Clear labels
- Error states

A luxurious product page that excludes users is not a complete product page.

---

# Product Page Integrity

Every product page must accurately represent the creation.

The page must never:

- Exaggerate product capabilities
- Make unsupported claims
- Misrepresent ingredients
- Manipulate availability
- Hide essential information
- Use misleading imagery
- Create false scarcity

The digital representation must earn the trust that the physical product is expected to deliver.

---

# Product Page Quality Test

Before approving a product page, ask:

1. **Can I immediately understand what the product is?**
2. **Does the first impression feel unmistakably SAB’A?**
3. **Do I understand why this creation exists?**
4. **Can I imagine its character or sensory experience?**
5. **Can I access the factual information I need?**
6. **Do I trust what I am being told?**
7. **Is the purchase action obvious?**
8. **Does the page create desire without pressure?**
9. **Does the product feel connected to the larger SAB’A world?**
10. **Does the experience remain premium on mobile?**
11. **Does the page remain fast and accessible?**
12. **Would the experience still feel strong without decorative effects?**

If the answer to these questions is consistently yes, the product page is performing its role.

---

# What the Product Page Must Never Become

The SAB’A product page must never become:

**Image + Name + Price + Add to Cart**

It must also never become:

- An endless wall of text
- A technical specification sheet
- A discount page
- A review marketplace
- A generic Shopify template
- A visual effects demonstration
- A historical lecture

It must remain a balanced environment where:

**Product + Story + Sensory Experience + Information + Commerce**

exist together.

---

# Core Standard

The product page should make the visitor feel that the creation has a place within the house.

It should create enough emotion to make the visitor care.

Enough information to make the visitor understand.

Enough trust to make the visitor confident.

Enough desire to make the purchase feel natural.

And enough continuity to make ownership feel like the beginning of the relationship, not its end.

---

# Core Statement

The SAB’A product page is not a sales sheet.

It is the digital chamber of the creation.

It introduces the product.

It reveals its character.

It communicates its meaning.

It allows the visitor to understand its experience.

It builds trust.

It creates desire.

It enables purchase.

And it begins the relationship of ownership.

**The objective is not simply to sell the product.**

**The objective is to make the visitor understand why the product belongs to SAB’A — and why it may belong to them.**
