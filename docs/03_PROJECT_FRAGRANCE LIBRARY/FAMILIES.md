# FRAGRANCE FAMILIES

The SAB’A Fragrance Families framework defines the olfactive families used to classify and organize SAB’A fragrances.

Its purpose is to provide a clear and consistent classification system while allowing each fragrance to maintain its own distinctive character.

---

## 1. Purpose

Fragrance families help SAB’A to:

- Classify fragrances consistently.
- Describe their overall olfactive direction.
- Organize the fragrance portfolio.
- Support development and evaluation.
- Improve internal and customer-facing communication.

---

## 2. Primary Families

SAB’A may use established international fragrance families as a reference, while adapting their application to the needs of the brand.

Core families may include:

- Floral
- Woody
- Amber
- Fresh
- Citrus
- Aromatic
- Fruity
- Gourmand
- Leather
- Chypre
- Oriental / Spicy

The final classification system should be validated and standardized as the fragrance library develops.

---

## 3. Secondary Classification

A fragrance may be described using a primary family supported by secondary characteristics.

These may include:

- Dry
- Creamy
- Smoky
- Warm
- Fresh
- Spicy
- Musky
- Resinous
- Powdery
- Green
- Earthy
- Mineral

Secondary descriptors should clarify the fragrance rather than create unnecessary complexity.

---

## 4. Fragrance Positioning

Classification should describe the fragrance rather than define its entire identity.

A fragrance may combine multiple olfactive directions while maintaining one primary classification.

The classification should reflect the dominant character of the composition.

---

## 5. Portfolio Balance

Fragrance families should help SAB’A maintain variety across the collection.

The portfolio should provide meaningful differences between fragrances without sacrificing overall coherence.

Classification should therefore support both:

**Individual Character + Collection Cohesion**

---

## 6. Core Principle

Fragrance families are a tool for organization and communication, not a limitation on creativity.

A SAB’A fragrance should be classified clearly while remaining free to develop its own distinctive olfactive identity.
