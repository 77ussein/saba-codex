Guidelines. Status Approved. Owner SAB’A.

Last updated August4,2026. Purpose. 

These guidelines define how SAB’A communicates through writing, ensuring that every sentence reflects the same identity, values, and voice. These standards apply across websites, product descriptions, packaging, advertising, emails, social media, customer support, internal documents, and AI-generated content. Core writing principles. Write with purpose, elegance, clarity, confidence, and authenticity. Every sentence should strengthen the identity of SAB’A. Tell stories, not sales pitches. Begin with meaning, not products. Focus on heritage, craftsmanship, inspiration, and emotion before discussing features or pricing. Simplicity creates luxury. Use short, meaningful sentences. Remove unnecessary words. If a sentence can be simplified without losing meaning, simplify it. Show, don’t boast. Avoid unsupported claims. Instead of saying a product is exceptional, demonstrate why through materials, craftsmanship, and thoughtful design. Respect the reader. Assume the audience is intelligent. Avoid manipulation or exaggeration. Communicate honestly, confidently and respectfully. Heritage with modern relevance. Draw inspiration from the timeless legacy of the ancient Kingdom of Sheba and present it through contemporary language and global standards. Emotional before commercial. Create admiration and desire before asking for action. Every message should leave an emotional impression, not just information. Consistency above creativity. Creativity should strengthen the existing identity of SAB’A, never replace or weaken it. Writing style. Use clear language, active voice, and concise paragraphs. Choose precise words over decorative language. Write with confidence, not arrogance. Words that define SAB’A. Timeless. Refined. Craftsmanship. Authenticity. Elegance. Purpose. Precision. Legacy. Quality. Confidence. Words to avoid. Cheap. Best ever. Perfect. Revolutionary. Limited time. Don’t miss out. Hurry. Guaranteed. Overused exclamation marks and marketing clichés. Before publishing, ask: Does this sound like SAB’A? Does it build trust? Is every sentence necessary? Would this still feel appropriate in ten years? If the answer to any of these is no, revise before publishing. Commitment. Every piece of writing for SAB’A must reinforce the same identity, philosophy, and long-term vision. Writing is not decoration; it is a core part of the brand experience
