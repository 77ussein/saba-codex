# Launch Strategy
## Purpose
SAB’A launches should introduce products, collections, and significant brand developments through a deliberate and coherent experience.
A launch should build understanding, curiosity, desire, and confidence without relying on artificial urgency or excessive promotion.
## Launch Principles
1. **Substance before Promotion**
2. **Preparation before Exposure**
3. **Curiosity before Conversion**
4. **Experience before Pressure**
5. **Clarity before Complexity**
6. **Consistency across Channels**
7. **Long-term Value over Short-term Attention**
## Launch Objectives
A launch may aim to:
- Introduce a new product
- Introduce a collection
- Establish a new chapter for SAB’A
- Generate qualified awareness
- Drive product discovery
- Support initial sales
- Build customer relationships
- Generate cultural or editorial relevance
- Encourage repeat engagement
Each launch should define a primary objective before execution begins.
## Launch Architecture
A SAB’A launch may follow:
**Preparation**  
→ **Tease**  
→ **Reveal**  
→ **Launch**  
→ **Experience**  
→ **Follow-up**  
→ **Evaluation**
Not every launch requires every stage.
The structure should reflect the significance, audience, and nature of the launch.
## Pre-Launch
The pre-launch phase prepares the audience and operational system for what is coming.
Activities may include:
- Product preparation
- Content production
- Website preparation
- Ecommerce setup
- Photography and visual production
- Customer communication
- Creator or partner coordination
- PR preparation
- Inventory readiness
- Internal launch briefing
Pre-launch communication should create curiosity without revealing more than necessary.
## Teasing
Teasing should introduce signals rather than explain everything.
It may use:
- Details
- Materials
- Fragrance impressions
- Visual fragments
- Short narratives
- Atmospheric content
- Behind-the-scenes elements
- Controlled product glimpses
Teasing should feel intentional rather than artificially mysterious.
## Reveal
The reveal provides the audience with a clear understanding of what is being introduced.
It should communicate:
- What it is
- Why it exists
- What makes it distinctive
- Relevant product information
- The experience it offers
- Where and how it can be discovered
The reveal should resolve curiosity while creating a reason to explore further.
## Launch
The launch moment brings the product or collection into active public availability.
Launch execution may include:
- Website publication
- Ecommerce activation
- Social content
- Email
- WhatsApp
- Paid media
- Creator partnerships
- PR
- Events
- Retail or physical experiences
All customer-facing touchpoints should communicate the same core idea.
## Launch Experience
The launch should be considered as a complete customer experience rather than a collection of marketing assets.
The experience may include:
**Discovery**  
→ **Understanding**  
→ **Consideration**  
→ **Purchase**  
→ **Unboxing**  
→ **First Use**  
→ **Follow-up**
Every stage should reinforce the quality and meaning of the launch.
## Availability Strategy
Availability should be planned according to the nature of the launch.
Possible approaches include:
- Full public release
- Limited initial availability
- Selected access
- Event-based introduction
- Phased release
- Regional release
Restrictions should have a legitimate operational or strategic reason.
Artificial scarcity should not be used as a substitute for genuine desirability.
## Channel Strategy
Launch channels may include:
- Website
- Ecommerce
- Email
- WhatsApp
- Social Media
- Paid Media
- Creators
- Partnerships
- PR
- Events
- Customer Experience
Each channel should have a defined role within the launch rather than repeating identical content everywhere.
## Launch Messaging
Launch messaging should remain:
- Clear
- Refined
- Distinctive
- Human
- Meaningful
- Consistent with SAB’A
Avoid:
- Artificial urgency
- Excessive claims
- Empty luxury language
- Aggressive calls to purchase
- Manufactured exclusivity
- Unsupported product claims
The launch should invite discovery rather than pressure the audience into action.
## Operational Readiness
No major launch should proceed without confirming:
- Product availability
- Inventory levels
- Product information
- Pricing
- Website readiness
- Ecommerce readiness
- Payment readiness
- Shipping readiness
- Packaging readiness
- Customer service readiness
- Content readiness
- Legal and compliance requirements
Marketing should never create demand that the business is unable to serve.
## Post-Launch
After launch, SAB’A should continue supporting the product through:
- Product education
- Customer communication
- Editorial content
- Customer experience
- Relevant recommendations
- Community engagement
- Performance optimization
The launch is the beginning of the product's relationship with the audience, not the end of communication.
## Measurement
Launch performance may be evaluated through:
- Reach
- Qualified traffic
- Engagement
- Conversion
- Revenue
- Customer acquisition cost
- Product sell-through
- Repeat purchase
- Retention
- Customer lifetime value
- Return on marketing investment
Performance should be interpreted against the launch objective and its intended timeframe.
## Launch Review
After completion, the launch should be reviewed for:
- What worked
- What underperformed
- Customer response
- Operational issues
- Content performance
- Channel performance
- Commercial results
- Brand impact
- Lessons for future launches
Significant findings should be documented for future reference.
## Core Principle
> A SAB’A launch should feel like the opening of something worth discovering, not simply the announcement of something for sale.
