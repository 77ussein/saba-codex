# Product Compliance

## Purpose

Product Compliance defines the framework used to ensure that SAB’A products meet applicable legal, regulatory, safety, labeling, and market requirements before and during commercial availability.

The objective is to ensure that products are not only well developed and presented, but also legally appropriate for the markets in which they are offered.

## Compliance Principles

1. **Safety before Commercialization**
2. **Compliance before Launch**
3. **Evidence before Claims**
4. **Accurate Information**
5. **Traceable Documentation**
6. **Market-Specific Review**
7. **Current Requirements over Assumption**
8. **Professional Review where Required**

## Scope

Product compliance may cover:

- Product composition
- Raw materials
- Product safety
- Manufacturing requirements
- Packaging
- Labeling
- Product claims
- Warnings and precautions
- Documentation
- Storage requirements
- Transportation requirements
- Import and export requirements
- Market-specific registrations or notifications

The exact requirements depend on the product category, formulation, market, and applicable jurisdiction.

## Product Classification

Each product should be appropriately classified according to its intended use and the requirements of the relevant market.

Classification may affect:

- Regulatory requirements
- Product claims
- Labeling
- Testing
- Registration
- Documentation
- Manufacturing requirements

Classification should be verified where uncertainty exists.

## Product Safety

Products should be developed, manufactured, packaged, stored, and distributed in a manner consistent with applicable safety requirements.

Where relevant, safety considerations may include:

- Ingredient safety
- Intended use
- Exposure
- Packaging compatibility
- Stability
- Storage conditions
- Handling
- Consumer warnings

Required safety documentation should be maintained.

## Ingredients and Materials

Materials used in SAB’A products should be appropriately documented.

Relevant records may include:

- Material identity
- Supplier
- Specification
- Relevant safety documentation
- Batch information where applicable
- Regulatory status where applicable
- Supporting technical documentation

Materials should be evaluated according to the requirements applicable to their intended use.

## Product Testing

Testing should be performed where required or where reasonably necessary to establish product safety, quality, stability, compatibility, or compliance.

Testing may include:

- Stability testing
- Compatibility testing
- Safety-related testing
- Microbiological testing where applicable
- Packaging testing
- Performance testing

Testing requirements should be determined according to the product and applicable regulations.

## Labeling

Product labels should contain all information required by the relevant jurisdiction and product category.

Depending on the product, this may include:

- Product name
- Product identity
- Net quantity
- Ingredients or composition
- Usage instructions
- Warnings
- Precautions
- Manufacturer or responsible party
- Country of origin where required
- Batch or lot information
- Date information where required
- Regulatory information

Labels must be accurate and should not contain unsupported claims.

## Product Claims

Claims made about SAB’A products should be supported by appropriate evidence where required.

Claims may relate to:

- Product characteristics
- Materials
- Fragrance
- Performance
- Safety
- Origin
- Sustainability
- Manufacturing
- Other consumer-relevant attributes

Marketing language must not create a misleading impression about the product.

## Packaging Compliance

Packaging should satisfy both functional and regulatory requirements.

Compliance considerations may include:

- Required labeling
- Warnings
- Material requirements
- Packaging quantity
- Recycling or disposal information where applicable
- Transportation requirements
- Product protection

Packaging changes should be reviewed when they may affect compliance.

## Market Requirements

A product compliant in one market may not automatically be compliant in another.

Before entering a new market, SAB’A should review applicable requirements relating to:

- Product category
- Ingredients
- Labeling
- Claims
- Registration
- Importation
- Packaging
- Consumer protection

Market-specific requirements should be verified before commercial launch.

## Documentation

Product compliance records should be maintained in an organized and traceable manner.

Relevant records may include:

- Product specifications
- Ingredient documentation
- Safety documentation
- Test reports
- Certificates
- Regulatory assessments
- Label approvals
- Product registrations or notifications
- Supplier documentation
- Compliance correspondence

## Change Control

Changes to a product should trigger a compliance review when they may affect regulatory status or product safety.

Relevant changes may include:

- Formula changes
- Ingredient changes
- Supplier changes
- Packaging changes
- Label changes
- Product claims
- Manufacturing changes
- Intended use
- Target market

No material compliance-sensitive change should be implemented without the appropriate review.

## Supplier Compliance

Where product compliance depends on suppliers or manufacturers, SAB’A should maintain appropriate documentation and contractual requirements.

Supplier documentation may include:

- Specifications
- Certificates
- Safety documentation
- Testing records
- Manufacturing information
- Regulatory documentation

Supplier compliance information should remain current where applicable.

## Product Release

Before commercial release, the relevant product should have completed the required compliance checks for its intended market.

Release review may confirm:

- Product classification
- Required documentation
- Safety requirements
- Labeling
- Packaging
- Claims
- Required registrations or notifications
- Applicable market requirements

## Non-Compliance

If a potential compliance issue is identified, SAB’A should assess the matter promptly.

Possible actions may include:

1. Pause release or distribution
2. Identify the affected product or batch
3. Verify the issue
4. Obtain appropriate professional or regulatory advice
5. Correct the issue
6. Document the resolution
7. Assess whether further action is required

## Recalls and Corrective Action

Where a product presents a significant safety or regulatory concern, SAB’A should follow an appropriate corrective action or recall process consistent with applicable requirements.

Such situations should be handled with appropriate professional and regulatory guidance.

## Record Retention

Compliance records should be retained for the periods required by applicable law, regulation, contractual obligations, or internal policy.

Records should remain:

- Accessible
- Secure
- Traceable
- Current where applicable
- Protected from unauthorized alteration

## Responsibility

Product compliance may involve:

- Product Development
- Quality
- Operations
- Packaging
- Legal
- Suppliers
- Manufacturers
- Regulatory specialists

Responsibilities should be clearly assigned for each relevant compliance activity.

## Professional and Regulatory Advice

Product compliance requirements vary significantly by product category and jurisdiction.

Where requirements involve regulatory interpretation, product registration, safety assessment, claims, or other specialized matters, SAB’A should consult an appropriately qualified regulatory or legal professional.

## Core Principle

> A SAB’A product should be ready for the market not only when it is desirable and well made, but when it is appropriately documented, compliant, and safe for its intended use.
