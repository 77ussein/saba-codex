# Legal Structure

## Purpose

Legal Structure defines the legal and organizational framework through which SAB’A operates.

The objective is to establish clear ownership, authority, responsibilities, and legal relationships while ensuring that the structure can support the development and growth of SAB’A.

## Legal Structure Principles

1. **Clear Ownership**
2. **Defined Authority**
3. **Documented Responsibility**
4. **Legal Compliance**
5. **Separation of Personal and Business Interests**
6. **Traceable Decisions**
7. **Scalability**
8. **Professional Legal Review where Required**

## Business Entity

SAB’A should operate through a legally recognized business structure appropriate to its activities, ownership, jurisdiction, and growth requirements.

The selected structure should be documented through the relevant official records.

Relevant information may include:

- Legal entity name
- Trading or brand name
- Entity type
- Registration details
- Jurisdiction
- Registered activities
- Ownership structure
- Authorized representatives
- Relevant licenses

## Brand and Legal Entity

SAB’A as a brand may operate under a legal entity that owns or controls the relevant business activities.

The relationship between the legal entity and the SAB’A brand should be clearly documented where necessary.

The brand identity does not replace the legal identity of the business.

## Ownership

Ownership should be clearly documented through appropriate legal records.

Relevant documentation may include:

- Ownership interests
- Shareholding
- Founder agreements
- Investment agreements
- Transfer records
- Related legal documents

Ownership changes should be formally documented according to applicable legal requirements.

## Authority and Representation

Individuals acting on behalf of SAB’A should have appropriate authority for their responsibilities.

Authority may relate to:

- Contracts
- Purchasing
- Banking
- Employment
- Suppliers
- Partnerships
- Intellectual Property
- Regulatory matters

Significant commitments should not be made without appropriate authorization.

## Contracts and Agreements

Material business relationships should be supported by appropriate agreements where required.

Examples include:

- Supplier Agreements
- Manufacturing Agreements
- Distribution Agreements
- Service Agreements
- Partnership Agreements
- Employment Agreements
- Confidentiality Agreements
- Licensing Agreements

Contracts should clearly define relevant responsibilities, rights, obligations, terms, and termination conditions.

## Intellectual Property Ownership

Intellectual property created for or on behalf of SAB’A should have clearly defined ownership.

This may include:

- Brand names
- Logos
- Designs
- Product concepts
- Packaging designs
- Written content
- Photography
- Digital assets
- Formulas or technical work where legally applicable

Ownership and usage rights should be documented through appropriate agreements where necessary.

## Licenses and Registrations

SAB’A should maintain the registrations and licenses required for its activities in each relevant jurisdiction.

These may include:

- Business registrations
- Commercial licenses
- Product-related registrations
- Import or export permissions
- Other regulatory approvals

Requirements should be verified according to the applicable jurisdiction and business activity.

## Legal Responsibilities

Legal responsibilities should be assigned appropriately across the business.

Operational teams may manage routine compliance requirements within their areas, while significant legal matters should be escalated for appropriate review.

Legal responsibility should not depend solely on informal understanding.

## Jurisdiction

Legal requirements may differ according to:

- Country
- State or region
- Place of incorporation
- Manufacturing location
- Storage location
- Sales market
- Customer location

SAB’A should identify the jurisdictions relevant to each material legal activity.

## Legal Changes

Changes to the legal structure should be reviewed before implementation where appropriate.

Examples include:

- Ownership changes
- New entities
- New jurisdictions
- Business activity changes
- Mergers or acquisitions
- Major investments
- Structural reorganizations

Material changes should be reflected in the relevant legal records and Codex documentation.

## Record Keeping

Legal structure records should be maintained in an organized and secure manner.

Records may include:

- Registration documents
- Corporate documents
- Ownership records
- Licenses
- Authorizations
- Agreements
- Amendments
- Legal correspondence

Current documents should remain distinguishable from superseded or historical records.

## Confidentiality

Sensitive legal and ownership information should be accessed only by authorized individuals.

Confidential records should be stored and shared according to applicable security and privacy requirements.

## Professional Legal Advice

Where legal interpretation, drafting, dispute management, or jurisdiction-specific advice is required, SAB’A should consult an appropriately qualified legal professional.

This Codex provides an internal framework and does not replace professional legal advice.

## Review

The legal structure should be reviewed when:

- The business changes materially
- Ownership changes
- New markets are entered
- New business activities are introduced
- Regulatory requirements change
- New legal risks emerge

## Core Principle

> SAB’A should have a legal structure that is clear enough to protect the present and flexible enough to support the future.
