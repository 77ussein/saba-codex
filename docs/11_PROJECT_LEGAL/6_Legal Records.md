# Legal Records

## Purpose

Legal Records defines how SAB’A creates, maintains, organizes, protects, and retrieves records relating to the legal operation of the business.

The objective is to ensure that important legal information remains accurate, traceable, secure, accessible to authorized parties, and distinguishable from historical or superseded records.

## Record-Keeping Principles

1. **Accuracy before Convenience**
2. **Current Records over Historical Versions**
3. **Traceability**
4. **Controlled Access**
5. **Secure Storage**
6. **Defined Ownership**
7. **Appropriate Retention**
8. **Clear Version Control**
9. **Evidence before Assumption**
10. **Confidentiality where Required**

## Scope

Legal records may include:

- Business registrations
- Licenses and permits
- Corporate documents
- Ownership records
- Contracts and agreements
- Trademark records
- Intellectual property records
- Product compliance records
- Privacy documentation
- Regulatory correspondence
- Legal advice
- Dispute records
- Claims and notices
- Approvals
- Other material legal documentation

## Record Classification

Legal records should be classified according to their purpose and status.

Possible classifications include:

- **Current** — currently applicable
- **Pending** — awaiting completion or approval
- **Expired** — no longer active but retained where required
- **Superseded** — replaced by a newer document
- **Historical** — retained for reference or legal history
- **Confidential** — requiring restricted access

## Current Source of Truth

For any legal matter, the latest approved and applicable record should be treated as the current Source of Truth.

Historical or superseded records must not override current legal documentation.

Where uncertainty exists, the responsible owner should verify the current record before relying on it.

## Record Ownership

Each important legal record should have an identifiable owner responsible for:

- Maintaining the record
- Monitoring relevant dates
- Updating status
- Coordinating required renewals
- Controlling access
- Ensuring appropriate retention

Ownership should be assigned according to the nature of the record.

## Record Metadata

Where practical, legal records should include:

- Document Name
- Document Type
- Owner
- Legal Entity
- Jurisdiction
- Effective Date
- Expiry Date where applicable
- Status
- Version
- Related Agreement or Record
- Confidentiality Level
- Storage Location
- Notes

## Contracts and Agreements

Material contracts should be maintained with sufficient information to identify:

- Parties
- Agreement type
- Effective date
- Term
- Renewal terms
- Expiry date
- Key obligations
- Termination provisions
- Amendments
- Related documents

Amendments should remain connected to the original agreement.

## Licenses and Registrations

Licenses, permits, registrations, and approvals should be tracked with relevant:

- Issuing authority
- Registration or license number
- Issue date
- Expiry date
- Renewal requirements
- Status
- Supporting documentation

Upcoming expirations should be monitored appropriately.

## Intellectual Property Records

Intellectual property records should be maintained according to the requirements established in the Intellectual Property and Trademarks documentation.

Relevant records may include:

- Trademark applications
- Trademark registrations
- Copyright records
- Design registrations
- Licensing agreements
- Ownership documentation
- Assignment agreements

## Compliance Records

Product and regulatory records should be maintained where required.

These may include:

- Product compliance documentation
- Testing records
- Certificates
- Registrations
- Notifications
- Regulatory assessments
- Label approvals
- Compliance correspondence

Records should remain linked to the relevant product where practical.

## Privacy Records

Privacy-related legal records may include:

- Privacy Notices
- Consent records
- Data processing documentation
- Data protection agreements
- Data requests
- Data incident records
- Regulatory correspondence

Access should be appropriately restricted.

## Legal Advice

Legal advice received from qualified professionals should be retained where it is material to an important business decision or legal matter.

Relevant records may include:

- Advice
- Opinions
- Memos
- Correspondence
- Supporting documents

Confidentiality and applicable legal privilege should be respected where relevant.

## Disputes and Claims

Material disputes, claims, notices, or legal proceedings should be documented and maintained securely.

Records may include:

- Initial notice
- Correspondence
- Evidence
- Agreements
- Legal advice
- Actions taken
- Resolution
- Final outcome

Access should be restricted to authorized individuals.

## Version Control

Legal documents should use clear version control where multiple versions exist.

A record should make it possible to distinguish:

- Draft
- Under Review
- Approved
- Executed
- Amended
- Superseded
- Archived

Drafts should not be mistaken for executed or current legal documents.

## Document Integrity

Legal records should be protected against unauthorized:

- Alteration
- Deletion
- Replacement
- Disclosure
- Loss

Where appropriate, records should preserve evidence of approval, execution, or authenticity.

## Access Control

Access to legal records should be based on legitimate business need.

Sensitive records may require additional restrictions based on:

- Confidentiality
- Legal sensitivity
- Personal data
- Commercial sensitivity
- Privileged information

## Retention

Records should be retained for periods appropriate to:

- Applicable law
- Regulatory requirements
- Contractual obligations
- Business needs
- Potential disputes
- Intellectual property requirements

Records should not be destroyed while they remain subject to a legal hold or other applicable preservation requirement.

## Legal Holds

When SAB’A becomes aware of a dispute, investigation, claim, or other matter requiring preservation of records, relevant records should be protected from deletion or alteration until the preservation requirement is appropriately released.

## Archive

Records that are no longer current but remain useful for historical, legal, or evidentiary purposes should be transferred to the appropriate archive structure.

Archived records must remain clearly identified as historical and must not override current legal records.

## Security and Backup

Legal records should be stored using appropriate security and backup measures.

Critical records should have reasonable protection against:

- Data loss
- Unauthorized access
- System failure
- Accidental deletion
- Physical damage where applicable

## Review

Legal records should be reviewed periodically to identify:

- Expired documents
- Upcoming renewals
- Missing records
- Outdated information
- Duplicate records
- Records requiring archival
- Changes in legal status

## Record Retention Register

Where practical, SAB’A should maintain a register for material legal records.

| Record | Owner | Jurisdiction | Status | Effective Date | Expiry / Renewal | Location |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Business Registration | Assigned Owner | Applicable Jurisdiction | Current | — | — | Legal Records |
| Trademark Registration | Assigned Owner | Applicable Jurisdiction | Current | — | — | IP Records |
| Commercial Agreement | Assigned Owner | Applicable Jurisdiction | Current | — | — | Contracts |
| Product Compliance Record | Assigned Owner | Applicable Market | Current | — | — | Compliance |
| Privacy Documentation | Assigned Owner | Applicable Jurisdiction | Current | — | — | Privacy |

The register should be updated when material legal records are created, changed, renewed, or superseded.

## Professional Legal Advice

Where legal interpretation or record-management requirements are unclear, SAB’A should obtain advice from an appropriately qualified legal professional.

This document establishes an internal records framework and does not replace legal advice.

## Core Principle

> A legal record is valuable only when SAB’A can identify what it is, know whether it is current, trust its integrity, and retrieve it when it matters.
