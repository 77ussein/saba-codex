# Trademarks

## Purpose

Trademarks defines how SAB’A identifies, protects, manages, uses, and monitors its trademarks and other distinctive brand identifiers.

The objective is to protect the names, marks, and identifiers that distinguish SAB’A and its products in the marketplace.

## Trademark Principles

1. **Protect Distinctive Identity**
2. **Register Strategically**
3. **Use Consistently**
4. **Maintain Clear Ownership**
5. **Monitor Relevant Markets**
6. **Respect Third-Party Rights**
7. **Maintain Accurate Records**
8. **Review Before Material Expansion**

## Trademark Scope

Potential SAB’A trademarks may include:

- SAB’A name
- Primary logo
- Word marks
- Product names
- Collection names
- Distinctive symbols
- Taglines where appropriate
- Other distinctive identifiers

Not every element will necessarily qualify for trademark protection.

Protection depends on the applicable law, jurisdiction, distinctiveness, and registration status.

## Trademark Ownership

Trademarks should be registered and maintained under the appropriate legal owner.

Ownership records should clearly identify:

- Trademark owner
- Mark
- Jurisdiction
- Registration status
- Relevant registration or application number
- Filing date
- Registration date
- Applicable classes
- Renewal requirements

## Trademark Clearance

Before adopting a significant new name, mark, or product name, SAB’A should assess whether the proposed identifier may conflict with existing third-party rights.

Where appropriate, clearance should consider:

- Relevant jurisdictions
- Similar marks
- Similar goods or services
- Existing registrations
- Potential commercial conflicts

Significant trademark clearance should be handled with appropriately qualified legal professionals.

## Registration

Trademark registration should be considered according to:

- Strategic importance
- Target markets
- Product categories
- Commercial plans
- Expansion plans
- Legal availability
- Cost and benefit

Priority should generally be given to marks that are central to SAB’A's long-term identity or commercial activity.

## Trademark Usage

Registered or protected marks should be used consistently with approved brand guidelines.

This includes:

- Correct spelling
- Approved logo versions
- Approved visual treatment
- Product naming conventions
- Appropriate legal notices where required

Trademark usage should not undermine the distinctiveness of the mark.

## Product and Collection Names

Product and collection names may become important commercial identifiers.

Before significant commercial use, names should be reviewed for:

- Distinctiveness
- Availability
- Potential conflicts
- Relevant jurisdictions
- Intended product categories

Approved names should be documented as part of the relevant product records.

## Trademark Classes

Trademark classifications should correspond to the actual and reasonably anticipated goods or services for which protection is sought.

Class selection should be determined with appropriate professional advice where required.

## Geographic Scope

Trademark protection is generally jurisdiction-specific.

SAB’A should identify the markets where trademark protection is strategically important.

Expansion into a new market should trigger a review of relevant trademark considerations before significant commercial activity.

## Renewals and Maintenance

Trademark records should include applicable:

- Renewal dates
- Filing deadlines
- Maintenance requirements
- Use requirements
- Supporting documentation

Renewal and maintenance responsibilities should be assigned to an appropriate owner.

## Monitoring

Relevant trademark use by third parties should be monitored where commercially appropriate.

Potential concerns may include:

- Similar brand names
- Similar logos
- Similar product names
- Misleading association
- Unauthorized commercial use

Potential conflicts should be assessed before action is taken.

## Enforcement

Where unauthorized use may affect SAB’A's trademark rights, the matter should be documented and evaluated.

Potential actions may include:

1. Investigation
2. Evidence preservation
3. Legal assessment
4. Direct communication
5. Platform or registry action
6. Formal legal action where appropriate

The response should be proportionate to the nature and importance of the issue.

## Third-Party Trademarks

SAB’A should respect the trademark rights of others.

Third-party marks should not be used in a manner that:

- Suggests unauthorized affiliation
- Creates misleading association
- Infringes applicable rights
- Misrepresents a relationship

Use should be based on an appropriate legal basis and relevant professional advice where necessary.

## Trademark Records

A central trademark record should be maintained where practical.

The record may include:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Mark | Trademark identifier |
| Type | Word, logo, symbol, etc. |
| Owner | Legal owner |
| Jurisdiction | Protected territory |
| Class | Relevant classification |
| Application Number | Filing reference |
| Registration Number | Registration reference |
| Status | Active, pending, expired, etc. |
| Filing Date | Date submitted |
| Registration Date | Date registered |
| Renewal Date | Applicable renewal date |
| Notes | Relevant legal or operational information |

## Brand and Trademark Consistency

Trademark protection and brand consistency should support one another.

The legal identity of SAB’A should remain aligned with the approved:

- Brand Identity
- Product Naming
- Packaging
- Website
- Marketing
- Content

Material deviations should be reviewed where they may affect legal rights or brand consistency.

## Changes to Protected Marks

Changes to an important trademark, logo, or other protected identifier should be reviewed before implementation.

This may include:

- Redesigns
- Rebranding
- Name changes
- Product naming changes
- New symbols
- New taglines

Existing registrations may not automatically cover materially different versions.

## Professional Legal Advice

Trademark law varies by jurisdiction and circumstances.

SAB’A should obtain appropriately qualified legal advice for matters including:

- Clearance
- Registration
- Opposition
- Infringement
- Enforcement
- Licensing
- Ownership disputes
- International expansion

## Core Principle

> A trademark is more than a name or symbol. It is a legal identifier of the identity SAB’A builds and the trust associated with it.
