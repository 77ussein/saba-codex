# Legal

## Purpose

The Legal section defines the legal framework supporting SAB’A and provides a structured reference for legal, regulatory, intellectual property, privacy, compliance, and contractual matters.

The objective is to protect SAB’A, its customers, products, assets, and operations while ensuring that important legal matters are properly documented and managed.

## Role of Legal

Legal documentation supports:

- Business Structure
- Intellectual Property
- Trademarks
- Product Compliance
- Privacy and Data
- Contracts
- Regulatory Requirements
- Legal Records
- Risk Management

Legal requirements should support the business without unnecessarily restricting practical operations.

## Legal Principles

1. **Compliance before Convenience**
2. **Protection before Exposure**
3. **Documentation before Assumption**
4. **Clarity before Ambiguity**
5. **Accuracy before Speed**
6. **Evidence before Interpretation**
7. **Professional Advice where Required**
8. **Current Requirements over Historical Practice**

## Legal Scope

The SAB’A legal framework may include:

- Corporate and business matters
- Commercial agreements
- Intellectual property
- Trademarks
- Product regulations
- Labeling requirements
- Consumer protection
- Privacy and data protection
- E-commerce requirements
- Employment matters where applicable
- Supplier and manufacturing agreements
- Legal disputes
- Regulatory records

The exact requirements depend on the markets and jurisdictions in which SAB’A operates.

## Legal Documentation

Important legal matters should be documented and maintained in an organized manner.

Relevant records may include:

- Registrations
- Licenses
- Contracts
- Agreements
- Trademark records
- Intellectual property records
- Regulatory documents
- Compliance documentation
- Legal correspondence
- Legal advice
- Dispute records

## Legal Review

Legal review should be considered when a decision may materially affect:

- Ownership
- Intellectual Property
- Customer Rights
- Product Compliance
- Privacy
- Commercial Commitments
- Regulatory Obligations
- Significant Financial or Legal Risk

Legal advice should be obtained from appropriately qualified professionals where the matter requires jurisdiction-specific interpretation or professional legal judgment.

## Regulatory Awareness

SAB’A should identify and monitor legal and regulatory requirements relevant to its activities.

Requirements may vary according to:

- Product category
- Market
- Country
- Sales channel
- Business structure
- Customer location
- Manufacturing location

Regulatory information should be verified against authoritative sources where practical.

## Legal Risk

Legal risks should be identified according to their potential impact on:

- Business continuity
- Brand
- Customers
- Products
- Intellectual Property
- Financial position
- Reputation

Material legal risks should be appropriately documented and escalated.

## Confidentiality

Sensitive legal information should be handled appropriately.

Access should be limited according to:

- Relevance
- Authority
- Confidentiality
- Legal requirements

Confidential legal records should not be distributed unnecessarily.

## Relationship with Other Codex Sections

Legal requirements may affect multiple areas of SAB’A.

Examples include:

- Brand → Trademarks and Intellectual Property
- Product → Product Compliance
- Packaging → Labeling and regulatory requirements
- Website → Privacy and consumer requirements
- Ecommerce → Terms, payments, returns, and consumer rights
- Marketing → Advertising and claims
- Operations → Supplier and manufacturing agreements
- Customer Experience → Privacy and customer rights

Where legal requirements affect another section, the applicable primary documentation should reflect the operational requirement while this section maintains the legal framework and records.

## Current Source of Truth

Current approved legal documentation should take precedence over:

- Outdated advice
- Informal assumptions
- Previous practices
- Historical documents
- Archived materials

Historical legal documents should remain in the Archive when appropriate and should not override current requirements.

## Core Principle

> SAB’A should protect what it builds by making legal responsibility part of the system, not an afterthought.
