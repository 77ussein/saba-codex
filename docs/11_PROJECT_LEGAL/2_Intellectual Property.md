# Intellectual Property

## Purpose

Intellectual Property defines how SAB’A identifies, protects, manages, uses, and documents the intellectual property associated with the brand and its business activities.

The objective is to protect the creative, commercial, technical, and distinctive assets that contribute to SAB’A’s identity and long-term value.

## Intellectual Property Principles

1. **Protect What SAB’A Creates**
2. **Document Ownership**
3. **Respect Third-Party Rights**
4. **Control Authorized Use**
5. **Maintain Clear Records**
6. **Protect Confidential Information**
7. **Review Rights before Publication or Commercial Use**
8. **Treat Intellectual Property as a Long-Term Asset**

## Intellectual Property Scope

SAB’A intellectual property may include:

- Brand Names
- Trademarks
- Logos
- Visual Identity
- Packaging Designs
- Product Concepts
- Product Names
- Fragrance Concepts
- Written Content
- Photography
- Video
- Illustrations
- Graphics
- Website Content
- Digital Assets
- Original Documentation
- Technical Materials
- Other protectable creative or commercial works

The applicable legal protection depends on the nature of the asset and relevant jurisdiction.

## Ownership

Ownership of intellectual property created for SAB’A should be clearly established.

Where work is created by:

- Employees
- Freelancers
- Designers
- Photographers
- Agencies
- Consultants
- Manufacturers
- Other third parties

the applicable ownership and usage rights should be documented through appropriate agreements where necessary.

## Brand Assets

Core SAB’A brand assets should be controlled and maintained as official assets.

These may include:

- Brand Name
- Logo
- Typography
- Color System
- Visual Elements
- Brand Content
- Packaging Identity
- Product Naming

Only approved versions should be used in official brand communications and commercial materials.

## Trademarks

Important names, marks, logos, and other distinctive identifiers should be evaluated for trademark protection where appropriate.

Trademark records should include, where applicable:

- Mark
- Owner
- Jurisdiction
- Application or registration number
- Filing date
- Registration date
- Status
- Relevant classes
- Renewal information

Trademark matters should be coordinated with the relevant legal professionals.

## Copyright

Copyright may apply to original works created for SAB’A, subject to applicable law.

Examples include:

- Written content
- Photography
- Video
- Graphic design
- Illustrations
- Website content
- Original documentation
- Creative campaigns

Ownership and licensing should be established before significant commercial use.

## Design Rights

Where applicable, SAB’A may seek protection for distinctive product, packaging, or visual designs.

Design protection should be evaluated according to:

- Originality
- Commercial importance
- Jurisdiction
- Registration requirements
- Expected product lifecycle

## Product and Fragrance Concepts

Product and fragrance concepts may contain valuable confidential or commercially sensitive information.

Where appropriate, SAB’A should protect such information through:

- Confidentiality controls
- Access restrictions
- Non-disclosure agreements
- Supplier agreements
- Appropriate contractual protections

Protection should reflect the nature of the information and applicable legal rights.

## Confidential Information

Confidential information may include:

- Formulas
- Product development information
- Supplier information
- Commercial terms
- Pricing structures
- Business plans
- Unreleased products
- Unreleased campaigns
- Technical documentation

Confidential information should only be shared with authorized parties who have a legitimate need to access it.

## Third-Party Intellectual Property

SAB’A must respect intellectual property belonging to others.

Third-party assets should only be used when SAB’A has an appropriate legal basis or permission.

This may include:

- Licensed photography
- Fonts
- Music
- Software
- Artwork
- Stock assets
- Trademarks
- External content

Licenses and usage restrictions should be reviewed before commercial use.

## Licensing

When SAB’A licenses intellectual property to or from another party, the agreement should clearly define relevant rights.

Considerations may include:

- Scope of use
- Territory
- Duration
- Exclusivity
- Media
- Modification rights
- Attribution
- Fees
- Termination

Licensing records should be retained.

## Digital Assets

Digital intellectual property should be managed with appropriate ownership and access controls.

Relevant assets may include:

- Domains
- Websites
- Social media accounts
- Digital design files
- Photography libraries
- Video libraries
- Source files
- Digital brand assets

Ownership and account access should remain under appropriate SAB’A-controlled arrangements.

## Intellectual Property Infringement

Potential infringement involving SAB’A intellectual property should be documented and assessed.

Potential actions may include:

- Investigation
- Evidence preservation
- Contact with the relevant party
- Platform reporting
- Legal notice
- Professional legal action

The response should be proportionate to the importance and nature of the infringement.

## Unauthorized Use by SAB’A

Potential unauthorized use of third-party intellectual property should also be addressed promptly.

If a concern is identified, SAB’A should:

1. Pause or restrict the relevant use where appropriate
2. Verify the rights
3. Review the applicable license or agreement
4. Correct the issue
5. Document the resolution

## Record Keeping

Intellectual property records should include relevant information such as:

- Asset
- Owner
- Creator
- Creation date
- Registration details
- License details
- Jurisdiction
- Usage rights
- Expiry or renewal dates where applicable
- Supporting agreements

Records should remain current and distinguish active rights from historical records.

## Intellectual Property Review

Intellectual property should be reviewed when:

- A new brand asset is created
- A new product or name is introduced
- New packaging is developed
- Third-party creative work is commissioned
- New markets are entered
- Licensing arrangements change
- Potential infringement is identified

## Professional Legal Advice

Intellectual property rights vary by jurisdiction and asset type.

Where registration, ownership disputes, licensing, infringement, or other legal interpretation is involved, SAB’A should obtain advice from an appropriately qualified legal professional.

## Core Principle

> SAB’A should treat its intellectual property as part of the value it builds, and protect it with the same care given to the products and experiences that carry it.
