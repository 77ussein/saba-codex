# Privacy and Data

## Purpose

Privacy and Data defines the principles and framework governing how SAB’A collects, uses, stores, protects, shares, and manages personal data.

The objective is to respect customer privacy, support responsible data practices, and maintain compliance with applicable privacy and data protection requirements.

## Privacy Principles

1. **Privacy by Design**
2. **Purpose before Collection**
3. **Minimum Necessary Data**
4. **Transparency**
5. **Security**
6. **Controlled Access**
7. **Responsible Retention**
8. **Respect for Customer Rights**
9. **Accountability**
10. **Compliance with Applicable Law**

## Scope

Privacy and data considerations may apply to information collected through:

- Website
- Ecommerce
- Customer Accounts
- Orders
- Payments
- Customer Service
- Email
- WhatsApp
- Marketing
- Loyalty Programs
- Analytics
- Cookies and Similar Technologies
- Events
- Other approved customer touchpoints

The applicable requirements depend on the jurisdiction, activity, type of data, and relationship with the individual.

## Personal Data

Personal data may include information that identifies or can reasonably be associated with an individual.

Examples may include:

- Name
- Email Address
- Phone Number
- Shipping Information
- Billing Information
- Account Information
- Order History
- Customer Service Records
- Communication Preferences
- Technical or Usage Information
- Other information where legally considered personal data

Only information necessary for a legitimate and defined purpose should be collected.

## Purpose of Collection

Before collecting personal data, SAB’A should establish the relevant purpose.

Potential purposes may include:

- Processing Orders
- Delivering Products
- Providing Customer Service
- Managing Accounts
- Processing Returns and Exchanges
- Communicating with Customers
- Improving Products and Services
- Analytics
- Marketing where permitted
- Fraud Prevention
- Legal and Regulatory Compliance

Data should not be collected without a defined and appropriate purpose.

## Transparency

Customers should receive clear information about relevant data practices.

Where applicable, privacy information should explain:

- What data is collected
- Why it is collected
- How it is used
- How it is stored
- Who may receive it
- How long it may be retained
- Applicable customer rights
- How to contact SAB’A regarding privacy matters

## Lawful Basis

Where applicable, processing of personal data should rely on an appropriate legal basis under the relevant jurisdiction.

Potential legal bases may include:

- Consent
- Contractual necessity
- Legal obligation
- Legitimate interests
- Other lawful bases recognized by applicable law

The appropriate basis should be determined according to the specific activity and jurisdiction.

## Consent

Where consent is required, it should be obtained through an appropriate and understandable mechanism.

Consent should not be assumed where the law requires explicit or affirmative consent.

Where applicable, customers should have an appropriate mechanism to withdraw consent.

## Marketing Data

Marketing communications should be managed according to applicable privacy and electronic communications requirements.

Customers should receive appropriate choices regarding:

- Marketing communications
- Communication preferences
- Unsubscribing
- Relevant personalization

Marketing data should not be used beyond its permitted purpose.

## Cookies and Similar Technologies

Where SAB’A uses cookies or similar technologies, their use should be managed according to applicable requirements.

Depending on the technology and jurisdiction, this may include:

- Essential functionality
- Analytics
- Personalization
- Marketing
- Measurement

Where consent is required, appropriate consent mechanisms should be implemented.

## Data Sharing

Personal data may be shared with third parties only where there is an appropriate reason and legal basis.

Third parties may include:

- Payment Providers
- Shipping Providers
- Technology Providers
- Ecommerce Platforms
- Customer Service Providers
- Analytics Providers
- Marketing Service Providers
- Professional Advisors
- Government or Regulatory Authorities where legally required

Third-party access should be limited to what is necessary for the relevant purpose.

## Service Providers

Where external service providers process personal data on behalf of SAB’A, appropriate contractual and security requirements should be considered.

Relevant considerations may include:

- Purpose
- Data access
- Security
- Confidentiality
- Retention
- Sub-processors
- Data deletion or return
- Applicable legal requirements

## Data Security

SAB’A should implement reasonable and appropriate measures to protect personal data from:

- Unauthorized access
- Unauthorized disclosure
- Loss
- Destruction
- Alteration
- Misuse

Security measures should reflect the nature and sensitivity of the information and the applicable risks.

## Access Control

Access to personal data should follow the principle of least privilege.

Individuals should only access information necessary for their role and responsibilities.

Access should be reviewed and removed when no longer required.

## Data Retention

Personal data should not be retained indefinitely without an appropriate reason.

Retention periods should consider:

- Legal requirements
- Contractual requirements
- Business purpose
- Operational necessity
- Dispute requirements
- Security considerations

When data is no longer required, it should be securely deleted, anonymized, or otherwise handled according to applicable requirements.

## Customer Rights

Depending on the applicable jurisdiction, individuals may have rights relating to their personal data.

These may include rights to:

- Access
- Correction
- Deletion
- Restriction
- Objection
- Data portability
- Withdrawal of consent
- Other rights established by applicable law

Requests should be handled according to the relevant legal requirements and internal procedures.

## Data Accuracy

SAB’A should take reasonable steps to maintain accurate personal data where accuracy is important to the relevant purpose.

Customers may be provided with appropriate mechanisms to update inaccurate information where applicable.

## Data Breaches and Incidents

Potential personal data incidents should be identified, documented, assessed, and escalated appropriately.

An incident may include:

- Unauthorized access
- Unauthorized disclosure
- Lost data
- Accidental exposure
- Security compromise
- Improper data handling

Where notification is legally required, the appropriate regulatory authority or affected individuals should be notified within the applicable requirements.

## International Data Transfers

Where personal data is transferred across jurisdictions, SAB’A should assess the applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

Appropriate safeguards should be implemented where required.

## Privacy by Design

Privacy considerations should be incorporated into relevant systems and processes from the beginning.

This may apply to:

- Website development
- Ecommerce
- Customer accounts
- Marketing systems
- Analytics
- New technologies
- Customer service systems
- New products or services involving personal data

## Documentation

Relevant privacy and data records should be maintained where appropriate.

These may include:

- Privacy Notices
- Consent Records
- Data Processing Records
- Data Retention Requirements
- Third-Party Agreements
- Security Procedures
- Data Incident Records
- Customer Requests
- Regulatory Correspondence

## Privacy Review

A privacy review should be considered when SAB’A:

- Introduces a new data collection process
- Launches a new digital system
- Uses a new third-party provider
- Introduces new marketing technology
- Expands into a new jurisdiction
- Changes how customer data is used
- Experiences a significant data incident

## Responsibility

Privacy responsibilities may involve:

- Management
- Legal
- Ecommerce
- Website
- Marketing
- Customer Experience
- Operations
- Technology Providers

Responsibilities should be clearly assigned for relevant activities.

## Professional Legal Advice

Privacy and data protection requirements vary by jurisdiction and can be highly specific.

Where interpretation, cross-border transfers, regulatory obligations, significant incidents, or other specialized matters are involved, SAB’A should obtain advice from an appropriately qualified legal or privacy professional.

## Core Principle

> SAB’A should treat customer data as information entrusted to the brand, not as an asset to be collected without purpose.
